# PART 1 — in plain words

## What he gets to look at

**One page (the "round 2 board"), one stop.** It has four parts:

1. **"In your words" (the handholding).** Four short boxes he types into, saved as he types:
   - Who is the one man who lands on this page? What happened in his week?
   - Ten years from now, what does he want to be able to say he did?
   - What should he feel in the first five seconds?
   - A line from anywhere you wish you had written, or a line you never want on this page.
2. **The look: five directions, all orange first.** Each copies how one real product marks its parts. A switcher
   shows one direction at a time, plus "Before" and "Now" to compare. Every direction is shown on the same nine
   screens, as **screenshots of the real app** with the direction switched on (not redrawn mock-ups), full length:
   front page, log in, Home, All tools, Time Log (phone and computer), Time calendar (computer), Your plan Today,
   the Quit door, The Field.
   - D1 **One orange** (like Strava): orange everywhere; parts told apart by icon and name only.
   - D2 **Coloured title** (like Apple Health's categories): his own idea. Only a part's title and icon take its
     colour; the rest is orange and grey.
   - D3 **Coloured icon** (like the iPhone Settings app or Things 3): each part has one small coloured icon square;
     titles and buttons stay orange and white.
   - D4 **Header glow** (like Spotify): the top of a part's page fades from its colour into black; every button
     stays orange, the way Spotify's play button stays green.
   - D5 **Free pick:** the designer's own idea, labelled as invention. Still orange first.
   - Each one says in one line what it copies and whether that was checked on the real product.
3. **New part badges**, three options, shown on the log-in page and in the page header, to replace L / Q / D.
4. **The front page words: five directions.** Each is a full set of words (small line, headline, line under it,
   the three doors, closing), shown as the real front page, full length. Each line can get a 👍 or a note on its
   own, so he can take the headline from one and a door line from another.

Every block has a 👍 and a note box that saves while he types (green "Saved"), with his earlier notes listed.

## What happens after

- If his notes pick a look and words clearly, the build starts (Phase 6).
- If not, a **short second board** shows at most two looks and two sets of words, rewritten with his "In your
  words" answers, plus a "make it cooler" pass on the front page, sign up and the Quit door. Then the build.
- The build puts the picked look and words live, then a new before/after page shows every page full length.
- **Separately, with care:** the privacy page (Phase P). It is rebuilt from one list of every tool and what data
  it keeps, and a test fails whenever a tool is added without being on that list. So the page cannot go out of date
  without the tests going red.

## What stays as it is

- The name, the 10K plate mark, the three parts and their addresses, the coherence rules R1–R5 (his, 2026-11-19).
- The pages he gave a 👍: Dating door, Inner Game, Prices, You, Dating tools, The Field.
- His data. Nothing in this plan writes to his data; the round 2 board is built from a branch and the local test
  stack, and nothing reaches the live site until his pick.

## How this way of working was chosen (CLAUDE.md: say what is checked)

Checked on 2026-11-19 (web search, third-party guides, not official docs):
- **Words:** current AI copy guides agree on one order: collect the customers' own words first (forums, comments),
  write the core story by hand, then let AI make many variants of headlines and buttons, and never invent proof.
  ([causo.ai](https://hub.causo.ai/guides/ai-for-landing-page-copywriting-2026),
  [gptprompts.ai](https://gptprompts.ai/how-to-use-chatgpt-for-copywriting),
  [systeme.io](https://systeme.io/blog/landing-page-copy-with-ai),
  [pulse.bot](https://www.pulse.bot/ai/blogs/build-launch-a-voc-landing-page-with-claude-code-in-5-steps-0ef6d013-4c41-4543-b159-c0ff0b710196/))
  The repo already holds such a word bank: `docs/research/change-your-life/02-comment-language-bank.md`.
- **Three tests per line** from Harry Dry (Marketing Examples): can you picture it, could it be proven false, could
  nobody else say it. Checked only through second-hand notes of his 76-minute interview
  ([demandcurve.com](https://www.demandcurve.com/lessons/fundamental-rules-of-good-copy),
  [upgrow.io](https://www.upgrow.io/harry-dry-copywriting-3-rules/)), not the interview itself.
- **Looks:** AI design guides now make 3–5 clearly different directions with the facts and parts held fixed, settle
  the direction first and polish second, and start from real reference products, because a vague brief returns "the
  average of every landing page" ([chatprd.ai](https://www.chatprd.ai/how-i-ai/workflows/how-to-build-a-high-fidelity-landing-page-with-claude-design),
  [rundown.ai](https://www.rundown.ai/guides/how-to-design-a-high-converting-landing-page-in-claude-design)).
- **Colour by role:** design systems give the brand colour to actions and let other colours mark only small things
  (Nord: accent on buttons and selected states; Cegid: do not flood screens with brand colour)
  ([nordhealth.design](https://nordhealth.design/design/foundations/colors),
  [eightshapes.com](https://eightshapes.com/articles/color-in-design-systems/)).

**From memory, not checked yet:** how Strava, Apple Health, iPhone Settings, Things 3 and Spotify actually colour
their parts. Phase 1 checks each on the real product's screenshots; a direction that does not hold up is replaced
by one that does, and the board says so.
