## What we are doing, in plain words

We map whole street conversations into **beats**. A beat is a stretch of
lines with one purpose, such as "says what he was doing", "the prepared
compliment", "the guess", "a tease she can answer either way", or "riffing on
something she gave him". From the beats we grow a **tree**. Each node is a
point in the conversation; each branch is what she did and what he did next.
Users then practise node by node, from the start of the conversation outward,
and get further each time.

Each coach is one **basket of evidence**. First we establish what is clearly
true for Social Stoic, then for Natural Lifestyles and the rest. Mixing styles
into scenarios comes later, once there are two baskets to mix.

The hard part is not labelling. It is making each round better than the last,
so that your reviews **teach** rather than **repeat**.

## How the reading works (built against drift)

1. **Open reading, no fixed label list.** In round 1, each set is described in
   my own words, beat by beat. Per beat:
   - which lines it covers;
   - what he does and why (a guess at the purpose);
   - what she gives back;
   - what it opens up (the possible branches);
   - prepared or spontaneous;
   - where else it would work;
   - how sure I am, and what I am unsure of.
2. **Two independent readers with different lenses.**
   - **Mechanics:** what happens, step by step.
   - **Learner:** what a man could practise here, and what could have gone
     differently.

   I merge the two. Where they disagree, the beat is marked unsure, so you see
   the doubt instead of a confident guess.
3. **A fresh pair of eyes every round.** One set per round is read **without**
   the lessons file. If the fresh reading finds things the guided reading
   missed, the lessons are narrowing my view, and I loosen them.
4. **Every round has a "doesn't fit" section.** It names what in these sets
   contradicts the patterns seen so far. An empty section is a warning, not a
   success.
5. **Names grow; they are not imposed.** After round 1, the beat names that held
   up become a vocabulary. Each name gets your definition, a count, examples and
   counterexamples. "Other: describe it" is always allowed.
6. **A checker before you look.** A critic agent checks every beat against the
   timeline: no claim without a line behind it, and speakers right. It also
   checks that voice-over is not read as conversation.

## How your reviews turn into learning

- **A lessons file per basket.** Each of your changes becomes one entry:
  - what I said;
  - what you said;
  - the general rule behind it, in your words where possible;
  - its scope: this coach only, or everyone.

  Every reader gets the file in the next round.
- **The quality measure is repeat mistakes.** After each round I count your
  changes and sort them into two kinds:
  - a **repeat**: a lesson we already had and I failed to apply. The target is
    zero.
  - **new insight**: expected, and welcome.

  If repeats don't fall, the method is wrong, and I change the method, not only
  the lesson.
- **"Your note → lesson → what changed"** is shown back on the page, so you can
  see the learning happen.
- **A claims ledger per basket.** Claims are written with their basis, for
  example "says what he was doing before the compliment: 4 of 5 sets", with the
  counterexamples listed. A claim counts as **clearly true** for a coach when it
  holds in about 80% of at least 10 sets, and you agree with it.
