## The one thing to get right

"you tend to drift away from what i want, and then make fixed rules that actually
serve to make future versions drift too" (R3-6).

- Do what his note points at. A rule from one note stays where he said it: "I
  never said 12 seconds. But theres a big difference between 1.5 sec and 12
  seconds" (chg-1). Tools still work across reel types (R5-ddel-1).
- Keep his hedges. "Just an idea" is one tool to try (R4-4). His examples are
  seeds: find 10-20 things his idea points at, then pick the best (RS-T3, RS-U7).
- His questions are questions, not orders (chg-4). Answer them with evidence
  from strong real reels (RS-T12).
- Never quietly drop something he said works: "very dangerous" (Outvoted Morning).
- Unsure what he meant? Ask him. Don't guess and build (RS-Q7).
- "maintain dynamism to maximize creativity", and have "a way of knowing when
  we're getting closer to target" (RS-P1).
- Say which claims are checked and which come from memory (ten ways, 2026-11-20).

## Why rounds repeat (Reel B, 16 rounds, counted 2026-11-27)

"we're still in a pretty bad process in terms of all this" (R16-10). His notes
by topic: caption and source placement 8 rounds, music 8, cuts inside words 4,
"show me, don't ask me" 4 (R10-2, R13-2, R16-8, R16-9), misread notes 3 (R4-6,
R3-5, R16-1). (Claude) The causes, and the fix for each:
- **No fixed look.** Each round re-decided captions, sources and music from a
  few reels, so a new round broke what the last one got right. Fix: one style
  sheet per track, chosen by him once from a big study ("then we decide once
  and for all", R16-4). A round never changes it unless his note does.
- **Over-fixing.** One note changed the whole format (R15 square picture: "This
  is not what i meant", R16-1). Fix: the smallest change that answers the note;
  anything bigger only as an extra choice beside it.
- **Asking instead of showing.** Fix: every open point is short clips of the
  same moment to pick from, never a question in words (R16-8, R16-9). Every cut
  Claude can't judge by ear is a 3-second clip with a 👍.
- **Whole reels to compare small things.** Fix: clips differ in one thing only,
  20 s at most (R10-2).
- **Misreads found late.** Fix: the page opens with his notes, one line each,
  and what changed for each, so a wrong reading shows in seconds.

## A round

The way that worked best (2026-11-26): "give me your best video, and then make a
short selection of the different points in each video where we're not fully
decided". His verdict: "much higher quality, the things i needed to give
feedback on was very clear too".

1. **Story first.** Know what the reel is about and what it promises before any
   animation (Same Night A1).
2. **One whole reel, the trunk.** Variants are branches on its beats, also shown
   inside full-length reels: "What does not work is if we begin developing fruits
   or branches that are not connected to the tree" (chat, 2026-11-23). Asked to
   improve one reel? Improve that reel, not "eight different things" (round 2).
3. **Variety.** From different kinds of animation, not the same shots in other
   colours (night run, 2026-11-21, summary). On how many versions: "as
   many as you can realistically make that would make sense ... but that depends
   on your ability to figure out what would make sense" (RS-Q3). Use cheaper
   models for the legwork (RS-P4). (Claude) Since the best-cut round, explore in
   Claude's own work and show him only the best and the open choices.
4. **Rough first, then quality.** A rough version is fine for judging pace and
   flicker (chat, 2026-11-22). Near-still sketches are not: "a bunch of very
   simple animations". Show frames before a long render ("more handholding",
   2026-11-18).
5. **Check before he looks.** (Claude) A fresh reviewer that did not build it
   checks the reel against every standing ask below and against the reel he
   liked. He had to repeat source and caption placement more than five times.
   Claude can't hear: flag any doubtful cut for him. He judges beauty
   (2026-11-21).
6. **His page.** Videos with a 👍 and an auto-saving note box on each. No
   reading: "Way too much reading for me" (Outvoted Trunk). Show only the part
   that differs (R10-2). Seconds visible while he watches. Plain version names:
   "these names are insane to me" (q2-v1).
7. **Testing one thing** (music, an ending): "Every time we have something, like
   'music' lets try a few variants" (2026-11-25).
8. **Don't update a page while he is on it.** It "reset what i was doing" (loop 3).
9. **Fewer rounds.** "if we could get to this point from no input from me, then
   that would be great" (R6-page). One round took him "30-40 min ... which is too
   much" (R5-page).

When he keeps repeating himself, propose time to research how to do the job
better (RS-M5). He can't show drafts to people: compare with published videos.

## His standing asks

**Every reel**
- A real story: hook, climax, resolution, not too fast. Story over "erratic
  zooming" (R5 reference reels).
- A strong top line that stays all reel, and the reel delivers on it (R5-V1,
  R5-V2). "celebrities" beats a vague "people" (chat, 2026-11-23).
- An ending with punch, never AI-sounding: "I hate the AI soundingness of this"
  (R6-end-1). His own example line: "quitting is a decision ONLY you can make,
  for reasons only you can decide" (E1_full, R6-B).
- Captions short and sharp (R5-O11). Text over moving pictures, not dark word
  cards (round 2). His line comes WITH the text, if both are there (E4).
- Endings: his face pops up in a circle while he says the comment line. "It works well
  with the pop up of me in a circle, so we should note this as a method that we can use
  always. Perhaps i film X ways of them commenting all at once, that we just always use"
  (SN r5, 2026-11-27).
- He appears in many reels, in ways that are easy to film: a closing line, his
  voice, a short moment, or animated into the story (RS-Q6, loop 3). Real people
  beat an AI narrator; an AI voice is fine as a placeholder (R3-1, chg-0).
  Breathing cut out of his voice, "if that is possible" (2026-11-20; method vC of
  the breath test was "probably the best").

Track-specific asks live in each track file (`tracks/`).

**Articles** (his notes on the February article; artN =
`git show 18efdb10:docs/articles/01-8020-rule/feedbackN.json`). (Claude) Use as tools for other text too.
- Specific beats generic: "the specificity of the example makes it good" (art1).

## Craft facts (Claude, measured)

- Transcript word times end before the sound does. Cut on the sound
  (`cutsafe.py`), then check by ear. A builder once called a chopped word "fixed"
  when it was not.
- A stitched answer must be one the man really gave to that question (round 9).
- Pauses at cuts: about 0.15-0.3 s inside one speaker, 0.35-0.5 s between
  speakers. Clips within about 1 LU of loudness. Never run loudnorm after apad or
  atrim. He never judged these numbers.
- His voice: never raise it into a 16-bit file. Keep it float until the last step, then
  low cut, de-esser, soft compression, and one look-ahead limiter
  (`reel-beliefs/r6/voice/polish.py`, `mix.py`). Round 5 chopped ~1,100 peaks and stacked
  a soft clip + limiter + loudnorm; he heard sharp "s" sounds (2026-11-27). Reel B's
  closing line (r15) had the same chop: redone in round 16 (`celeb-reels/r16/code/voice16.py`: this chain plus a gentle shelf for his low rumble).
- A pencil sound must have no low whoosh: he hears it as breathing (SN r5).
- `barscheck.py` finds black edges in a render.
- Music under talking (round 14, 7 strong reels measured): 19-38 dB under the
  voice, or none (3 of 7), and mostly bass so the voice range stays free. It
  rises toward the end or when a career clip comes on. Even out each song's
  level over time, or its own build (drums coming in) jumps under a word.
  Round 15, the 12 most-viewed posts of Reel B's own five clips: most use no
  music under talking; when used, a flat bed 13-18 dB under; a rise only on the
  last line. Full screen leaves no face-free steady caption spot in close-ups;
  a square picture in a black frame does (measured on every frame).
- Cut picture pieces on frame numbers, not seconds, or lip-sync drifts.
