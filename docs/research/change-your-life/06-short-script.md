## Why it is shaped like this

The study's strongest structural finding is that **resonance tracks recognition, not instruction** — seven of the ten lowest-resonance videos in a 474M-view corpus are tactical listicles. So this opens on a comment the audience wrote and upvoted, not on a promise, a tip, or a face.

- **Hook rule.** The first frame is a comment, not a claim, and not a face. Seven of the ten lowest-resonance videos in the corpus open with a promise or a tip.
- **One mechanism.** Duration is the only mechanism in this cut. The corpus is full of videos that name six mechanisms and land none.
- **Cutdowns.** Three sub-60s cuts live inside this master: beats 0–14 (the comment), 33–60 (the duration mismatch), 104–140 (what the audience built). The third is the strongest standalone.
- **Runtime.** 3:04 at a natural read. Trim beats 52 and 85 first if it needs to come under 3:00.
- **Do not use.** No countdown lists, no “5 tips”, no borrowed motivational-speech register, and nothing from the Gadzhi cold open — it is emotional coercion and the same video narrates suicidal planning.

## Transcript — what a viewer hears

**Recognition.** Someone left this comment under a video about fixing your life. Thirty-three thousand people agreed with him. So I read ninety-one of these videos. All the way through. Every word.

**The pattern.** Three hundred thousand words of transcript. Half a billion views. And they all do the same thing. They describe your problem — really well — and then they stop.

**The gap.** Here's what none of them tell you. Nobody says how long it takes. One video says thirty days. One says a decade. One says five years minimum. One says three to seven. Same question. Same audience. Thirty days to fifteen years. So you start, and at week three you're not where the video implied you'd be, and you conclude the thing that everybody concludes. That it works for other people. Not for you.

**The mechanism.** There's one exception in ninety-one videos. One creator says the quiet part: everyone tells you what to do, and nobody tells you how long it'll take — and that's what screws people. Imagine nobody had told you a degree takes four years. You'd quit in month four and decide you were stupid. That's the whole thing. It isn't that you're weak. It's that you were given a deadline nobody could meet, by people who never named one.

**The turn.** Now here's the part I didn't expect. I also read eighteen hundred of the top comments. In forty-one of the ninety-one, somebody in the comments had written out the steps. Not a summary. The actual instructions, typed out as a numbered list, because the video said them out loud and never wrote them down. Twenty-eight different channels. Nobody organised it. It just keeps happening, under videos that have nothing to do with each other. Under others, people post a start date and a promise, and come back a year later to edit the same comment with what actually happened.

**The offer.** So we built it. It asks which problem is actually yours — because most people are treating the wrong one. It asks what your week actually holds before it suggests anything. It tells you how long. Up front. Even when the honest answer is that nobody knows. And it makes you write the letter before you fall off — the one you'd write to someone you love who quit. Then you cross out their name and write your own. It's free. Link's below. Go and do the thing you already know about.
