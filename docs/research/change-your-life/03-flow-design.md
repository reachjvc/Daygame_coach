# The "change your life" flow — what the research says to build

Derived from `00-synthesis.md`. Every stage below exists because the corpus proves a specific gap, and the gap is cited. This is a design argument, not an implementation plan — no file paths, no milestones. If it's approved, that comes next.

---

## The idea in one paragraph

Ninety-one videos teach the same lesson and skip the same seven things. The product is not another explanation of habits, identity or discipline — the audience already has those, and says so at 33,000 likes. The product is **the execution layer between the reframe and Monday morning**: work out which problem is actually yours, say out loud what you can afford, pick one thing and defer the rest with a date, size the rep to your worst day, write your relapse letter *before* you relapse, and get pulled back in on a schedule. Every one of those is something the corpus's audience improvises by hand, in comment boxes, on videos that are ten years old.

## The design rules that fall out of the research

Four commitments, each one contradicting how the category normally works.

1. **Diagnose before prescribing.** Dr. K: *"The number one reason why problems persist is because theyve made a diagnostic error not a treatment error"* — and *"if you're single and you think you know what the problem is there's a very high percentage chance that you are wrong."* He says accurate diagnosis needs a professional and offers no self-serve tool. That tool is the front door of this product.
2. **Constraints before goals.** Every framework in the corpus starts one step past where the user is standing. D'Avella tells people to purge their kitchen; his two top comments (83,000 likes combined) are people who don't control their kitchen. Nothing gets prescribed that the user's stated constraints can't support.
3. **The app is not a place to consume.** Dr. K's sharpest point applies to us: content that feels free trains the brain to refuse the real cost of change — *"Either you are learning or you are wasting time. Don't confuse the two."* So: **content unlocks after a logged rep, never instead of one.** This single rule differentiates the product from everything in the corpus.
4. **Never make day 34 worth the same as day 0.** Project 50's restart-from-day-one rule produces a comment section of day-one pledges and essentially no finishers. Dr. Marks supplies the replacement: *"Relapse isn't failure, it's feedback"* and *"It's the return, not the mistake, that builds resilience."*

And one thing to protect against: Sisyphus 55's critique is that tracking apps *are* the pathology — "the auto exploiting subject carries around its own labor camp." That's a real risk for a product like this. Mitigation is built into stage 8: an explicit end state, deferral with dates rather than infinite backlog, and a cap on how many things can be live at once.

## The stages

### Stage 0 — Differential: which problem is actually yours

**Why it exists.** The corpus's single most common structural failure is treating one cause as universal. The videos split into camps that never acknowledge each other — willpower-deficit vs system-failure vs material-conditions vs core-beliefs vs no-social-access — and each sells its camp to everyone. Dr. K names the meta-error and doesn't solve it.

**What it does.** Six to ten discriminating questions that route the user to a *primary constraint*, not a personality type. The output is one sentence: "your bottleneck right now is X."

The candidate layers, each drawn from a camp in the corpus:

| Layer | Signature | Corpus source |
|---|---|---|
| **Material** | No money, no time, no control of your space, caring for someone, shift work | Man Carrying Thing; the 83k-like D'Avella comments |
| **Access** | You know what to do and there is nowhere and no-one to do it with | Dr. K on third spaces; "You will NOT meet someone, ever, without meeting new people" |
| **Skill** | You get the opportunity and don't know the moves | Todd V; Coach Kyle; Charisma on Command |
| **Belief/identity** | You discount every success; "I'm broken" | Dr. K's core beliefs; Aaron Beck model |
| **Regulation** | You know, you want to, you don't do it | Brewer; Izadi; Dr. Marks on basal ganglia |
| **Direction** | You'd act if you knew at what | Leipzig's question two |
| **Clinical / crisis** | Anhedonia, suicidality, addiction | Sisyphus 55 |

**Questions, phrased the way the audience phrases it** (see `02-comment-language-bank.md`):
- When you imagine doing the thing, what stops you first — not knowing how, not having anywhere to do it, or not being able to make yourself?
- In the last month, did you get a real chance at this and not take it? *(access vs skill vs regulation)*
- If a stranger handed you a perfect step-by-step plan tomorrow, would you be able to run it this week? What would get in the way? *(surfaces material constraints without asking about money directly)*
- When something goes well, how long does it stay true for you? *(belief filter)*
- Is there anything you already know you should do and just… don't?

**The escape hatch is mandatory.** Two of the routes must not lead to a protocol. If the answers indicate crisis, the flow stops offering self-improvement and surfaces real resources — because this corpus contains a suicidality video for a reason, and because the "tell no one" advice that half the genre gives is actively dangerous for that user. If the answers indicate a hard material constraint, the flow says so plainly instead of prescribing monk mode. Naming a material cause is itself relieving: it's why Memeable Data got 6.4M views for telling men the math wasn't their fault.

### Stage 1 — The constraints ledger

**Why it exists.** Gap #5, the most consistently invisible thing in the corpus. Also independently identified as the biggest hole in the existing Ali Abdaal research (`docs/research/ali-abdaal/00-synthesis.md`, hole #2).

**What it does.** Six short inputs — hours genuinely free per week, money available per month, control over your own space (full / partial / none), people who depend on you, health limits, social access. Output is a **budget** that every later stage must respect and can be checked against.

This is what makes the fit test possible: if the plan doesn't fit the budget, the plan gets cut — not the user's self-esteem. Ali Abdaal's one real constraints mechanism is exactly this, and it's the best thing in his system.

**Questions:**
- Realistically, how many hours a week are yours? Not ideally — this week.
- Can you change your own environment? Can you throw food out, delete an app, rearrange a room, be alone for 20 minutes?
- Who depends on you?
- What's the money picture for anything that costs money?

### Stage 2 — Candidate generation (not purpose formatting)

**Why it exists.** Gap #6, and the single largest documented failure in the corpus. Leipzig's *How to know your life purpose in 5 minutes* has 20.4M views, a 1.31% like-rate — one of the weakest in the entire dataset — and a top comment reading *"I'm mad jealous of people who know what they want to do."* Ali Abdaal's whole career framework requires knowing which tasks you enjoy; his two highest comments (7,600 and 2,000 likes) both say that's the actual hard part and it isn't addressed.

Purpose frameworks **format an answer you already have.** They cannot produce one. This stage produces one.

**What it does.** Never asks "what's your purpose." Generates candidates from evidence the user already possesses, then narrows. Divergent first, convergent second — the one genuinely good sequencing idea in the Abdaal material.

**Questions, each one sourced:**
- What do you already lose time in? *(observable, not aspirational)*
- What could you explain to someone worse at it than you? *(Leipzig's Q2, reframed downward so it's answerable)*
- What are your **core projects** right now — not what type of person you are? *(Brian Little, TED: "Don't ask people what type you are; ask them, 'What are your core projects in your life?'")*
- What do other people find hard that comes easily to you? *(Manson: "what seems to cause other people a lot of stress and pain comes easily to me")*
- **Which flavour of difficulty are you actually willing to eat?** *(Manson's "favorite flavor of shit sandwich" — "You don't choose a life, you choose a set of problems," a line his own commenter compressed for him at 141 likes)*
- Who do you envy, and for what specifically?

Note the corpus's own answer to the passion question, which contradicts the mainstream: Academy of Ideas — *"very often our passions follow the development of our skills"* — and the high-school counsellor in Abdaal's comments: *"You don't find your passion. You develop it."* The flow should treat a candidate as a hypothesis to test, not a truth to discover.

### Stage 3 — Selection, with a forced trade-off

**Why it exists.** Gap #2. Everyone teaches review; nobody teaches choosing. Improvement Pill says pick one and never says which. Abdaal names his selection system and skips it. struthless's life-changing one-thing was picked *for him by a mentor* — the one input a viewer cannot get from the video. Odysseas refuses outright: "I really don't want to tell you how to do this." Meanwhile his comment section: *"There are too many things and I want to do all of them 😭"* (219) and *"i want to be able to do everything… and i end up doing nothing at all"* (5,300).

**What it does.** Three moves, all from the corpus:
1. **Rank on paper** — Odysseas's ten-minute hierarchy of interests, ordered by "how precious they were."
2. **Cut to a hard number and defer the rest with a date.** Not "someday" — a date. Deferral with a return date is what makes cutting survivable, and it's the mechanism Odysseas gestures at and never builds.
3. **Fit test against Stage 1.** Block it into the actual week. If it doesn't fit the budget, cut the goal. This is the only place the constraints ledger earns its keep.

**Questions:**
- If you could only move one of these forward in the next 90 days, which one and why that one?
- What are you choosing *not* to do, and when will you look at it again?
- Put it in this week. Where does it actually go? What comes out to make room?

Ninety days is deliberate. Nathaniel Drew: *"i cannot see clearly beyond three months from now… i think the magic number is more like two two and a half months."*

### Stage 4 — One rep, sized to the worst day

**Why it exists.** The most robust consensus in the corpus (thread #1 and #5), and the place where the videos are actually right.

**What it does.** Converts the chosen thing into a single observable action with a written definition of what counts. Sizing rule from Justin Sung: *"You want it to work on the worst day"* — his top comment turned it into "design for your worst day, not your best" at 1,100 likes. Hormozi: "start with something small, so small that you can't lose." Her worked example is a client whose entire first week is making the bed and photographing it.

**The thing the corpus never does: define what counts as a rep.** Hormozi's audience never asks what to track, because she never says. This flow makes the user write it: "a rep is done when ___." Clear's version is the model — the reader who wasn't allowed to stay at the gym longer than five minutes, "He's not measuring the results. He's measuring did I show up or not."

**Questions:**
- What's the smallest version of this you could do on your worst day of the last month?
- Write down what counts. When have you done it?
- Where does it attach — what already happens right before it?

### Stage 5 — A ladder, where every rung ends in permission to leave

**Why it exists.** This is the single most valuable mechanism in the entire corpus for this product, and it comes from the dating material: **Todd V's five-rung approach ladder, where each rung ends with explicit permission to walk away.** "physically walk over say literally anything and then you're free to go."

It is the only mechanism in 91 videos with reported behaviour change from self-described level −5 people. Its comment section contains the sentence that names the corpus's deepest gap: *"All the programs and coaches miss this very important step of getting from lvl -5 to lv1."*

**What it does.** Turns the rep into a progression of rungs, each barely harder than the last, each carrying an explicit exit clause. Advance rule from Todd V: "do each step until it seems easy and then move on to the next one" — an ease criterion, not a calendar.

**Why the permission clause is load-bearing:** it removes the outcome from the rep. The action is complete on execution, not on result. That is what let people at the floor actually move, and it is what "just put yourself out there" never gives them.

### Stage 6 — The relapse protocol, written before the relapse

**Why it exists.** The largest unserved gap in the corpus. Hamza *names the exact failure point* — "The fourth day or close enough is the day that it just comes crashing down" — and then never addresses it. Not one video in the DATE, MEN or GEN categories ships a recovery procedure. Dr. Marks is the sole exception.

**What it does.** Before the user starts, they write two artifacts:

1. **The letter.** Shahroo Izadi's exercise, the best single instrument in the corpus: *write what you would say to someone you love who fell off track, then cross out their name and write your own.* Twenty seconds, fully visual, and it lands her entire thesis. It exists in her books, never in the interview — nobody has operationalised it.
2. **The tape, played forward.** Izadi again: pre-write the relapse script — where it happens, what you'll tell yourself, what you'll do instead. James Clear's "failure premortem" is the same move: imagine six months forward, you failed, tell the story of why.

**The rules, stated up front:**
- Never miss twice (Clear).
- A miss does not reset anything. Explicitly designed against Project 50.
- The miss is data: what triggered it, what was I telling myself, what's different next time (Dr. Marks).

### Stage 7 — Return, and the dated commitment

**Why it exists.** Because the audience built this themselves, independently, on at least six unconnected channels, and it is the clearest product signal in the research. Dated public pledge → scheduled return → visible log → an audience of strangers. People are using **the like button as a reminder service** and **comment upvotes as bookmarks** because nothing better exists.

**What it does.** Ships the thing they improvised: a dated commitment with an end date, a scheduled return check-in, and a visible log of what actually happened.

**The disclosure setting is a real decision, not a default.** The corpus genuinely disagrees (divergence #1) — Clear prescribes an accountability partner and a $10 forfeit; Better Ideas calls telling anyone "genuinely destructive"; Thewizardliz invokes the evil eye. So make it the user's choice: private, a named partner, or a stranger audience.

**With one floor that is not negotiable:** a user who came through Stage 0's crisis route is never routed into isolation. Sisyphus 55's video is in this corpus because low social support and "an aversion to self-disclosure" are primary predictors of suicide. Privacy can be a default; isolation cannot be a prescription.

### Stage 8 — Duration honesty and the review

**Why it exists.** Gap #3. The genre gives estimates spanning **30 days to 15 years for the same request**. Dr. K's contribution is the whole point: *"everyone tells you what to do no one tells you how long it'll take and that's what screws people."* People quit at month three of a three-year process because nobody told them it was a three-year process.

**What it does.**
- States an expected horizon up front and scores progress against *that*, not against a month.
- Uses Kurzgesagt's honesty where honesty is all that's available: "anything between 15 and 250 days… You won't know how long it will take for you."
- Reviews with a **status enum, not a streak**: Abdaal's green / yellow / red, where yellow is off track *with* a plan and red is off track *without* one. That distinction — "behind" versus "adrift" — is the most useful small idea in his system and it separates two states every other tracker conflates.
- Reads progress week-over-week, not day-over-day (Hormozi: "progress doesn't go like this"). Trend, not purity (Thomas Frank: "not focus too much on purity, on perfect streaks").
- **Has an end.** The 90-day block closes, the deferred list comes back up, and things can be dropped. This is the guard against Sisyphus 55's labour camp.

## What this reuses versus what is new

The repo already carries a lot of this scaffolding. This design should not rebuild it.

| Already exists | Where |
|---|---|
| Vision → goal-system, horizons, weekly evaluation, morning ritual | `src/goals/visionPlanService.ts`, `/test/vision-plan` |
| Ten-year vision ladder, life areas, why-per-goal, belief/desire scoring | `src/goals/lifeMasteryService.ts`, `/test/life-mastery-v1` |
| North Star, life direction | `northStarService.ts`, `lifeDirectionService.ts` |
| Goal hierarchy, milestones, habit ramps, heatmap, daily action view | `src/goals/` components |
| Intake matching | `intakeService.ts` |

**Genuinely new, and each one is a corpus finding no existing module covers:**

1. **Stage 0 differential** with a crisis and material-constraint escape hatch — nothing in the repo routes a user by *cause*.
2. **Stage 1 constraints ledger** as a budget that later stages are checked against — flagged as the biggest hole in the Abdaal research too.
3. **Stage 2 candidate generation** — the repo's vision ladder assumes you can answer it. Twenty million people can't.
4. **Stage 5 permission-to-leave ladder** — the highest-value mechanism found, and the one closest to this product's actual domain.
5. **Stage 6 relapse protocol written in advance** — the largest gap in the corpus, absent from the repo.
6. **Stage 7 dated commitment + scheduled return** — the mechanism the audience literally invented in comment boxes.
7. **The consumption gate** — content unlocks after a logged rep.

## Open questions worth deciding before building

- **How hard is the consumption gate?** It's the most differentiating rule here and also the most likely to annoy people. A soft version (content visible, but the rep prompt comes first) may capture most of the benefit.
- **Does the crisis route belong in a self-improvement product at all,** or should Stage 0 simply decline and hand off? I lean toward decline-and-hand-off; building crisis support is a different product with different obligations.
- **Which single domain does this ship in first?** The dating-for-men material has the strongest evidence *and* the most acute audience — but it also has the corpus's lowest resonance for tactical content, which means the framing has to lead with recognition, not tips.
