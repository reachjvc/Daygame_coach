# "How to change your life" — corpus synthesis

**Method.** 72 search queries → 1,231 unique candidate videos → 94 curated → **91 full transcripts read end to end (300,390 words)** by 12 parallel agents, plus **1,822 top-ranked comments (4.75M likes)** after astroturf filtering. Corpus totals **474,193,612 views** and **515,920 comments**. Channel histories paged back 4–6 years to build age-matched performance baselines.

Detail lives in `01-corpus-table.md` (ranked metrics), `02-comment-language-bank.md` (the audience in its own words), `03-flow-design.md` (the product), `04-short-video-playbook.md` (how this becomes a 3-minute vertical), `05-per-video-extracts.md` (all 91), and `06-short-script.md` (the finished script). Raw transcripts, metadata and the rerunnable harvest rig: `~/.cache/cyl-corpus/`.

## Part A — Read this part

### The one finding

Ninety-one videos, half a billion views, and almost none of them tell you what to do on Monday morning. The genre has converged on **excellent diagnosis and absent execution**. It names your problem with uncomfortable precision, delivers a reframe, and stops.

The audience knows. The single most-liked comment relevant to method, in the entire corpus, is on Better Ideas' *8 steps to unf\*\*\* your life*:

> **"Step 9: Stop watching these videos. You already know what to do."** — 33,000 likes

And because the videos ship no machinery, **the audience builds it in the comment box.**

> **Correction, recorded rather than quietly fixed.** The table below was originally ranked and argued by like count. That was wrong, and the data is unambiguous: **of the 100 most-liked comments in the corpus, zero report anything that actually happened.** Likes measure wit and recognition inside one video's audience — the right test for a hook, the wrong test for a finding. Ranked properly, by how many unconnected channels show the same thing, the picture changes: the strongest item here is a viewer writing out the video's own steps (**41 of 91 videos, 28 channels**), while the like-button-as-reminder device that got headline treatment holds up on **2 videos**. Full ranking and method in the table further down; reproduce with `~/.cache/cyl-corpus/convergence.py`. Read the list below as *individually striking examples*, not as a measure of prevalence:

| What they improvised | Where | Likes |
|---|---|---|
| Dated public commitment, then returned **a year later** to edit the same comment with the outcome | Mateusz M, *Unbroken* | 4,200 |
| "I want to come back here 10 years from now and say, 'I did it'" — updated in 2026 with her nursing-board pass | Team Fearless | 7,000 |
| The like button repurposed as a **reminder service**: "whenever someone likes this comment" | TEDx / von Fliss | 4,600 |
| A bookmark, because the app has no "return tomorrow": "Plz someone like this so that i can come back" | Better Ideas | 2,400 |
| Hand-typed step lists extracting the framework the video never stated | Odysseas 7,000 · Better Ideas 5,700 · Tenzin Lhoden 2,500 · Thewizardliz (9 steps) |
| A self-invented commitment device: "I will do 1 push up per like, per day" | Hamza | 286 |
| Day counters nobody asked them to keep: "day 523", "Today is day 1000", "24 weeks strong" | Clear · Better Ideas · Dr. Marks |
| A bean jar — one bean moved per completed session — because the episode shipped no tracker | Mel Robbins / Clear | 210 |
| **The same 4-step list reconstructed by three separate commenters** because the talk never numbered its own steps | Judson Brewer, TED | 9,300 / 364 / 316 |

Dated pledge → scheduled return → visible log → stranger audience. That is a complete accountability product, invented **because none of the videos provide one** — though measured properly it is narrower than that list makes it look: the pledge appears under 6 videos on 5 channels, and the return-and-report under 6 videos on 5 channels. Real, and not genre-wide.

### Ranked by convergence instead of likes

How widely each comment-derived finding actually appears. This is the defensible ranking; the like column is included precisely so the disagreement between the two is visible.

| Finding | Videos | Channels | Median likes | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Note the top and bottom rows: the best-evidenced finding has a fifth of the likes of the worst-evidenced one. Sorting this layer by popularity inverts it.

Every count is a **lower bound** — regexes over free text catch a fraction of how people phrase things, and only the top ~20 comments per video were harvested. A low count means "not demonstrated", never "does not happen".

**What this changes downstream.** The constraints stage of the flow design is *better* supported than I first argued, just on different evidence — 15 videos across 14 channels, rather than one video's 83,000 likes. The dated-commitment stage is *thinner* than implied and should be built as a bet, not a certainty. And the "audience built the missing product" headline survives, but its load is now carried by the step-lists finding rather than by the bookmark anecdote.

### Resonance tracks recognition, not instruction

The metrics say something counterintuitive and directly useful. Ranking all 91 videos by like-rate (age- and size-independent, so it measures resonance rather than algorithmic push):

- **The ten lowest-resonance videos in the corpus are dominated by tactical dating listicles** — seven of the bottom ten are DATE-category how-to content. Coach Corey Wayne 1.10%, TopThink's *How to Get a Girlfriend in 7 Steps* 1.65%, Courtney Ryan 1.72%, alpha m. 1.85%.
- **The highest-resonance categories are the ones that make the viewer feel seen**: masculine self-improvement 5.08% median, anti-self-help 5.08% median — against a corpus median of 3.62%. Top individual performers: struthless 8.34%, Odysseas 8.31%, Andrew Kirby 7.62%, Better Than Yesterday 7.09%, Sisyphus 55 7.06%.
- Adam Leipzig's *How to know your life purpose in 5 minutes* has **20.4M views and a 1.31% like-rate** — one of the weakest in the corpus. Twenty million people watched and did not feel helped.

**For the short: lead with recognition, not with tips.** A list of tactics is the lowest-performing shape in this entire dataset.

### The seven things every video assumes and never provides

Ranked by how consistently the gap appears and how loudly the comments demand it:

1. **A relapse protocol.** Near-universal absence. Hamza *names* the failure point — "The fourth day or close enough is the day that it just comes crashing down" — and then never addresses it. Project 50's rule is to restart from day one on any miss; its comment section is full of day-one pledges and contains essentially no finishers. Only Dr. Tracey Marks handles it: *"Relapse isn't failure, it's feedback"* and *"It's the return, not the mistake, that builds resilience."*
2. **Selection.** Everyone teaches review; nobody teaches choosing. Ali Abdaal names his goal-selection system — "the GPS system which is my personal favorite" — and skips explaining it. struthless's life-changing one-thing was chosen *for him by a mentor*, the one input a viewer cannot get from the video. Odysseas refuses outright: "your system is personal. I really don't want to tell you how to do this."
3. **Duration — and where it is stated, it is wildly incoherent.** Dr. K makes it his thesis: *"everyone tells you what to do no one tells you how long it'll take and that's what screws people"* — his answer is three to seven years. Iman Gadzhi says five years minimum, "I've never seen it happen in less than five years." James Clear talks in two-year and ten-year arcs. Matt D'Avella runs 30 days. Mel Robbins' episode featuring Clear is titled *How to Change Your Life in 1 Month* while Clear says identity payoff "sometimes takes a decade or more." Kurzgesagt is the most honest: "anything between 15 and 250 days… You won't know how long it will take for you." A viewer sampling this genre receives estimates spanning **30 days to 15 years for the same request**.
4. **Measurement.** Roughly nine of 91 prescribe any tracking artifact at all. James Clear's wall-calendar chain is the strongest, and notably it *is* the reward, not just the record. Everyone else offers a feeling.
5. **A constraints layer.** Money, hours, dependants, housing control, health — assumed, never entered. Iman Gadzhi's protocol requires relocating to Cape Town and a gym on the 75th floor. Thewizardliz's first step is "start looking for a job in a different country." James Clear's system needs a live-in partner to enforce $10 forfeits. The audience answering these videos says it is broke, young, living with controlling parents, working two jobs, or caring for someone.

   The cleanest measurement of this gap: Matt D'Avella's *I quit sugar for 30 days* prescribes purging your kitchen, and its **two highest-liked comments — 83,000 likes combined — are people saying they do not control their kitchen.** "it's so much easier when you don't have a family that's also addicted" (41,000). "I'm 17, and my family eats horribly."

   The sharpest version is Dr. K's *Why The Dating Crisis Is Just Natural Selection*: he proves at length that unstructured face-to-face social contact has disappeared, then prescribes party eye-contact games, warm introductions from friends, and platonic female friendships — all of which require the access he just demonstrated the viewer has lost. A commenter states it exactly: *"How about we start with a realistic situation, actually?"*
6. **Candidate generation.** Purpose frameworks *format* an answer you already have; they don't produce one. Leipzig's question two — what are you supremely qualified to teach? — is where twenty million people stop. The top comment (23,000 likes) is *"I'm mad jealous of people who know what they want to do,"* later edited to *"not only did i not find my purpose, but my life has been on an exponential decay since. sorry that my situation didn't become inspirational. i tried, i really did."*
7. **A route from level −5 to level 1.** Named exactly by a commenter on Todd V: *"All the programs and coaches miss this very important step of getting from lvl -5 to lv1."* Videos begin above where the viewer is standing.

### The five claims almost everyone agrees on

1. **Willpower is the villain; environment is the fix.** The broadest consensus in the corpus, and it crosses every category — Kurzgesagt, Clear, Hormozi, Dr. Marks, Better Than Yesterday, D'Avella, Justin Sung, Newel of Knowledge. Dr. Marks gives the best mechanism: willpower lives in the prefrontal cortex, habits in the basal ganglia, so "you're asking your conscious brain to fight an automatic system that's **faster older and stronger**."
2. **Act first, feel second.** Hormozi's "emotion follows motion", struthless's "action comes before motivation", Thomas Frank's "acting in accordance with your thoughts, not your feelings", Todd V's "approaching is primarily a physical act", and Academy of Ideas' Morita-therapy version — act *while* anxious or depressed, because the feelings are a symptom of not acting.
3. **Identity beats goals.** Clear's "every action is a vote", Abdaal's "will you vote is an action, will you *be a voter* is an identity", Thomas Frank's "No thanks, I'm not a smoker", Kirby's explicit "we're focusing on identity level change we're not focused on processes or systems."
4. **Knowing is never the bottleneck.** Manson: "learning can feel like progress even when it's not progress." Sivers via Ferriss: "If more information were the answer, we'd all be billionaires with six pack abs." Behaviour-change scientist Shahroo Izadi: people "don't know why they're not doing it despite wanting to do it."
5. **Small beats big.** Two-minute rule, ten squats, "so small that you can't lose", "lower your standards until you get started."

### Where they genuinely disagree — this is the valuable part

These are unacknowledged, high-stakes conflicts. Each is a design decision the product must make deliberately.

| # | The split | Side A | Side B |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Tell someone / tell no one** | Clear: accountability partner, habit contract, $10 forfeit. Sisyphus 55, on suicidality: low social support and "an aversion to self-disclosure" are *primary predictors* | Better Ideas: telling people is "genuinely destructive" — "you've been generously rewarded for inaction". Thewizardliz: keep goals private, the evil eye is real |
| 2 | **Accept yourself / refuse to** | Izadi: "tough love when you're speaking to yourself often isn't very smart love". Beck, Manson | Leo Skepi: "I don't want to accept myself how I am"; accept *where* you are, not *who*. Thewizardliz: "always be kind to yourself… that's toxic". Hamza: "have a little bit of hatred towards yourself" |
| 3 | **Measure / don't measure** | Hormozi: "confidence comes from data, not dopamine". Clear: don't break the chain | Martha Beck: "don't be counting things and measuring how it's going to happen, that's all left hemisphere thinking". Better Ideas: tracking becomes "completely obsolete" |
| 4 | **Inner work first / reps first** | Dr. K: diagnose, then identity, beliefs, emotions — gym and flirting come *last*. TopThink gates dating behind "are you happy being single?" | Todd V: leave the house, walk over, say anything. Better Ideas: stop cerebralizing, "get out the door". Dr. K half-reverses himself: "at some point your ass needs to go out and learn how to flirt" |
| 5 | **Immersion / tiny steps** | Monk mode (21 days–3 months), Project 50, 24-hour dopamine detox | Clear's two minutes, Hormozi's "so small you can't lose", Ferriss's "lower your standards until you get started" |
| 6 | **Your fault / not your fault** | The genre's default personal-responsibility frame | Man Carrying Thing: "the issue is that no one wants to hire entry-level positions". Memeable Data: the app's math, not your profile. Top comment on Manson (11,000 likes): inherited wealth |
| 7 | **Find your passion / build it** | Leipzig: what are you supremely qualified to teach? | Academy of Ideas: "very often our passions follow the development of our skills". A high-school counsellor in Abdaal's comments: "You don't *find* your passion. You develop it" |
| 8 | **Goals / anti-goals** | Abdaal: write them down, review weekly, 42% more likely | Clear: "FORGET ABOUT GOALS, FOCUS ON SYSTEMS INSTEAD" — "successful and unsuccessful people share the same goals" |
| 9 | **Scarcity / transparency** (dating) | Coach Corey Wayne: "women are more attracted to guys they have to work for"; This.: be scarce, "hanging a limited edition sign on your neck" | Psych2Go: "don't try to play it cool or play hard to get… be honest about who you are and how you feel". Henry Grey Earls: "being emotionally available is a power move" |

Conflict #1 carries real risk and the product must not treat it as a preference. Sisyphus 55's video on suicidality is in this corpus precisely because the "tell no one" advice, applied to a user in crisis, is dangerous. Privacy can be the default; isolation cannot be the prescription.

### What this means for the product

The gap is not information. It is the **execution layer between the reframe and Monday morning** — and the audience is so starved of it that it improvises the thing in comment boxes on videos that are ten years old.

Two design commitments fall straight out of the evidence and are genuinely differentiating:

- **Selection and constraints come before goals.** Every framework in the corpus starts one step past where the user is standing. Ours starts at the constraint ledger and the differential.
- **The app is not another consumption surface.** Dr. K's mechanism — content feels free, so the brain refuses to pay the real cost of change — is the sharpest critique of the entire category, and it applies to us. Content should unlock *after* a logged rep, never instead of one.

## Part B — Evidence detail

### B1. Performance measurement, and its limits

The headline metric is a **performance multiplier**: this video's views-per-day divided by the median views-per-day of *the same channel's other uploads from the same era* (within half to double its age; ≥6 peers required). It answers "did this topic outperform what this creator normally does?", which raw view counts cannot.

**A correction worth recording.** The first version compared each video to the channel's 30 most recent uploads and ranked *Atomic Habits — APB Speakers* at **1711×**. That was an artifact: an old viral hit on a 45k-subscriber channel that has since gone dormant, so the denominator had collapsed. Comparing a six-year-old video to last month's uploads compares two different eras of a channel. Age-matching removed the artifact. Nineteen videos still have no reliable baseline and are reported with **no number rather than a fake one**.

Two caveats to state whenever this table is used:

- **The ratio inflates on small channels**, where peer videos have few views. Tier is the headline; the number is supporting detail.
- **Selection bias.** Videos were chosen for high view counts, so this corpus studies *what worked*. The near-absence of underperformers is a property of the selection, not a finding.

Distribution: 27 breakout (≥10×), 30 strong (3–10×), 10 above baseline, 6 on baseline, 1 underperformed, 19 no baseline.

### B3. Highest-signal individual videos

Flagged independently by multiple reading agents:

- **`ioa6BjuSOt4` HealthyGamerGG — *Getting a Girlfriend is NOT an Achievable Goal***. The only video in 91 that names duration as the missing variable. Comments are the clearest evidence of the failure mode the whole corpus is about: people who complied for years and got nothing. Top comment, 9,700 likes: *"'Work on yourself' is the equivalent of 'Have you tried turning it on and off?'"*
- **`05tG47pv1vM` HealthyGamerGG — *How To Get A Girlfriend***. "The number one reason why problems persist is because theyve made a diagnostic error not a treatment error" — plus the admission that accurate self-diagnosis needs a professional, and no self-serve differential exists. Directly buildable.
- **`x3lypVnJ0HM` Memeable Data — *Why Men Get So Few Matches on Dating Apps***. 6.4M views, 21,080 comments, a fully quantified mechanism ("25 → 12 → 7 → 1") and zero prescription. The largest diagnosis-to-action vacuum in the corpus, at scale.
- **`vVsXO9brK7M` TEDx / Leipzig — *How to know your life purpose in 5 minutes***. 20.4M views; the framework formats a purpose you already have and cannot generate one. The comment thread is twenty million people stalled at the same question.
- **`44iAPrQoYU8` Shahroo Izadi — *How I Lost 120lbs With Kindness***. Best single exercise in the corpus: write what you'd say to a loved one who fell off track, cross out their name, write your own.
- **`2bQjfTHlwzU` Todd V — *Approaching Girls made Easy***. The only mechanism in the corpus with reported behaviour change from self-described level −5 people, because **every rung ends with explicit permission to walk away**.
- **`_XIihESyy5g` Better Ideas — *8 steps to get your sh\*\* together***. "Tell no one" — the most argument-generating claim in the corpus and one half of divergence #1.
- **`-moW9jvvMr4` Judson Brewer, TED — *A Simple Way to Break a Bad Habit***. Best mechanism in the corpus for breaking something: don't override the urge, get curious about it until you're disenchanted — "we even told them to smoke." The talk never numbers its steps and three separate commenters reconstruct the identical four-step list. Missing: a dose, a failure branch, and any lapse re-entry.
- **`yJc4Fst1LMg` Coach Kyle — *How To Approach ANY GIRL in Public***. The most diagrammable mechanism in the corpus and the one closest to this product's core: approach geometry (behind = creepy, head-on = flight response, side-and-slightly-ahead = safe), and "the open actually starts the moment the girl sees you."
- **`Scwsg9Ai3rg` Sisyphus 55 — *The Myth of Self Improvement***. Read this one as a **design constraint, not a source**. It argues that compulsive self-improvement is the delivery mechanism for burnout, and it names tracking apps as the pathology: "the auto exploiting subject carries around its own labor camp." Its own audience catches the trap — "Great! Now that i know i can plan to be more spontainious."

### B4. Data-quality notes for anyone reusing this

- **Astroturf is pervasive.** Book-promo bot rings run fixed templates ("Wisebits", "Axalem", "Manifest and Receive by Eva Hartley", "Clara Hartley's anxiety quiz") across at least eight of these comment sections with bought like counts. 38 comments (14,810 likes) were filtered; the filter is in `~/.cache/cyl-corpus/comments.py` and should be re-run and extended before any new comment analysis.
- **Three videos have no transcript** (2 have no English captions, 1 hit a persistent HTTP 429 on the subtitle endpoint): `H-XfCl-HpRM`, `OfsTJnpRxeg`, `HUvLE7BlB40`. 91 of 94 were read.
- **Category labels are approximate.** Reading agents flagged mislabels: Kurzgesagt `75d_29QWELk` and von Fliss `eVFzbxmKNUw` are GEN not DATE; Tenzin Lhoden `HWAt8j9WLB0` is GEN not ANTI; Hormozi `7e6RSGdvHD8` is GEN not DATE. Counts by category are indicative, not exact.
- Transcripts are auto-captions: no punctuation, occasional mis-hearings (struthless's "ibis" becomes "iris"). Quotes are reproduced as the captions render them.
- **Three factual defects found in source videos — do not reuse these claims.** (1) *This. — The Science Of Getting Girls* misdescribes its own Ben Franklin study: the cited 1971 experiment concerns insults vs encouragement and does not show that asking favours creates liking. The same video contradicts itself on genetics within two minutes, and an 8,200-like comment catches it. (2) Ali Abdaal's "write down your goals → 42% more likely" comes from the Dominican University study, which is widely contested and presented without caveat. (3) Team Fearless's billionaire-reading roll-call has been disavowed by the channel itself.
- **Content warning on `RrO_ahijc4g` (Iman Gadzhi, *5 Traits of The Top 1% Man*).** Its cold open is deliberate emotional coercion — "imagine her with another guy make it graphic make it sting make it hurt" — and the video narrates three weeks of the creator's own suicidal planning with an untrained marathon presented as the cure. Do not reuse that segment in any form.
- Baseline views are rounded to three significant figures and dates are coarse ("1 year ago"), which is fine for a median but means the multiplier is an indicator, not a measurement.
