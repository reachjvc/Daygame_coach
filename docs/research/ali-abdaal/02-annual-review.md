## 1. Overview

Ali Abdaal's annual system is **not one fixed process** — it is a lineage of increasingly structured versions, running roughly 2017 → 2026. Three things are stable across all of them:

1. **Reflect on the year that's ending** (calendar-walk + gratitude + lessons).
2. **Work out direction** — what he called "bucket list" early on, then "visualisation exercises", then **"Find your North Star"**.
3. **Convert direction into something executable** — early on he refused to set goals at all; later this became SMART goals, then the "12-Month Celebration", then **Quarterly Quests + the GPS Method**.

The single biggest change over the years: **he moved AWAY from annual goals.** In Dec 2024 he opens his flagship annual-planning video with:

> "habit number one that I have gotten a lot of value out of is kind of ignoring the notion of annual goals … this second method of goal setting is what I refer to as quarterly quests"
> — *6 Habits to Make 2025 Your Best Year Yet* (YouTube `W2afI0n8pUk`, 12 Dec 2024) **[VERIFIED]**

So the modern system is: **annual reflection + long-horizon direction → an annual theme → 90-day Quarterly Quests → weekly/daily execution.** The annual layer sets direction; the quarter is the real planning unit.

## 3. The current full process (numbered, with verbatim prompts)

### Step 0 — Setup
- **[VERIFIED]** He does it in a written document you keep forever: *"the nice thing about writing these down is that you can then look back on your Annual Planning documents every year – it's a nice way to actually remember your life a little more"* (`my-annual-reflection-goal-setting-method`).
- **[VERIFIED]** Strong preference for doing it **in a group**: *"I find it much more inspiring and useful to work through them during a dedicated session with other people also doing the same thing"*; he runs it at team retreats and creator retreats, *"with [a] yoga + meditation Spotify playlist in the background"*.
- **[VERIFIED]** Intuition over analysis: *"if you **think** too hard about them, you'll work yourself into knots. But if you **don't think too hard** about them, and go with your 'gut' or your 'body' or your 'soul' … you'll find the answers will come to you a lot more readily."*
- **[INFERRED]** In the live 2024 workshop he time-boxed each prompt tightly (reportedly ~3-minute writing windows) to force intuitive answers — per the neurohackingly review, not from Ali directly.

---

### Step 1 — Reflect on the year

**Verbatim, from the official 2024 Annual Planning Workshop workbook** (Scribd `715663563`) **[VERIFIED]** — 8 prompts in this order:

1. **Key Events**: "What actually happened in 2023? Take a look through your calendar and note down the key moments from each month." — the workbook lists January through December with bullet slots, and Ali's own worked example: *"Trip to Pakistan for Ayesha's wedding / Dinner with school friends for the first time in 3 years / You get the idea…"*
2. **Major Milestones**: "What were the 3-5 most significant events or achievements in 2023 for you? How did these impact your life?"
3. **Gratitude**: "What are you most grateful for in 2023? Think about people, experiences, or opportunities that enriched your life."
4. **Challenges Overcome**: "What were the biggest challenges or obstacles you faced in 2023? How did you overcome them, and what did you learn from these experiences?"
5. **Unfulfilled Aspirations**: "Were there goals or aspirations you had for 2023 that you did not achieve? Reflect on why they were not met and how you feel about it."
6. **Relationships and Connections**: "Reflect on your relationships in 2023. How have they evolved? Were there new relationships that had a significant impact on you?"
7. **Professional or Academic Growth**: "How did you progress in your career or studies in 2023? What were the key learnings and how have they shaped your future aspirations?"
8. **Looking Forward**: "Based on your experiences in 2023, what would you like to do differently in 2024? Are there new areas you'd like to explore or changes you want to make?"

**The Nov-2025 short-form version of Step 1** (TikTok, promoting Spark 2026) **[VERIFIED]** compresses it to four questions:
> "What were your key achievements? / What are you proud of? / What challenges did you face? / What new relationships did you develop?"

---

### Step 2 — Find your North Star

**Verbatim, 2024 workbook** **[VERIFIED]** — 5 prompts, in order:

1. **The Ideal Tuesday Prompt**: "What does your ideal ordinary Tuesday look like? What are you doing? Who are you with? How are you spending your time? What makes this day perfect for you?"
2. **The Fearless Dream Prompt**: "What would you like to do, if you knew you couldn't fail?"
3. **The Fail-Proof Dream Prompt**: "What would you like to do, even if you knew you'd fail at it?"
4. **The Future TED Talk Prompt**: "You're attending the TED conference, and everyone in the audience (including you) is deeply moved and inspired by what the speaker is talking about. The speaker is you, 20 years in the future. What is 'You + 20 Years' talking about, and what's so inspiring about it?"
5. **The Obituary Writing Prompt**: "What do you want your obituary to say? Write it out."

Purpose, in his words **[VERIFIED]**:
> "by thinking about where your *North Star* is, you'll be able to look at your life right now and ask: 'To what extent is what I'm currently doing aligned with this future that I think I want?'"

**Predecessor version (2021)** **[VERIFIED]**: instead of the Ideal Tuesday he used two **"ideal ordinary week"** visualisations — *"five years from now you write down in great detail what your ideal ordinary week looks like"*, then the same for *"this time next year"*. His warning against lazy visions: *"a lot of us have this idea of I want to do the same but more of it … we really want to question that"*, and *"the most important thing to come out of this is actually what are you **not** doing … what is no longer a priority and then why are you still doing that thing right now?"*

### Step 3 — Dreams (divergent)

**[VERIFIED]** Set a **10-minute timer** and list everything you might want *over the next 10 years* (`my-2024-goals-method`). Framing sentence: **"At some point in the next 10 years…"**

The **10 Dream prompts**, verbatim from the 2024 workbook (workbook order):
1. What do you want to learn?
2. What do you want to see?
3. What do you want to have?
4. Who do you want to be?
5. What do you want to try?
6. What do you want to do?
7. Where do you want to go?
8. What do you want to create?
9. What do you want to contribute to?
10. What do you want to overcome?

**[VERIFIED]** In the newsletter version the last prompt is "What do you want to experience?" rather than "overcome", and he star-marks his four favourites: *"I've personally gotten the most mileage out of the first four"* — **learn / have / be / do**.

### Step 4 — Timeframe tagging

**[VERIFIED]** Verbatim from the workbook:
> "Now, put a NUMBER (1, 3, 5 or 10) by each of your dreams. That number represents how many years (roughly) the timeline for that dream is. Eg: 'Learn Mandarin - 10' 'Organise a surfing trip - 1' 'Get to 10M subscribers - 5'"

---

### Step 5 — Goals (convergent)

**[VERIFIED]** (`my-2024-goals-method`): "look through your list … and decide on **3-5 things** you want to set as your 'Goals' for 1 year, 3 years, 5 years and 10 years. **Ideally there would be a balance here amongst work / health / relationships.**"

His stated purpose for the 3/5/10-year tiers **[VERIFIED]**:
> "The point of setting these 'goals' for the 3-10 year time horizon isn't really to actually work towards them right now … they're purely a reflection of the direction that I'd like to be headed towards right now."

**Alternative / current goal-setting move — the "12-Month Celebration"** (Dec 2023 video) **[VERIFIED]**:
- Prompt: *"In each of the salient areas of your life, ask yourself, what would I like to be celebrating 12 months from now?"* Imagine sitting at dinner with a friend, celebrating the progress.
- Then converge: *"if I had to boil it down to just one thing and I could only celebrate one thing, what would that one thing be?"* — one goal per area, because *"generally having a single goal to work towards helps the brain focus."*
- Add the suffix (credited to Dicky Bush): **"while enjoying every step along the way."**
- Write it down somewhere you'll see it. Then add the **why**.

## 4. Legacy prompt sets (still useful, still his)

### 4.1 The 2020 question set — verbatim

Source: `aliabdaal.com/newsletter/the-most-important-thing-i-do-each-year/` **[VERIFIED]**. Framing quote he opens with: *"Human systems grow toward what they persistently ask questions about." — David Cooperrider and Diana Whitney*

**The bare-minimum questions (3):**
- When have you felt proud of yourself in 2020? what were you doing?
- If you had to teach one thing you learnt this year (that would improve one's quality of life) what would that be?
- What kept you up at night with excitement this year? Was it worth it? Would you want to do more of it?

**Give-me-more questions (18):**
- How did you have fun differently in 2020?
- How did you suffer differently in 2020?
- What people/kind of people did you spend less time with in 2020?
- What people/kind of people did you spend more time with in 2020?
- What did you feel guilty for in 2020?
- What 3 people are you the most grateful to in 2020 and why? How can you thank them? How can you find more people like this in your life?
- What have you learnt about the way that you deal with uncertainty in 2020?
- What new habits did you create in 2020? Which ones would you want to keep?
- Did you feel you spent enough time with your family/significant other in 2020? How would you change this in 2021?
- What things did you stop caring about in 2020?
- What new thing did you spend a lot of money on in 2020?
- What new thing did you spend a lot of time on in 2020?
- What do you feel you've gotten a lot better at in 2020?
- What did you rediscover pleasure in in 2020?
- When you felt at peace this year, what were you doing?
- Which of last years goals did you not achieve? Why did you not achieve them? Will you carry them on to 2021 and if not why?
- How much closer are you to your "by this age" milestones (if you have any)? Do you find yourself caring less or more about these?
- At the end of next year, if you are drastically exceeding your expectations, what would you be doing?

His own published answers to several of these are in `my-annual-review-2020`. Closing line **[VERIFIED]**: *"However much time and effort you put into these, I think it's worth doing an Annual Review in some capacity, even if it's just a 5-minute job."*

### 4.2 The Odyssey Plan — verbatim

Source: `the-odyssey-plan-revisited` and `how-i-design-my-life` **[VERIFIED]**. He calls it *"a life-design exercise from Stanford Business School"* (elsewhere: Stanford Life Design Lab, from *Designing Your Life* by Bill Burnett & Dave Evans).

The three prompts, exactly as he writes them:
1. "Write out, in detail, what your life would look like 5 years from now if you continued down your current path."
2. "Write out, in detail, what your life would look like 5 years from now if you took a completely different path."
3. "Write out, in detail, what your life would look like 5 years from now if money, social obligations, and what people would think, were irrelevant."

**[VERIFIED]** Time taken: *"after writing for an hour, I got the main gist of my 3 life paths."*
**[VERIFIED]** His own headings were 🛣 **Current Path**, 🚐 **Alternative**, and a third (unlabelled in the published version). His post-exercise reflections are a good model of how to *use* the output: he looked for **common threads** across all three paths — *"All 3 life paths seemed to have the common thread of 'stop worrying about numbers, views, revenue'"*, *"All 3 life paths also involve me being married with 2 kids"*, all three involved reading/writing/making videos.
**[NOT VERIFIED]** The *Designing Your Life* book adds a scoring step per path (resources / likability / confidence / coherence, 1–5). **Ali does not mention this scoring anywhere I found.** Don't attribute it to him.

### 4.3 The 2021 "anti-wasteman system" (execution table)

Source: YouTube `ERGbgvvCJ8o` **[VERIFIED]**. A table, one row per dream, with these columns in order:

| Column | Prompt |
|---|---|
| Dream | The emotionally resonant version ("learn to draw like a concept artist"). He distinguishes: *"a dream is like emotionally resonant"* vs. a goal. Credits August Bradley's "value goal". |
| Why | "why do I want to do the thing" |
| SMART goal | Convert the dream to specific/measurable/achievable/realistic/time-bound |
| How surprised would I be if I failed? | Rated **1–10** ("so how surprised would I be if I actually didn't end up doing this thing by this time next year") |
| Top 3 reasons why you might fail | Pre-mortem |
| Who can help me achieve this goal? | Explicitly *"who comes first"* — cites *Who Not How* (Hardy & Sullivan) |
| How can I stack the deck in my favour to succeed? | Time-blocking, courses, accountability buddy |
| **Take action right now** | *"what is the action step that you're going to do right now when you're doing your annual review"* — he signed up to an online art course mid-review and had a teacher within days |

## 5. The Wheel of Life — full spec

**Primary source: Ali's own words in YouTube `c_DOG_mXz5w`, 22 Dec 2023 — "How to Make Next Year The Best Year of Your Life". [VERIFIED]**

### Structure
Draw a circle → split into **3 segments**: **Work, Health, Relationships** → split each third into **3** → **9 segments**. Then a **10th category (Joy)** which sits **outside/underneath the wheel**, not as a wheel segment.

### The exact life areas (verbatim from his transcript)
> "within health, we have three things. And that is **body, mind, and soul**. or you can call it physical health, mental health, spiritual health, but I kind of like body, mind, and soul because it sounds more cute. Under relationships, we've got **romance, family, and friends**. And then under work, we have **mission, money, and growth**. And you can call mission your career or job or whatever you want to call it, but that's kind of the main thing that you were doing for work."

| Domain | Areas |
|---|---|
| **Health** | Body · Mind · Soul |
| **Relationships** | Romance · Family · Friends |
| **Work** | Mission · Money · Growth |
| *(off-wheel)* | **Joy** |

**⚠️ Do not confuse this with the outdated third-party list "body, mind, soul, money, career, mission, friends, family, romance" that some blogs cite — the Work triad is Mission / Money / **Growth**, per Ali's own transcript.**

### Scale and what is being rated
- **Scale: 0 to 10.** Verbatim: *"we want to rate on a scale from 0 to 10 how aligned we feel in each of these different areas of our life."*
- Crucially it is **alignment, not satisfaction** **[VERIFIED]**:
  > "by aligned, I mean my actions in the here and now are consistent with where I think I would like to be … I prefer to think of it as alignment rather than satisfaction because … you might be a 3 out of 10 in terms of satisfaction with money because you're not yet a millionaire … but you might be a 10 out of 10 in alignment because you are taking the actions necessary to get to where you want to be."
- Joy is rated with the same alignment framing: *"How aligned do you feel with your actions in the here and now compared to where you want to be in terms of the amount of joy you are experiencing in your life?"*
- **Shading:** if pen-and-paper, shade in each wedge to its score (10 = full, 5 = half), producing *"this sort of FIFA radar chart"*.

### What he does with low scores
**[VERIFIED]** Two moves, both in his words:
1. **Circle one per domain**: *"it is worth highlighting or underlining or circling … in each of these different areas in work in health and relationships. What is the one that really stands out to you as needing some amount of work?"* — his own example: friends 4/10 (circled), soul 4/10 (**not** circled — *"I'm not overly concerned about that"*), body 5/10 (circled), mission 9, money 9, growth 7/10 (circled).
   Note: the criterion is not "lowest score" — it's *lowest score I actually care about*.
2. **Feed the circled areas into goal-setting**: *"whenever you have a sense of where you are now and where you want to be, that is what creates … goals"*, then run the **12-Month Celebration** on them (all 10 areas if doing a full annual plan; the 3 circled ones if doing a quick pass).

**Explicitly not for self-flagellation** **[VERIFIED]**: *"that's not to beat yourself up … This is a pure solid inventory of where you are."*

### Time and cadence
- **[VERIFIED]** *"it literally takes 30 seconds to just bring up these different 9 to 10 categories and to just rate yourself out of 10 on each one."* Re-runnable *"whenever you feel like your life is somewhat misaligned … or you feel a bit burnt out."*
- **[VERIFIED]** He also runs it live for groups (~100 people in Austin, from `QFeYISWoPh4`).
- There is a hosted quiz version at **play.aliabdaal.com/wheeloflife** ("less than 5 minutes … you'll receive your current score and visual map", emails you "your personalised Wheel of Life with suggestions on how to re-balance it"). **[VERIFIED]** that the quiz exists and is his; **[NOT VERIFIED]** its internal question wording — the quiz body is behind a form and I could not read the item text.

## 7. The cascade — annual → quarterly → weekly → daily

The handoff is explicit and it is the **Quarterly Quest** that does the work.

**LifeOS course architecture** (`aliabdaal.com/spark/`) **[VERIFIED]**:
- **Pillar 1 — Vision**: *Life Compass* ("4-part framework" to align actions with values) → *Future Sketch* ("a flexible **3-year roadmap**") → *Quarterly Quests* ("The **GPS Method** will help you break down your ambitious yearly goals into actionable **12-week sprints**, complete with milestone tracking").
- **Pillar 2 — Action**: *Focus Hours* → *Productive Days* ("bridges the gap between your long-term vision and daily execution") → *Balanced Weeks*.
- Time cost: *"2-3 hours for initial setup, then 30-60 minutes weekly for planning and reflection."*

### 7.1 Quarterly Quests — the 2024 GPS worksheet (verbatim field prompts)

**[VERIFIED]** from the June 2024 Alignment workbook. Each quest gets a **fun name**, then:

**GOAL**
- **Facts**: "What will be true when you've accomplished the goal? Describe the tangible outcomes … These are the facts that will confirm your success."
- **Feelings**: "How will you (and/or others around you) feel when you've accomplished the goal?"
- **Function**: "What function does this goal unlock? … What new opportunities or benefits will it provide?"

**PLAN**
- **Steps**: "What are the 3–5 major steps in your plan to get you to your goal?"
- **Schedule**: "What's the rough schedule, or timeline? Estimate a timeframe for each step."
- **Support**: "Who could you enlist for help?"
- **Snags**: "What potential obstacles might you encounter, and how would you overcome them?"

**SYSTEM**
- **Actions**: "What daily or weekly actions will help you get to the goal?"
- **Accountability**: "How will you track your progress? Who's your accountability buddy or group?"
- **Adaptation**: "How will you adapt if things don't go according to plan?"

### 7.2 GPS Method — the 2025 rewrite (current)

**[VERIFIED]** from `D_KSR3S6W8I` (Dec 2025). Same GPS acronym, **different sub-fields** — this is the newest version and it supersedes 7.1:

- **G — Goal**: (1) **What** — specific and concrete, ideally numeric; (2) **Why** — ideally intrinsic, not "should"; (3) **Anti-goals** — *"What are the things you want to avoid on route to achieving your goal?"* (e.g. "I don't want to have to work on the weekends").
- **P — Plan**: (1) *"what are the three to five **major moves** that will get me to my goal"*; (2) two realism percentages — **in theory** ("assuming we followed the plan to 100% accuracy, would we actually get to the goal?") and **in practice** ("will I actually follow the plan?"). *"if any of these numbers is under 80% … that is a sign that I need to rethink my plan."*; (3) the **crystal ball method** — *"you did not achieve your goal … What are the top three reasons as to why?"*, then a countermeasure for each. He credits Gabriele Oettingen / WOOP.
- **S — System**: (1) **Tracking** ("how can I track my progress towards it?" — cites a meta-analysis of 138 studies / ~20,000 participants); (2) **Reminders** ("how will you remind yourself of your goal and of your plan" — daily journaling, weekly review, recurring calendar blocks, vision board as desktop wallpaper, the Momentum app); (3) **Accountability** (buddy, squad, WhatsApp group).
- Time: *"less than 10 minutes per goal that you set, maybe even 5 minutes"*. Also usable *"as a diagnostic tool"* on a goal you're already stuck on.

### 7.3 Quarterly cadence

**[VERIFIED]**
- **3–4 quests max per quarter**, split into two categories, **Work** and **Life** (`W2afI0n8pUk`). Later hardened further: *"setting a primary 'Quarterly Quest' for work and for life – the ONE THING, in each of these areas, that if you did in the next 90 days, would have the biggest impact"* (`my-biggest-mistake-with-goal-setting`).
- **[INFERRED]** Third-party accounts add "up to 3 side quests per area, with less emphasis on completion" — plausible but I did not find this in an Ali-authored source.
- Projects longer than 90 days get **broken into 90-day chunks**.
- He hosts a **free quarterly Alignment Workshop** on Zoom (~3,000 attendees, Jun 2024), plus branded "Spring Reset" / "Summer Reset" editions in 2026. Named cadence for 2026: Spring Reset 4 April, Summer Reset 4 July.
- **[VERIFIED]** Personal accountability loop: *"I have a Google doc that I use with my CEO coach, his name is Eric, and we look at the Google doc **every quarter** … we'll figure out, okay … for each of these different areas, what can we make tangible progress on **in the next 90 days**?"*

### 7.4 Weekly / daily

**[VERIFIED]**
- **Ideal Week** (from `c_DOG_mXz5w`): open a blank Google Calendar named "ideal week", block the non-negotiables (wake, sleep), then **find calendar time for each 12-Month Celebration goal**. If a goal has no time, *"you can change the goal … to reflect the time that you actually have."* This is the mechanism that reconciles ambition with reality.
- **Weekly review**: quarterly goals get re-read in the weekly planning session; the June-2024 workbook also references a **"Weekly Reflection Log"** as an input to the quarterly reflection.
- Reminders layer: recurring calendar blocks, vision board as desktop wallpaper, habit-tracker notifications.
