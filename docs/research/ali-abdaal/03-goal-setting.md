## 1. Overview

Ali Abdaal does not have one goal-setting system. He has had **at least four named systems in six years**, and his position on whether goals are even worth setting has publicly reversed twice. Anyone building on "Ali Abdaal's goal method" needs to specify *which era*.

The four named artefacts, in order:

| Era | Name | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| 2019–2022 | **Anti-Wasteman System** | An 8-field Notion table per goal: Dream → Why → SMART goal → habit → "how surprised would I be if I failed?" (1–10) → top 3 failure reasons → who can help / stack the deck → action right now. **[V]** |
| 2023–2024 (book) | **NICE goals** | Day-to-day goal format replacing SMART: Near-term, Input-based, Controllable, Energising. **[V-2nd]** |
| 2024– | **Quarterly Quests** + **12-Month Celebration** | 90-day goal cycles (3–4 max), seeded by an imagined future celebration, split work/life. **[V]** |
| 2024–present (current) | **GPS: Goal → Plan → System** | The current master framework. Nine sub-fields (3 per letter), including **anti-goals** and the **Crystal Ball method**. **[V]** |

The through-line across all four: **specificity, control, a compelling emotional "why", pre-mortem of failure modes, a written artefact, and a recurring review loop.** The thing that changes is the wrapper.

His single most stable belief, restated in almost every video: **the bottleneck is execution, not knowledge.** *"It's not a knowledge problem, it's an execution problem."* (quoting The 12 Week Year, endorsed) **[V]**

## 2. Evolution of his stance

### 2.1 Phase 0 — Anti-goal (pre-Dec 2020): "goals are pointless, focus on the system"

Verbatim opening of *I WAS WRONG - How I Set Goals* (31 Dec 2020) **[V]**:

> "so for a long time i used to think that goals were a bit pointless — what is the point of setting a goal if we can't control the outcome and we're just going to be unhappy until we hit the goal, and then when we hit the goal we're just going to set a new goal"

His two stated objections at the time **[V]**:
1. Goals "encourage us to focus too much on the destination and not enough on the journey."
2. Goals "restrict our happiness… when we set a goal we're making this implicit contract with ourselves, which is that once I reach this goal then I will be happy."

His practice then: *"for this YouTube channel… I've never really had any goals for it… what was more important than the goal was actually the system: I want to release at least one to three videos every week without fail."* **[V]** He explicitly credits James Clear / Atomic Habits for the systems-over-goals framing. **[V]**

### 2.2 Phase 1 — The public reversal (Dec 2020)

Same video, he retracts. The reversal was driven by two Deep Dive interviews: **August Bradley** (systems thinking) and **Benjamin Hardy** (psychologist, *Personality Isn't Permanent*). Seven reasons he lists **[V]**:

1. **The goal is the compass.** Bradley, quoted verbatim: *"the goal is the compass… at the same time a goal by itself isn't anything, you need a system and process to get there — you need both of them… they work complementary, they're perfectly in tandem."*
2. **A goal doesn't have to be a SMART goal.** Bradley: *"a goal could be 'I just want to be more relaxed in my life'… a peaceful existence… nothing scheduled on my calendar could be a goal… any goal is a valid goal if it's what matters to you."*
3. **You already have goals, you just haven't articulated them.** Hardy/Bradley called him out: *"I don't believe you don't have goals. I do believe you might not have clarity on them, but I don't believe that they're not in you."*
4. **All behaviour is outcome-oriented** (teleology / prospection).
5. **Be honest about what you want.** Hardy: *"I think the whole argument against goals goes against human nature."*
6. **Goals don't have to make you unhappy** — antidote is Dan Sullivan's *measure the gain, not the gap* (measure against your former self, not your ideal).
7. **Goals give you purpose** (Viktor Frankl: *"what man needs is not a tensionless state but a striving and struggling for a worthwhile goal."*)

His resolution is explicitly **non-resolution**: *"it's really about the yin and yang balance between self-improvement and self-acceptance… the two can coexist in harmony… rather than trying to solve the equation."* **[V]**

### 2.3 Phase 2 — "Screw SMART goals" / control-first (2021–2023, the book-writing years)

In *My New Life Goals (2022)* **[V]**:

> "i'm not really a huge fan of the whole smart goal vibe because… achievable and realistic are kind of the same thing, timely and sort of specific are kind of the same thing… but everyone talks about smart goal settings so we're gonna humour that"

And the control principle, stated as a rule:

> "generally for goals that we want to set, most of our goals should be goals that are 100% within our control. So I don't want to write a book that hits a New York Times bestseller list — although I do — but I don't have that as a goal… because it relies on someone else being the gatekeeper to whether I actually accomplish this thing."

In a Deep Dive snippet with Hasan Kubba (*The Simple Way To Achieve Your Goals*) he describes chapter one of his then-unwritten book **[V]**:

> "i was working on a draft of chapter one this morning which is all about like why you shouldn't really set hard goals and you should actually focus on setting easy goals… screw smart goals, focus on goals that are within your control, and once you've got a goal then think of it purely as a destination and then just completely forget about it"

He also states a **three-step implicit process** in the Oliver Burkeman conversation **[V]**:
1. A **destination goal** that is within your control (e.g. "write a book I'm proud of").
2. Broken into **journey goals** — the system (e.g. "write X hundred words a day").
3. **Lower the bar of quality as much as possible** — his literal to-do item was *"write 500 crappy words for crappy first draft of chapter two"*; he says putting "crappy" in twice makes it feel doable.

Burkeman's counter, which Ali accepts **[V]**: open-ended "do your best / be proud of it" goals are unhelpfully vague; *"quantity-based goals can be really helpful… firstly it's specific, secondly it's within your control, and thirdly it's somewhat drained of… the emotive."*

### 2.4 Phase 3 — NICE goals (Feel-Good Productivity, 2023–24)

**Important discrepancy — do not fudge this.** The acronym has two published expansions:

- **March 2023, his own tweet** (x.com/AliAbdaal/status/1636816553520840707): *"Forget SMART goals. Instead, set NICE goals - **Near-Term Identity-Based Controllable Energising**"* **[V]**
- **The published book (2024)**: **Near-term, Input-based, Controllable, Energising** **[V-2nd, multiple independent summaries agree]**
  - Near-term: *"Focus on the steps you need to take each day or over the next week"*
  - Input-based: *"Focus on the process over the outcome"*
  - Controllable: *"Make sure the achievement of the goal is within your control"*
  - Energising: *"Think about whether you can integrate the energisers (Play, Power and People)"*

So the "I" changed from **Identity-based** to **Input-based** between the tweet and publication. **[V — both readings sourced]** Identity did not disappear; it resurfaced as a separate step in the 2024/25 evidence-based video (§4.5).

Book-level critique of SMART he endorses: SMART goals can produce *"tunnel-vision and loss of intrinsic motivation."* **[V-2nd]** He positions NICE as the day-to-day layer, with SMART goals optionally retained as complementary longer-range targets. **[V-2nd]**

### 2.5 Phase 4 — GPS (2024 → present, current position)

By May 2024 he describes GPS as *"not in the book but I've developed this over the last few months"* **[V]**. By the current flagship video (*Make 2026 the Best Year of Your Life (Evidence-Based)*, YouTube ID `D_KSR3S6W8I`) it is a fully specified 9-field system with a free custom-GPT ("GPS Coach") built on it. **[V]**

Critically, GPS **explicitly refuses the systems-vs-goals dichotomy** he once took a side on **[V]**:

> "you've got the systems camp which was popularized by James Clear… the general vibe that people took away is that you don't need to worry too much about the goals… This is all well and good. However, if your goal is to win the gold medal in the 100 metres Olympics, that is a very different system you would follow compared to if your goal is to win gold medal in swimming… Then on the other side of the camp, you have the people that don't really care about systems… this is sort of like the manifestation law of attraction folks… I think both camps do have some kind of merit. And so basically **the plan is the bridge between the goal and the system.**"

## 3. THE GOAL OBJECT SPEC (current — GPS)

This is the answer to "what fields does a well-formed Abdaal goal have". All nine are explicitly specified by him in `D_KSR3S6W8I`. **[V]**

### G — Goal (3 fields)

| Field | Definition (his words, condensed) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **1. What** | The specific, concrete outcome. *"whenever you can put a number on your goal… that's a hack for getting more specific and quantifiable"* | Failure mode he names: "stop procrastinating", "start a YouTube channel", "financial freedom" — all too vague. *"starting a business that makes you 100 grand a year… is very different than starting a business that you can sell for $300 million."* |
| **2. Why** | Emotionally compelling, ideally **intrinsic** reasons. | Explicit anti-pattern: *"A lot of us choose goals from a sense of should or a sense of obligation."* He cites evidence that extrinsically-motivated goals are both less likely to be hit AND less enjoyable. |
| **3. Anti-goals** | *"What are the things you want to avoid on route to achieving your goal?"* — constraints on the method, not on the outcome. | See §6. |

His own worked example **[V]**: *What* = "reduce my visceral fat levels by 50%". *Why* = "I recently became a father and I would like to not die of a heart attack particularly soon… family history of heart disease." *Anti-goal* = "I don't want the process to eat into the time that I spend with my wife and my daughter — so I don't really want to be going on runs in the evenings, because that's family time."

### P — Plan (3 fields)

| Field | Definition | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **4. 3–5 major moves** | *"What are the three to five major moves that will get me to my goal?"* | Example given: 10k steps/day; 150g protein/day; <2,200 kcal/day; 3× zone-2 cardio/week; 3× weight training with progressive overload. |
| **5. Two realism percentages** | (a) *"Is it realistic **in theory** — assuming we followed the plan to 100% accuracy, would we actually get to the goal?"* (b) *"**In practice**, will I actually follow the plan?"* Both scored as a **%**. | **Hard threshold, stated explicitly: "if any of these numbers is under 80%… that is a sign that I need to rethink my plan."** This is the only numeric gate in his current system. |
| **6. Crystal Ball method** | *"project into the future and you did not achieve your goal… what are the top three reasons why it didn't work?"* — then a mitigation for each. | He attributes this to Gabriele Oettingen's **WOOP** (Wish, Outcome, Obstacle, Plan) / mental contrasting. Time cost he quotes: ~1–3 minutes per goal. |

### S — System (3 fields)

| Field | Definition | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **7. Tracking** | *"how can I track my progress towards it?"* | Evidence he cites: meta-analysis of 138 studies / ~20,000 participants on progress monitoring. |
| **8. Reminders** | *"how will you remind yourself of your goal and of your plan?"* | Named mechanisms: daily journaling, weekly review, **recurring calendar blocks** ("my favourite thing"), habit-tracker app notifications, a **vision board as desktop wallpaper**. |
| **9. Accountability** | An accountability buddy, squad, coach, or paid program. | Historic example: a £100 forfeit pact with his brother for a daily 15-minute practice. **[V]** |

He also gives GPS a **diagnostic** use, not just a generative one **[V]**: *"if we are struggling with something we can just run it through the GPS system and then we'll see exactly why we are struggling"* — i.e. the failure is localisable to Goal (don't know what you want / it's unrealistic), Plan (strategy is ineffective), or System (you know what to do and aren't doing it).

### 3.1 Superseded but still-referenced format: the Anti-Wasteman System (2020–22)

Eight fields, per goal, in a Notion table **[V]**:

1. **Dream** — deliberately emotionally-resonant and vague (*"a dream is emotionally resonant"*; he notes August Bradley calls this a "value goal"). e.g. "learn to draw like a concept artist."
2. **Why** — the compelling reason.
3. **SMART goal** — the specific, checkable version. e.g. "by end of 2021 I'll have illustrated a scene from Wheel of Time / Mistborn / Stormlight Archive and uploaded a speed painting."
4. **Habit** — *"what habit do I want to do that is going to make this more likely to happen?"* e.g. 9am–12pm blocked for writing, Tue–Sun.
5. **"How surprised would I be if I failed?" — 1 to 10.** (1 = not surprised at all, 10 = very surprised.) This is his confidence/realism probe, the ancestor of the GPS 80% gate. He rated the concept-art goal a **2/10** and the book goal a **7/10**. **[V]**
6. **Top 3 reasons I might fail.**
7. **Who can help me?** (explicitly citing *Who Not How*, Hardy & Sullivan) **+ How can I stack the deck in my favour?**
8. **What action can I take RIGHT NOW?** — he insists on doing it during the planning session. (He signed up for an art course mid-annual-review and had a mentor within days.)

The public "9-Step Goal Setting System" LinkedIn post (2022) is this same list, split slightly differently, with "make it SMART" and "add one habit at a time" as separate steps. **[V — post text confirmed via two independent sources]**

### 3.2 Sentence-level formatting rules he applies

- **Append "…while enjoying every step of the journey" to every goal.** Credited to Dicky Bush. His stated 2024 goals literally read: *"To get into the best shape of my life while enjoying every step of the journey to getting there"*; *"To create and run a productivity weekend seminar while enjoying every step of the journey along the way"*; *"To host 6 mini-adventures for friends and family while enjoying every step of the journey."* **[V-2nd, Penguin]**
- **Tie the goal to an identity**, spending "an extra 20 seconds": not "improve my bench press" but *"I am a healthy person"*; not "make videos" but *"I am a YouTuber"*. **[V]** He quotes Tony Robbins: *"the strongest force in human personality is the need to stay consistent in how we define ourselves."* **[V]**
- **Prefer goals 100% within your control**, converting outcome goals into input goals. **[V]**
- **Prefer a project over a skill.** *"it's a lot easier to work on a goal when that goal is a side effect of something else that you want to do."* He rewrote "learn to cook" → **"host regular dinner parties"**; "take stretching seriously" → **"learn acro yoga"**; and notes he only ever learned to code/piano/guitar as a by-product of a project. **[V]** This is one of his most distinctive and least-copied ideas.
- **Lower the quality bar in the task wording** ("write 500 crappy words"). **[V]**

## 4. Horizon nesting

He uses **six** horizons in practice. Note that **the 5-year and 10-year layers are workshop/annual-review artefacts, not part of his operating loop** — his live loop is quarter → week → day.

```
LIFETIME / VALUES   "North Star"          annual workshop only
      ↓
10-YEAR DREAM LIST  ~10-min brain-dump    annual workshop only
      ↓
5-YEAR VISION       "ideal ordinary week" annual review (he admits he's bad at this)
      ↓
ANNUAL              theme + 12-Month Celebration + ~3 goals
      ↓
QUARTER (90 days)   ★ QUARTERLY QUESTS — 3–4 max. THE OPERATING UNIT.
      ↓
WEEK                weekly reset → top 3 priorities
      ↓
DAY                 morning manifesto → "today's adventure" + focus log
```

**Lifetime / values — "North Star."** Workshop step 2. Prompts include: design your ideal Tuesday hour-by-hour; *"What would you choose to do if you knew you couldn't fail?"*; *"What would you still do if you knew you would fail?"*; a future-TED-talk visualisation 20 years out; write your own obituary. **[V-2nd, workshop write-up + his own TikTok caption naming "align with your North Star"]**

**10-year.** A Jim Rohn / Zig Ziglar–derived exercise: set a ~10-minute timer and dump everything you might want over the next 10 years across relationships / career / personal development, prompted by *"What do I want to learn / have / be / do?"* Then **step 4: put a number 1/3/5/10 next to each item** to assign it a horizon. **[V-2nd, workshop write-up]** He separately endorses a Ray Dalio + Tony Robbins version: brainstorm audaciously with the "is this realistic?" filter switched *off*, then apply realism later. **[V]**

**Annual.** Three components: (a) an **annual theme**; (b) the **12-Month Celebration** (see §5); (c) roughly **3 goals** *"that you absolutely want to achieve this year"*, ideally one each for career, relationships, personal development, each stress-tested with **"ask yourself why 5 times."** **[V-2nd, workshop; the 5-whys technique is separately [V] from his Ray Dalio video]**

**Quarter — the core unit.** *"instead of thinking about setting goals for the year you actually focus on setting goals for the next quarter, i.e. the next 90 days"* **[V]**. Structure:
- **3–4 quests maximum.** *"if I set any more than four I don't end up making progress on any of them"* **[V]**
- Split into **two categories: Work and Life.** **[V]**
- Within each category: **1 Main Quest (must-achieve) + 1–3 Side Quests (bonus).** **[V — his own TikTok caption]**
- Long projects become **90-day chunks** (book writing → "complete the first draft of the first three chapters"). **[V]**
- Rationale: (i) 90 days fits on one sheet of paper; (ii) Parkinson's law / the **deadline effect** — annual goals get procrastinated to November; (iii) **periodization** — do things in series, not parallel (he traces this to 1970s Eastern-bloc Olympic training via *The 12 Week Year*). **[V]**
- Attribution: *The 12 Week Year* (Moran & Lennington), *Traction* (Wickman), *Get Scalable* (Deiss). He is candid that *"basically the whole book is about quarterly planning; it's calling it 12 weeks because it just sounds cooler."* **[V]**

**Week — the "weekly reset."** 10–20 minutes. Explicitly from David Allen's GTD. Contents: review last week's calendar, **celebrate wins**, then the pinned prompt **"what were my quarterly quests and how are they going?"**, then set **top 1–3 priorities for the week**. **[V]**

**Day — the "morning manifesto."** ~2 minutes, before work. Two prompts: **"What were *our* weekly priorities and how are they going?"** (he uses "our" deliberately — *"it feels like I'm harnessing all parts of my brain"*) and **"What is today's adventure?"** (deliberately not "most important task" — *"by phrasing it as an adventure it feels good"*). **[V]**

The nesting is therefore **a reminder chain, not a decomposition tree**: each layer's job is to re-surface the layer above it. *"every single week I'm reviewing my quarterly goals and every single day I'm reviewing my weekly priorities."* **[V]**

**Life-inventory layer that sits across horizons — the Wheel of Life.** Nine segments in three thirds **[V]**:
- Health → **body, mind, soul**
- Relationships → **romance, family, friends**
- Work → **mission, money, growth**
- Plus a tenth off-wheel category: **joy**

Rated 0–10 — and he insists the rating is **alignment, not satisfaction**: *"my actions in the here and now are consistent with where I think I would like to be… you might be a 3 out of 10 in terms of satisfaction with money because you're not yet a millionaire… but you might be a 10 out of 10 in alignment because you are taking the actions necessary."* **[V]** He then circles **one** area per third to work on. He notes the gap between "where I am" and "where I want to be" *is* the goal: *"whenever you have a sense of where you are now and where you want to be, that is what creates what we call goals."* **[V]**

(An earlier, simpler split he used was **Life / Medicine / Business**, with Life → health, relationships, happiness. **[V, 2020]**)

## 5. How a goal gets *chosen* — the 12-Month Celebration

His stated *"preferred method for goal setting"* as of Mar 2024. **[V]**

Procedure **[V]**:
1. For each salient life area, ask: **"What would I like to be celebrating 12 months from now?"** — imagined concretely as a dinner with a specific friend who is holding you accountable.
2. Generate **multiple candidate celebrations** per area (he lists five for physical health: packed on muscle / got lean / worked out 3× a week for 12 months / became more flexible / played sport weekly).
3. **Collapse to one.** *"if I had to boil it down to just one thing and I could only celebrate one thing, what would that one thing be? Because generally having a single goal to work towards helps the brain focus."* Everything else becomes *"the cherry on top."*
4. Append **"while enjoying every step along the way."**
5. **Write it down** in a durable, bookmarked place.
6. **Add the why** to the same document.

This is his cleanest goal-selection mechanism: it produces a *felt* outcome first, then reduces to one, rather than starting from a target metric.

**Selection questions for quarterly quests** (his own caption, verbatim) **[V]**:
> "👉 For Work: *If everything else remains the same, what one area, if improved, would have the biggest impact on my work?*
> 👉 For Life: *If everything else stays the same, what one area of my life, if I made progress, would have the biggest impact?*"

## 6. Anti-goals

**Yes, he uses them, and they are a first-class field in his current system (GPS field 3).** **[V]**

**Definition as he uses it:** *"What are the things you want to avoid on route to achieving your goal?"* — constraints on the **method**, not negations of the outcome. **[V]**

His worked examples **[V]**:
- Goal "build a £100k/yr lifestyle business in 12 months" → anti-goals: *"I don't want to have to work on the weekends. I don't want to have to work more than 40 hours a week."*
- Goal "reduce visceral fat 50%" → anti-goal: *"I don't want the process to eat into the time that I spend with my wife and my daughter — so I don't really want to be going on runs in the evenings."*

His rationale, verbatim **[V]**: *"these are all good constraints that you can set on yourself, because it's not just about achieving your goals. It's about achieving our goals **without torpedoing the other aspects of our life**."*

**Provenance is confirmed: Andrew Wilkinson**, via Deep Dive episode *"The Pursuit of Wealth: Barista to Billionaire"* (YouTube `VPJYBnmxNMI`, 2024). Ali raises it: *"one of the things I think you mentioned in the book is this idea of anti-goals… is that where this idea of anti-goals comes in, like figuring out what are the things you want to avoid?"* **[V]**

Wilkinson's own framing, verbatim from that episode **[V]** — this is the canonical mechanism:

> "Charlie Munger… has this great saying: **show me where I'm going to die so I don't go there**… you want to instruct yourself of all the bad things that can happen and then you can avoid it and you can **invert**… it's hard for us to know what will make us happy… but what I do know for a fact — everyone fundamentally knows what makes them unhappy… about five or six years ago Chris and I sat down and we made a list of what are all of our fears, what are all the things we want to avoid — **if we were to design a most miserable life for us, what would that be, and then how do we avoid it?**"

Wilkinson also gives the transferable version: when hiring a CEO, *"what are your fears? How does this go wrong? Let's list them all out… and then we'll mitigate each of those."*

**Note a real distinction:** Wilkinson's original anti-goals are *life-design* level ("never schedule an in-person meeting that could be an email"). Ali has **narrowed them into per-goal guardrails**. Both are in play in his content, but the GPS field is the narrower one. **[I]**

## 8. Prioritisation and limits

He is unusually explicit about **caps**, and the cap is the thing he says he gets wrong most often.

| Layer | Stated cap | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Quarterly quests, total | **3–4.** *"if I set any more than four I don't end up making progress on any of them"* | **[V]** |
| Per area, per quarter | **1 Main Quest + 1–3 Side Quests.** *"if I set more than three goals, I often struggle to achieve any of them"* | **[V]**, his caption |
| Annual goals | **~3**, ideally one each: career / relationships / personal development | **[V-2nd]** |
| Per life-area at annual planning | **1**, collapsed from many candidates | **[V]** |
| Weekly | **top 3 priorities** | **[V]** |
| Daily | **1** — "today's adventure" | **[V]** |

**His own diagnosis of the failure mode**, from the newsletter *"My biggest mistake with goal-setting"* **[V-2nd, search excerpt]**: setting too many goals at once — *"time, energy, focus, attention and motivation are all finite resources, and trying to do lots of things in parallel is often a recipe for burnout and overwhelm."* And on the ambitious specifically: they *"burn themselves into the ground through ambition, lack of balance, and a failure to show restraint when setting goals."*

**Ranking / scoring methods he actually uses:**
1. **Wheel of Life 0–10 alignment ratings** → circle the lowest/most salient in each third. **[V]**
2. **The "biggest impact if improved" question** for work and life (see §5). **[V]**
3. **"How surprised would I be if I failed?" 1–10** — a self-honesty probe, not a ranking. **[V]**
4. **The two GPS realism percentages with an 80% floor** — this is a *gate*, not a ranking: below 80%, change the plan (or the goal). **[V]**
5. **The Ideal Week calendar as a capacity constraint.** This is his most concrete prioritisation device: build a blank week, block the non-negotiables, then try to physically place a recurring block for each goal. *"if you realize that you don't actually have time for a particular thing… that means you can change your goal… Maybe it's not a goal you want to work on for this year."* **[V]** i.e. **the calendar, not the wishlist, decides the goal count.**
6. **Strategic abandonment** (Oliver Burkeman, *Four Thousand Weeks*): *"we have to strategically decide what are the things that we want to fail at. And I strategically decided last year to fail at the art lessons, to fail at the busking thing, to fail at the six-pack abs thing."* **[V]**

## 9. Tracking and review

### 9.1 Where the goals live

- **A Google Doc he calls "Goalkeeper."** *"I personally have a Google doc that I've named goalkeeper and I've been doing this for the last couple of years."* This is the current home of his quarterly quests. **[V]**
- **A separate Google Doc shared with his CEO coach (Eric)**, reviewed **every quarter**, containing goals per life area + the why. **[V]**
- **Notion** historically — the Anti-Wasteman template and annual review template are public Notion templates. **[V]**
- **Things 3** for projects, with **🟢/🟡/🔴 emoji status** on each project, updated ~weekly. **[V]**
- **A vision board as desktop wallpaper.** **[V]**

### 9.2 What he measures

- **Traffic-light status per quest:** *"generally I give it red, yellow or green, or I just say kind of on track or off track."* Semantics are precise: **green = on track; yellow = off track but there's a plan; red = off track without a plan.** **[V]**
- **The Focus Log** — his self-declared "single biggest hack": a Google Sheet logging **minutes of fully-focused time on the priority project, per day**. Origin: his CEO coach, during the book's first draft. *"just the fact that I was now aware of what that number was meant that number went up."* **[V]**
- **Leading-indicator counts** where available: words written per day (book), syllabus topics RAG-rated (Cambridge exams), workouts logged, MyFitnessPal calories/protein, step count, Whoop sleep score. **[V]**
- For students in his paid program: a weekly **Google Sheet scorecard** — hours worked, content posted, discovery calls taken. **[V]**

### 9.3 Review cadence

| Cadence | Ritual | Duration | Key prompt |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daily | Morning Manifesto | ~2 min | "What were our weekly priorities and how are they going?" / "What is today's adventure?" |
| Weekly | Weekly Reset (GTD weekly review) | 10–20 min | "What were my quarterly quests and how are they going?" + celebrate wins + set top 3 |
| Quarterly | Quarterly Alignment Workshop (he runs a **free public Zoom** version each quarter, ~thousands attend) + coach review | ~90 min | reflect on the quarter, recalibrate, set next quests |
| Annually | Annual Review → Reflect / North Star / Plan | several hours | see below |

**[V for daily/weekly/quarterly; V + V-2nd for annual]**

### 9.4 The Annual Review (three steps, stable since 2019)

**Step 1 — Reflect.** Gratitude split five ways (**people / experiences / accomplishments / things / game-changers**); a **month-by-month calendar walkthrough** to rebuild the year's timeline; "cool stuff I discovered" (articles, podcasts, books, YouTube channels — harvested from his own newsletter archive); reflective questions (*"If you had to teach one thing you learnt this year that would improve one's quality of life, what would that be?"*, *"What kept you up at night with excitement this year?"*, *"How did you have fun differently?"*). **[V]** He states a secondary purpose bluntly: *"when you do an annual review time just goes slower."*

**Step 2 — Plan / North Star.** Visualisation exercises: **ideal ordinary week 5 years out**, then **ideal ordinary week 1 year out**. He admits *"I'm not very good at doing these visualization exercises."* His coach's warning, which he flags as the key trap: *"a lot of us have this idea of 'I want to do the same but more of it'… that's a very easy trap to fall into… the most important thing to come out of this is actually **what are you not doing** — what is no longer a priority, and why are you still doing that thing right now?"* **[V]** Then the **bucket list** — rewritten from scratch each year, then diffed against last year's (*"if I forgot to write it down, it's usually because that thing is no longer necessarily a priority"*), then split into "this year" vs "someday/maybe". **[V]**

**Step 3 — Execute.** The Anti-Wasteman table, ending in **"take action right now"** during the session itself. **[V]**

Current-era annual review, per his own 2025 caption **[V-2nd]**: *"1️⃣ Reflect… 2️⃣ Align with your North Star… 3️⃣ Make a plan — choose an **annual theme** and develop **quarterly quests**."* — same three-step skeleton, updated vocabulary.

## 10. Failure handling

This is one of the better-documented parts of his method, because he publishes his own misses.

**1. Expected hit-rate is low, and he says so publicly.** Newsletter *"We failed 78% of our goals"* (Jun 2025): his team hit **4 of 18 quests**. He cites *Traction* — companies doing structured goal-setting for the first time typically hit ~30%. His stated root cause: *"We all just set too many goals."* **[V-2nd, search excerpt]**

**2. Personal misses are reported in detail.** In *My New Life Goals (2022)* he walks through six 2021 goals and marks four as failed: bestselling book (missed), six-pack abs (abandoned — *"I don't really care massively about the goal"*), busking (abandoned), concept art (abandoned after 3–4 months — *"I was waking up in the morning and I was like, ah crap, I have an art lesson now"*). **[V]**

**3. Abandonment is a legitimate, named outcome, not a failure.** *"it's actually completely okay to change your mind… the more we try something the more data points we get about the thing, and so there's very little point in clinging to this vision that we vaguely decided a year ago just because we decided it a year ago."* He pairs it with the (probably apocryphal, he says so) Eisenhower/Einstein line: *"plans are totally pointless but planning is indispensable."* **[V]**

**4. Diagnosis before retry.** Failure is routed through GPS: is it a Goal problem, a Plan problem, or a System problem? **[V]**

**5. Never measure against the ideal.** *"Measure the gain, not the gap"* (Dan Sullivan) — measure against your former self. **[V]**

**6. Reframe as data.** He argues from neuroscience that **learning only occurs on prediction error**, therefore *"learning almost by definition happens when we make mistakes."* Same event, two available interpretations: *"I'm a failure"* vs *"through the process I've learned a bunch of things."* **[V]**

**7. Streak-breaking is defused deliberately.** Via Burkeman quoting Dan Harris: aim to do things **"daily-ish"** — *"it reduces this kind of 'oh, if I break my streak it's all over and I might as well spend the next three weeks not doing anything.'"* Ali endorses this. **[V]**

**8. Missed weekly resets are explicitly normalised.** *"sometimes a couple of weeks pass where I haven't looked at my list of goals… damn, I've actually been less consistent with the goals that I set because I simply forgot to look at them."* The recovery is just the next morning-manifesto prompt catching it. **[V]**

## 11. Critiques and prescribed fixes

| What he says goes wrong | His prescribed fix |
|---|---|
| **Not setting goals at all** ("mistake number one") | Set them; goals are the compass/direction. **[V]** |
| **Setting them but not writing them down** ("mistake number two") | Write them down. Cites the Dominican University study: **~42% more likely to achieve**. (He treats this figure as robust; it is a small, often-overstated study — flagging as a weak evidential link, not a fabrication on his part.) **[V]** |
| **Setting them in January and never looking again** ("mistake number three") | Weekly reset + morning manifesto; harness the **reticular activating system** (his Fiat 500 example). **[V]** |
| **Too many goals** — his self-declared biggest mistake | Hard caps: 3–4 quarterly quests; 1 main quest per area; collapse candidates to one; use the Ideal Week as capacity constraint; strategically choose what to fail at. **[V]** |
| **SMART goals** — redundant letters ("achievable"/"realistic", "specific"/"time-bound"); tunnel vision; loss of intrinsic motivation; obsession with outcomes | **NICE goals** for the day-to-day; SMART optionally retained as a long-range complement. **[V]/[V-2nd]** |
| **Outcome goals you don't control** (subscriber counts, bestseller lists, revenue targets) | Convert to input/controllable goals ("write a book I'm proud of"; "release 1–3 videos a week"). He says this even of his own business goals: *"I don't like having those goals personally, and then we break those down as a business into goals that are within our control."* **[V]** |
| **New Year's resolutions specifically** — over-weighted culturally; enthusiasm decays by late January | Two fixes: (a) **start a week early — December 23rd** — *"it feels like less of a big deal and more like a simple change you could really have made whenever you wanted"* **[V-2nd]**; (b) replace the annual frame with quarterly quests entirely. **[V]** |
| **Annual timeframes** — Parkinson's law; you procrastinate until November; circumstances change | **90-day periodization**; things in series, not parallel; exploit the deadline effect. **[V]** |
| **Vague goals** ("stop procrastinating", "start a YouTube channel", "financial freedom") | Get concrete; attach a number. **[V]** |
| **"Should" goals / extrinsic motivation** | Interrogate the why; 5 whys; require emotional resonance. *"a should is based on fear and based on avoidance of negativity and based on duty, and these things tend not to motivate us."* **[V]** |
| **Plans that can't work even if executed** | The two-percentage realism check with the **80% floor**. *"I know a lot of people who are choosing to start their first businesses because they want to get to $10,000 a month in passive income… but then the plan that they are following is just very unlikely to actually get them there."* **[V]** |
| **Over-researching instead of starting** | **Lean Learning** (Pat Flynn): identify the next step → gather the **minimum viable information** → take action → repeat. *"the goal is minimum viable information, not maximum possible information."* **[V]** |
| **Pure positive visualisation** | Mental contrasting / WOOP / the Crystal Ball. He notes people who over-visualise success are in some studies **less** likely to achieve. **[V]** |
| **Grinding / discipline as the mechanism** | *"What would this look like if it were fun?"* + append "while enjoying every step of the journey" + swap "run every morning" for "do fun cardio workouts" + call them **quests** and **adventures** rather than OKRs. **[V]/[V-2nd]** |
| **Goal-efficiency instead of goal-selection** | *"the needle is moved in the choice of what you are actually working on… rather than in how fast you're getting there."* **[V]** |
| **"Same but more" annual planning** | Ask what you will **stop** doing. **[V]** |

## 12. The "experiment" framing — precise status

**Do not overstate this one.** In the 18 goal-related transcripts I pulled, he does **not** use a "treat this goal as a 4-week experiment" formulation. The experiment framing is a **book-level** device, not part of his named goal systems.

What is supported:
- *Feel-Good Productivity* asks the reader to **"think like a productivity scientist"** and is structured around **experiments** the reader runs; *"not all of them will work for everyone. But even when an experiment doesn't work, it can give you useful feedback."* **[V-2nd, multiple independent summaries]**
- Failure reframed as data collection under an experimental mindset — *"it's not a failure, it's an experiment that tells you to try something new."* **[V-2nd]**
- **In his own transcript words, the nearest equivalents are** (a) the mistakes-as-prediction-error/learning argument (§10.6) **[V]**, (b) *"the more we try something the more data points we get about the thing"* **[V]**, and (c) *"they've always been with this second method"* trial-and-error reasoning about which goal system works for him **[V]**.
- Third-party writeups state *"what works for one person might not work for another, and what worked for you last year might not work this year"* as his position. **[V-2nd]**

**Verdict: the experiment framing is real but belongs to his *productivity-technique* layer, not his *goal-object* layer.** A "4-week experiment" goal type would be an extension of his thinking, not a quotation of it. **[I]**
