## 1. Overview

### 1.1 The one-sentence summary

Abdaal does **not** have a proprietary vision/values framework. What he has is a **curated, heavily-attributed prompt library** — ~20 journaling prompts borrowed from Designing Your Life, Tim Ferriss, 80,000 Hours, Clayton Christensen, Bronnie Ware, Brian Tracy, Grace Lordan, Luke Burgess/René Girard, Cal Newport, Joe Hudson and Anne-Laure Le Cunff — plus his own packaging language: **"North Star"**, **"Ideal Ordinary Week"**, **"the Wheel of Life"** (his 9+1 category version), **"the fast-forward test"**, and the sequencing **where are you going → where are you now → how do you get there**.

### 1.2 His core mental model: wayfinding, not destination-setting

**[VERIFIED]** From *Why You Feel Lost in Life* (19 Jan 2024, `PtMLCL_T2ak`, auto-captions):

> "if you imagine you are lost in a forest you'd be lost if you don't know where you're trying to go that's reason number one you'd be lost if you don't know where you are that's reason number two and even if you did know where you are and where you're trying to go you would still feel lost if you didn't know the path to getting there"

> "the point of all of these exercises is really an idea called wayfinding which is from a book called designing your life by Bill Burnett and Dave Evans... wayfinding is the process of figuring out where to go even when you don't have a clear destination in mind"

> "the point isn't that you need to have your entire life mapped out none of us have our entire lives mapped out"

> "this is just a rough first draft it's just a version 0.1 we don't have to be wedded to any of these answers"

**[VERIFIED]** He is explicit that the output is *direction*, not a plan. From the annual-review newsletter ([My Annual Reflection & Goal-Setting Method](https://aliabdaal.com/newsletter/my-annual-reflection-goal-setting-method/), 24 Dec 2023, exact text):

> "I like to think of life as a journey. You can choose to go anywhere, but it's useful to identify where your personal *North Star* is so that you know roughly what direction you want to travel."
> "the important thing about taking a step back to reflect about this stuff and identifying your *North Star* is that it gives you a *direction* to aim towards. You don't want to be overly attached to any of your answers, or cling too strongly to this version of your future. But by thinking about where your *North Star* is, you'll be able to look at your life right now and ask: 'To what extent is what I'm currently doing aligned with this future that I think I want?'"

### 1.3 His anti-overthinking instruction (matters for product design)

**[VERIFIED]** Same newsletter, exact text:

> "you already know the answers to all these questions, somewhere inside of you. If you *think* too hard about them, you'll work yourself into knots. But if you *don't think too hard* about them, and go with your 'gut' or your 'body' or your 'soul' or whatever non-mind entity you'd like, you'll find the answers will come to you a lot more readily."

**[VERIFIED]** And explicitly gives permission to do a subset (`PtMLCL_T2ak`):

> "you don't need to answer all 18 of these prompts that we're going to talk about... even if you just answer one or two or three just like the ones that really internally resonate with you... I guarantee it will give you more clarity"

**[VERIFIED]** Writing it down is non-negotiable for him (`PtMLCL_T2ak`):

> "you cannot solve a complex mathematical equation without writing it down you cannot build a bridge or a building without writing it down this is us trying to figure out where you want to go with your life this is a big ass question... I would even recommend handwriting it if you can that would be gold standard"

### 1.4 The three-part structure he actually teaches

### 2.1 The Odyssey Plan ⭐ his single most-cited exercise

**[VERIFIED]** He calls this the journalling prompt that "most single-handedly changed my life" and credits it with him leaving medicine.

**Verbatim prompts** (exact newsletter text, [How I Design My Life](https://aliabdaal.com/newsletter/how-i-design-my-life/) and [The Odyssey Plan Revisited](https://aliabdaal.com/newsletter/the-odyssey-plan-revisited/)):

> 1. Write out, in detail, what your life would look like 5 years from now if you continued down your current path.
> 2. Write out, in detail, what your life would look like 5 years from now if you took a completely different path.
> 3. Write out, in detail, what your life would look like 5 years from now if money, social obligations, and what people would think, were irrelevant.

*Note the wording drift.* His **first** publication of it ([What should I do with my life?](https://aliabdaal.com/newsletter/what-should-i-do-with-my-life/), Dec 2019, exact text) had prompt 3 as:

> 3. Write out, in detail, what your life would look like 5 years from now if money and social obligations were irrelevant.

The "**and what people would think**" clause was added by the 2022/2023 retellings.

**His own naming of the three paths** (`MoGTXsusX6Q`, auto-captions): "**your current path**", "**the alternative path**", "**the radical path**".

- **Time:** he gives conflicting figures, all small. "spending half an hour to write out my answers to each of these" (`FQJ3MsQFKNg`, 26 May 2021). "after writing for an hour, I got the main gist of my 3 life paths" (How I Design My Life newsletter, exact). LinkedIn markets it as "This 30-minute Stanford exercise".
- **Inputs:** blank page. Nothing else.
- **Outputs:** three written 5-year narratives. Then a **felt-sense read** of each — the diagnostic is emotional, not analytical.
- **Cadence:** **[VERIFIED]** "every year when I do my annual review I will just redo my Odyssey plan just to make sure that I'm thinking big and I'm not allowing the inertia of my existing path to keep me on the road" (`MoGTXsusX6Q`).
- **Origin:** *Designing Your Life* by **Bill Burnett & Dave Evans** (Stanford Life Design Lab). He is scrupulous about crediting it — "It's an exercise taught by professors at the Stanford Life Design Lab (which is apparently a thing)" (exact). **His discovery route:** a YouTube video by **Unjaded Jade** — "I came across *The Odyssey Plan* in [this video by Unjaded Jade](https://youtu.be/DM2WjzaErUs)" (exact, Dec 2019). He has also mis-remembered it as "a life-design exercise from **Stanford Business School**" (exact, How I Design My Life) — that's wrong; it's the d.school / Life Design Lab.
- **Sequence position:** first / entry-point exercise for "where am I going".

**How he reads the output — the actual decision rule.** **[VERIFIED]** exact newsletter text (Dec 2019):

> "When I was writing this out, I honestly felt a bit depressed. I had a feeling of 'is this really it?'... Don't get me wrong – this future-vision is a pretty idyllic life. But there's nothing particularly… cool about it."
> "try out the Odyssey Plan for yourself. Map out where your current path will lead in 5 years time. How do you feel about that? Is it really where you want to be? **Does the process of getting there excite you?** If so, great – keep doing what you're doing. But if not, then maybe think about why you're following the Yellow Brick Road if you don't like the idea of getting to Oz."

He then anchors that decision rule to **Tim Ferriss, *The 4-Hour Work Week***, quoted exactly in the same issue:

> "Excitement is the more practical synonym for happiness, and it is precisely what you should strive to chase. It is the cure-all. When people suggest you follow your 'passion' or your 'bliss,' I propose that they are, in fact, referring to the same singular concept: excitement. This brings us full circle. The question you should be asking isn't, 'What do I want?' or 'What are my goals?' but 'What would excite me?'"

**A failure mode he names on himself** — worth capturing, because it's a real UX problem for any product built on this. **[VERIFIED]** exact:

> "I wonder if my imagination is pretty limited. Obviously, the first path is a continuation of my current life... But the second, which was supposed to be completely different, ended up going down the 'creating content on the internet' route as well. And the third also tended towards 'I've got a substack where I write about philosophy'... Either this is a severe lack of imagination, or I just love the idea of sharing stuff online"

He re-frames this positively elsewhere: **[VERIFIED]** "sometimes you might even realize that like oh I just I've got a bit of a failure of imagination" (`MoGTXsusX6Q`) — i.e. the collapse of all three paths into one is *itself* signal.

### 2.2 Deathbed / gravestone / funeral / obituary family

He runs **five distinct variants**. They are not interchangeable — he explicitly uses different audiences to surface different value dimensions.

#### (a) The Gravestone Technique
**[VERIFIED]** *How To Figure Out What You Want To Do With Your Life* (26 May 2021, `FQJ3MsQFKNg`, auto-captions).

> **Prompt:** "what do we want written on our gravestone"

- His own answer, stated repeatedly across years: **"good dad", "good husband", "inspirational teacher"** (2021 version) / **"good father, good husband, good teacher"** (*Why I Left Medicine... Forever*, 30 Nov 2021, `mZOVLrLXKCE`).
- **Output → decision:** "I care more about being a decent teacher than I do about saving lives as a doctor" — this is the sentence that ends his medical career.
- **Origin:** he names **Clayton Christensen's** HBR article / book *How Will You Measure Your Life?* as what "turned me onto this mode of long-term thinking". The gravestone framing itself he does not attribute (it's folk-canonical — Covey's "Habit 2: Begin With The End In Mind" funeral visualisation is the usual ancestor). **[INFERRED]** on the Covey lineage — he never says Covey. He *does* use the phrase "begin with the end in mind" as a section title in `PtMLCL_T2ak`.
- **Time:** not stated. Minutes.

#### (b) The Funeral Speakers prompt (the one he uses most)
**[VERIFIED]** `MoGTXsusX6Q` (2 Apr 2024) and `PtMLCL_T2ak`.

> **Prompt:** "what would you like people to say at your funeral" — "and here you can think of **family**, you can think of **friends**, you can think of **co-workers**, and you can think of **people whose life has been impacted by your work**"

The four-audience split is the whole point:

> "generally family and friends and co-workers even are going to comment on like the personality traits and like how much of a nice person you were... but someone whose life has been impacted by your work is going to comment on... the impact that your work had on their life"

**Mandatory follow-up** (this is the step that makes it actionable):
> "then I will ask myself the follow-up question **to what extent am I actually living in alignment with that**... and usually when I ask that question I realize ooh actually I have been overemphasizing work at the expense of relationships"

- **Output:** a North Star statement. His: **[VERIFIED]** "the North Star is if you visit me at my funeral… I would love for you to say that something about my content… helped change the trajectory of your life."
- **Time:** not stated; used as a quick recurring gut-check, not a set-piece.

#### (c) The Wikipedia Page prompt
**[VERIFIED]** `FQJ3MsQFKNg` and exact newsletter text ([The Power of Questions](https://aliabdaal.com/newsletter/the-power-of-questions/)):

> "'What would I want written in the achievements section of my Wikipedia page?'"

Purpose: deliberately separates **achievement/impact** from **character**. "the Wikipedia page question is more about like achievements and accolades and what's the impact you want to have on the world overall rather than just kind of the impact you want to have on your close friends and family members" (`FQJ3MsQFKNg`).

#### (d) Write Your Own Obituary (his workshop set-piece)
**[VERIFIED]** Live-coached on camera in `PtMLCL_T2ak`; also confirmed by a third-party workshop attendee.

**Protocol as actually run:**
1. Verbally brainstorm bullet points in two buckets — **relationships**, then **work/achievements**. ("Tintin let's start with relationships so what sort of stuff… you'd like people to say about you when you're dead and I'll just write it down"; then "what about in the work front so work slash achievements").
2. Don't write prose. "we're not going to bother writing this out in full because what we're actually going to do is chuck it into ChatGPT".
3. Feed bullets to an LLM → it writes the eulogy.
4. Read it back. The emotional response is the data.

**[VERIFIED]** Third-party confirmation from a paying workshop attendee (*Productive Happiness* Substack, [5 steps to plan your best year ever](https://productivehappiness.substack.com/p/5-steps-to-plan-your-best-year-ever), 10 Jan 2024):
> "**Write your obituary (using Chat-GPT!)** … we have been invited to **imagine what we'd hear if we were a ghost attending our own funeral**. We wrote bullet points of what we'd like our loved ones to say about us, and fed them to chat GPT who wrote us a most moving eulogy."

- **Cadence:** "I've done this exercise many times in the last few years" (`PtMLCL_T2ak`). He publishes his own obituary in the video description.
- **Sequence:** he files it under **"Method 5 — begin with the end in mind"**, i.e. last of the five direction-finding methods.

#### (e) The Obituary Method — productised 5-question version
**[VERIFIED]** *I Tried AI as a Life Coach for 365 Days* (31 May 2025, `QF2wIywvDhk`). Built into VoicePal, his own app. **Exact 5 questions as read out:**

> 1. What would I want people to say was my greatest contribution or impact on other people's lives?
> 2. What qualities or characteristics would I hope people would remember most about me?
> 3. What relationships would I want to be highlighted as central to who I was?
> 4. What accomplishments or milestones would reflect a life well-lived according to my values?
> 5. What regrets would I least want to have when looking back on my life? And what changes could I make now to prevent them?

- **Method:** speak the answers aloud (AirPods, on a walk), up to 90 minutes of recording, get a cleaned transcript, then have an LLM draft the obituary.
- **Stated purpose:** "a good way of clarifying **what you care about, what your own values are**, and it's one way of answering the question what the hell should I do with my life."

#### (f) Related deathbed-adjacent prompts

**[VERIFIED]** "**If I knew I was going to die 2 years from now, how would I spend my time?**" — prompt #10 in `MoGTXsusX6Q`. He deliberately uses **2 years, not tomorrow**: "generally if you knew you were going to die tomorrow you'd probably do very destructive things". He varies the horizon (2 / 5 / 10 years) because "the time horizon does sort of dictate what projects you want to work on" — a 2-year horizon kills book-writing; a 10-year horizon permits two books. Follow-up: "**why am I not just doing those things now?**"

**[VERIFIED]** "**when I'm on my deathbed what will I regret having done or not having done**" — #7 of 10 in *How to Make Difficult Decisions* (30 Nov 2022, `XILu7hxDr2w`).

**[VERIFIED]** He leans on **Bronnie Ware's *The Top Five Regrets of the Dying*** as a standing checklist, and recites it (his gloss in brackets is his own addition):
> 1. I wish I'd had the courage to live a life true to myself, not the life others expected of me
> 2. I wish I hadn't worked so hard **[on stuff I didn't care about]**
> 3. I wish I'd had the courage to express my feelings
> 4. I wish I'd stayed in touch with my friends
> 5. I wish I'd let myself be happier

> "at one point I even had this list of like things that literally the top of my kind of daily notes page when I was doing journaling" (`XILu7hxDr2w`)

The whole of *How to Guarantee You'll Regret Your Life* (5 Jul 2024, `kxBs0Vr1aJ0`) is an inversion sketch built on Ware's list.

**[VERIFIED]** **The Solomon conversation / 85-year-old self.** From *Change Your Life by Journalling* (`E8vwGLMTx5I`), attributed to **Alex Hormozi**:
> "I have a conversation with my future self who's 85 and I asked him for advice on what I should do now… my 85 year old self is generally telling me to chill the F out"
Mechanism given: "people give significantly better advice about their own scenarios if they don't know it's about themselves."

### 2.3 The "Ideal Ordinary Week" ⭐ his own branded exercise

This is the one piece of vocabulary that is distinctively his (though the underlying idea is calendar-blocking).

**[VERIFIED]** Exact newsletter text, [My Ideal Ordinary Week](https://aliabdaal.com/newsletter/my-ideal-ordinary-week/):

> "Imagine your ideal week, with all the activities you'd make time for – work, exercise, time with family, hobbies etc.
> Then create a blank Google Calendar and block out reasonable times for your top priorities. For example:
> - 8-9am every Tuesday and Thursday for exercise.
> - 6-8pm every day for 'family time'.
> - 30 mins of reading each night, and lights off at 11pm."

**Two-phase protocol (exact):**
> "**Phase 1 – Ideal ordinary week right now.** Trying to imagine your ideal week **right now**, accounting for all the constraints of your current life. Once you're done, move those blocks to your **real** calendar and set them as recurring events.
> **Phase 2 – Ideal ordinary week in the future.** A year from now, or 3 years from now, what would you love a normal week to look like? … this is more of a goal-setting activity, to know what you're working towards."

- **Time:** "**15 minutes brainstorming your ideal week**" (exact, newsletter). Elsewhere: "I redid this exercise this morning in about **10 minutes**" (`FQJ3MsQFKNg`).
- **Inputs:** a blank calendar (Google/iCal/paper).
- **Outputs:** a recurring-events calendar, which he treats as three things: intentionality forcing-function, **nudge system** ("At 10pm I get a calendar notification saying 'No more devices, bed and reading'… It's a gentle suggestion from my *intentional self*"), and **gatekeeper** for saying no.
- **The key follow-up question** (`FQJ3MsQFKNg`): "**what is stopping me from just having this as my ideal ordinary week now? what are the barriers that are in the way of me achieving this vision of my life?**"
- **Origin:** **[VERIFIED]** he credits **Simon Severino** (business coach, Strategy Sprints) — "I came across this from my friend Simon Severino who's a business coach at Strategy Sprints" (`FQJ3MsQFKNg`). He does **not** credit Designing Your Life or Cal Newport for it.
- **Horizon:** varies. "**two years in the future**" (`FQJ3MsQFKNg`); "a year from now, two years from now, five years from now, ten years from now" (`mZOVLrLXKCE`).
- **Sequence:** he uses it in *two* places — as a direction-finding prompt (what do I value?) and as the **execution layer** at the end of annual planning (see §3.3). This dual use is deliberate.

**Its role in him quitting medicine** — **[VERIFIED]** `mZOVLrLXKCE`:
> "I also did this exercise called the ideal ordinary week… I realized I was spending a bunch of time reading a bunch of time writing a bunch of time teaching… and I did this exercise and I realized that **working as a doctor does not fit into this kind of conception of my life at all**"

#### 2.3a The Ideal Tuesday
**[VERIFIED]** `PtMLCL_T2ak`, verbatim:
> "the ideal Tuesday prompt which is **what does your ideal Tuesday look like what are you doing who are you with how are you spending your time what makes this day perfect for you** and you can imagine the ideal Tuesday **a year from now** or 5 years from now — I like to imagine it a year from now"

The attendee flags the design reason: "he chose Tuesday for this exercise, cuz it's easy to think of your ideal weekend day, but choosing a work day helped me reflect on my current job situation."
He also mentions an **ideal Sunday** variant (`iuHuxX22OOQ`).

#### 2.3b The Ideal End State exercise
**[VERIFIED]** *How to Create Your Ideal Life in 7 Minutes* (23 Aug 2024, `iuHuxX22OOQ`).

> **Prompt (as quoted from the originator):** "literally you make a **bulleted list** of if you could have your ideal life what would it look like and you should be **very specific** around like what a day looks like — like who you're around, what you're doing, how you're feeling"

- **Time:** "**it only takes like an hour**" (quoted from originator).
- **Origin:** **[VERIFIED]** **Kevin Dahlstrom**, heard on **Michael Karnjanaprakorn's** podcast. Not Abdaal's.
- **The diagnostic** (quoted verbatim from Dahlstrom): "every time I've gone through this exercise with someone it really highlights the **inconsistencies between what they say they want and their actions**… let's say you just bought a Porsche 911 but on your list it doesn't say drive a fancy car — well that begs the question, are you being honest about what you really want, or are your actions not aligning with what you want?"
- **What Abdaal does with the output (exact):** "you look at what your life is like currently and you look at this future ideal end state… and you figure out okay cool **are the actions I'm taking aligned with eventually getting me to this sort of place** and if they're not, like either it's worth changing the goal… or it's worth changing the actions"
- Abdaal's own list is published in that video and is a useful specimen: four kids, open-door family home, gym whenever he feels like it, books "every few years whenever I want to explore an interesting question", walkable office/studio/event space, annual ski + surf trip he pays for, music jam sessions, a holdco of small profitable businesses at $10M+ profit, a Tony-Robbins-style / Derren-Brown-style one-man stage show, and the **10% Pledge** for business + personal giving.

### 2.4 The Wheel of Life ⭐ his standing "where am I now" instrument

**[VERIFIED]** Appears in *Feel-Good Productivity* (final chapter), in `AriiQaEWyI8`, `c_DOG_mXz5w`, `E8vwGLMTx5I`, `MoGTXsusX6Q`, and as a free quiz at **https://play.aliabdaal.com/wheeloflife**.

**His structure — 3 → 9 (+1):**

```
WORK           HEALTH          RELATIONSHIPS      (+ outside the wheel)
├ Mission      ├ Body          ├ Romance          └ JOY
├ Money        ├ Mind          ├ Family
└ Growth       └ Soul          └ Friends
```

**[VERIFIED]** exact structure from `c_DOG_mXz5w`: "within health we have three things and that is **body, mind, and soul**… Under relationships, we've got **romance, family, and friends**. And then under work, we have **mission, money, and growth**." Plus in `AriiQaEWyI8`: "I would add a final category which is **outside of the wheel** and I would call it **Joy**."

- **Prompt (his preferred wording):** "rate yourself from a scale of 0 to 10 — **how aligned do you feel in this area of your life**". He explicitly prefers *alignment* over *satisfaction*: "By aligned, I mean **my actions in the here and now are consistent with where I think I would like to be**." (`c_DOG_mXz5w`)
- **Time:** trivially short and deliberately so — "sometimes I just think about it if I'm like waiting in line somewhere or if I'm on the toilet" (`AriiQaEWyI8`).
- **Inputs:** nothing. Output: 10 numbers → one identified area to move.
- **The low-scorer handling rule** (important, and unusually humane) — **[VERIFIED]** `AriiQaEWyI8`: "if you're rating yourself very low across all areas of life then I suspect you're the kind of person who's probably a little bit harsh to yourself… **ask yourself what is it that makes this category a three rather than a zero — what am I giving myself points for** — and then okay what would it take to get it to a four… **we're not shooting for 10 out of 10 here we're just shooting for a little marginal improvement**"
- **Cadence:** weekly for some of his team ("he's been doing The Wheel of Life every Sunday for the last like several years"); he uses it as a greeting — "instead of being like how's it going I was like **how's the Wheel of Life**".
- **Origin:** **not his**. Standard life-coaching instrument (Paul J. Meyer / Success Motivation Institute lineage; popularised as "Level 10 Life" by Hal Elrod's *Miracle Morning*). **[VERIFIED]** that a third-party observer described his Dec-2023 video as "a new way of using the **Level 10 Life** exercise". **[INFERRED]** that his 3×3+Joy decomposition is his own contribution — I found no source using exactly this taxonomy before him, but I could not prove originality.

### 2.5 Energy Audit

**[VERIFIED]** Prompt #4 of 10 in `MoGTXsusX6Q`, and a core method in `AriiQaEWyI8`.

> **Prompt (verbatim):** "**what have I done in the last 2 weeks that has energized me and what have I done in the last 2 weeks that has drained my energy** — this is the energy audit"

**Protocol (verbatim):**
> "you don't even need to write it out… you can flick [through your calendar] for the last two weeks and you can **colour code your calendar events or you can even rename your calendar events with a plus or a plus plus if it gave you energy or a minus and a minus minus if it drained your energy and sort of a plus minus if it was kind of neutral**"

- **Time:** minutes. **Inputs:** your last 2 weeks of calendar. **Output:** a +/- annotated calendar → two lists.
- **What he does with it:** (a) do more of the pluses, less of the minuses; (b) for unavoidable minuses, apply the *Feel-Good Productivity* **3 Ps — Play, Power, People** to convert a drain into a gain. Aspiration: "it would be so sick if everything on your calendar was a net gain in energy rather than a net loss."
- **Origin:** **[VERIFIED]** he credits **Prof. Grace Lordan**'s book (auto-captions render it "Professor Grace Len's book *Think Big, Take Small Steps*" — the real title is ***Think Big: Take Small Steps and Build the Career You Want***, and she's at LSE). He also flags the parallel: "the guys who wrote the book **Designing Your Life** also have a similar one which they call the **Good Time Journal** where you rate your energy and engagement in each of these different activities" (`AriiQaEWyI8`) — correct attribution; DYL's Good Time Journal logs *engagement* and *energy* per activity.
- **He models honest negative results with it:** he audited video-gaming, which he *wanted* to be energising, and found "annoyingly **it drains my energy** a lot of the time… maybe actually this whole 'I want to play more video games' is actually just me **clinging to an identity that I had when I was 14 years old**." That's a good template for how the exercise is supposed to bite.
- **Sequence:** "where am I now" (ep 2), and as a real-time decision filter (see §2.8).

### 2.6 Values — what he actually does (and doesn't)

**Direct answer to the brief: no. Abdaal does not teach a values sort, a values card-deck, or a "pick your top 5 from this list of 200" exercise. I found no such exercise anywhere in his public material.** **[VERIFIED]** by absence across 20 transcripts, the newsletter archive, and the LifeOS sales page.

What he does instead — values are **inferred from other exercises' outputs**, never elicited directly:

1. **From the funeral/obituary prompts.** "This is generally a good way of clarifying what you care about, **what your own values are**" (`QF2wIywvDhk`).

### 2.8 Fear-setting (Tim Ferriss) and decision prompts

#### Fear-setting
**[VERIFIED]** He teaches it as Ferriss's, by name, in at least three places. Two versions exist.

**Version A — 5 questions**, from *How to Make Difficult Decisions* (30 Nov 2022, `XILu7hxDr2w`, auto-captions, his own paraphrase of Ferriss):
> 1. "**what am I not doing because I'm scared**" (+ "what am I missing out on by not doing the thing that I'm scared of")
> 2. "**why am I not doing this thing**… what are the other reasons"
> 3. "**what's the absolute worst case scenario if I did the thing**"
> 4. "**what can I do to mitigate against the risk of that worst case scenario happening, or to deal with the worst case scenario when it does happen**"
> 5. "**if I had to reverse this decision how would I go about it**"

**Version B — closer to Ferriss's actual 3-page structure**, from *Change Your Life by Journalling* (`E8vwGLMTx5I`):
> "**what is the worst thing that will happen if I do the thing that I fear doing? what can I do to prevent each of the worst things from happening? if the worst case scenario happened, what can I do to repair it? what are the benefits of an attempt or partial success? if I don't do the thing that scares me, what will my life look like in six months, one year and three years?**"

- **Time:** **[VERIFIED]** "asking yourself these questions for **just 10 minutes** can genuinely be 10 minutes that completely changes your life" (`E8vwGLMTx5I`).
- **Origin:** **Tim Ferriss** — "fear-setting", from *The 4-Hour Workweek* + his TED talk "Why you should define your fears instead of your goals" + the blog post *Fear-Setting: The Most Valuable Exercise I Do Every Month*. Abdaal names all of these.
- **[VERIFIED] He has filmed himself doing it for real**, on a real decision, in *Reflecting On My Fears – Day in the Life* (3 Dec 2021, `HPcIwHwZIhI`). The decision was "**what if I genuinely stopped caring about the numbers**" (views/revenue/subscribers). He googles "tim ferriss fear setting exercise", lands on the blog post, and works it live. Quote: "the reason Tim Ferriss says this exercise is so powerful is because a lot of the fears that we have…". He describes tearing up at Ferriss's line about the lawyer: "**it's not giving up to put your current path on indefinite pause**". This is the single best primary artefact of him *using* an inherited exercise rather than describing it.

#### The 10 decision questions (*How to Make Difficult Decisions*, `XILu7hxDr2w`)
**[VERIFIED]**, verbatim, in his countdown order (10 → 1):

| # | Prompt |
|---|--------|
| 10 | "**in one year's time, what would I regret not having started today?**" |
| 9 | "**what would my mental Board of Advisors say?**" — a handful of admired people, living or dead. His: Derek Sivers, Tim Ferriss, Ryan Holiday, Cal Newport. |
| 8 | "**what is the risk of doing nothing?**" — "inaction does not actually have zero consequences; doing nothing is also a decision" |
| 7a | "**what core value am I optimizing for?**" |
| 7b | "**when I'm on my deathbed, what will I regret having done or not having done?**" |
| 6 | "**how certain am I about this decision, and how certain do I need to be to make this decision?**" |
| 5 | "**how might I treat this like an experiment?**" |
| 4 | "**do I want to be the sort of person who does X?**" (identity-based) |
| 3 | "**will this energize me or will this drain me?**" |
| 2 | The **quitting framework** (below) |
| 1 | **Fear-setting** (above) |

*(Note: he numbers two questions "7" — that's his error, not a transcription artefact.)*

**The quitting framework**, credited to **Steven Bartlett, *Happy Sexy Millionaire***, verbatim:
> "why am I thinking about quitting? Generally the two options are **it sucks** or **it's hard**.
> If the thing is **hard** → is the challenge worth the potential reward? Yes → keep going. No → quit.
> If the thing **sucks** → do I believe I could make it not suck? No → quit. Yes → is it worth the effort to try and make it not suck? Yes → don't quit. No → quit."

**On "how might I treat this like an experiment"** he gives the best-documented worked example in his whole corpus: rather than applying for anaesthetics specialty training and *then* negotiating part-time, he "just picked up a few extra shifts in the emergency department and tried to recreate that life for myself where I was working one day a week as a doctor and I realized — oh, within a few weeks, I actually don't really enjoy this."

**Note what is NOT in his corpus:** I found **no** use of **regret minimisation framework (Bezos) by name**, **no 10/10/10 (Suzy Welch)**, and **no two-way-door / one-way-door (reversible vs irreversible)** framing. He gets adjacent to reversibility only via fear-setting Q5 ("if I had to reverse this decision how would I go about it") and via `0j3SW7R40SU` where "reversible" appears in passing. → see §6 Gaps.

#### The fast-forward test (his own coinage)
**[VERIFIED]** *How To Build A Life You Love* (4 Nov 2022, `EwSD4uCSX_M`), verbatim:
> "let's take a standard day in your life and let's say with each thing that you do you have the option to **fast forward to the end** of the thing — do you press the button, where you don't experience the thing that you're doing, you just fast forward to the end and get the result?… back when I was working as a doctor I actually would… I wouldn't have pressed that button if I was teaching medical students… **my definition of a life that I love is one where I wouldn't fast forward very many things in my life**"

**[VERIFIED]** Its twin, the **skip test**, appears in `0j3SW7R40SU` (11 Jun 2026), where **Paul Millerd credits it back to Abdaal**: "I think I got this idea from you called the **skip test**… **Would you skip this task if you could just get to the end result?**"

### 2.9 The career decision framework — money / fun / impact

**[VERIFIED]** *How To Find The Perfect Career – The 3 Part Decision Framework* (2 Sep 2022, `lJ6n52Lsjfo`), made in paid partnership with **80,000 Hours**.

Opening claim: "if you ask most people what they want from their careers they'll say some combination of **I want to make money, I want to have fun, and I want to help people**".

**Part 1 — Money is a satisficing good.**
> "there's **satisficing** and there is **maximizing**… money is a satisficing good… beyond a certain point — usually studies say around about **seventy thousand dollars** if you're in the US or like **sixty thousand pounds** if you're in the UK — you start getting diminishing marginal utility."

**Part 2 — Fun is also a satisficing good.** Three sub-levers:
1. **Job characteristics** research-backed: "autonomy… you like your work colleagues… you are learning stuff and growing… you are playing to your strengths… and the job has no major negatives like **commuting more than 20 minutes**". *(This list is 80,000 Hours' six ingredients of a satisfying job, lightly reworded.)* **[INFERRED]** on the exact provenance of the list.
2. **Career capital** (Cal Newport) — don't follow passion, get so good you can trade skill for autonomy.
3. **Cheap experimentation before commitment.** His killer stat/analogy, verbatim: "when it comes to going out to dinner at a restaurant we spend maybe 10-15 minutes deciding what restaurant to go to and it might last like two hours, so that is 10% of the time… and yet when it comes to making decisions about our careers we spend a lot less than 10% of our total work time… that adds up to **80,000 hours** and we definitely don't spend **8,000 hours** of our lives actually making decisions about what career to pursue, even though we probably should."

**Part 3 — Helping people. Straight 80,000 Hours / effective-altruism.** Three tests, verbatim:
> "firstly **what is the problem that you are working on** — like what problem area is it; secondly **how effective are you at working on it**; and thirdly **how much personal fit** do you have for that particular thing"
Plus **scale × neglectedness** ("if you are the 301st person working on a problem you can probably have way more impact than if you are the 40,000th and first"), worked through climate change (big but well-funded), AI safety, and pandemic prevention.

- **Origin:** **80,000 Hours** (Oxford-affiliated nonprofit; Will MacAskill, EA movement). Openly a sponsored video; he names them repeatedly and links their free career guide. The scale/neglectedness/tractability + personal fit framework is theirs, not his.
- **Sequence:** this is his *career-specific* layer, downstream of the general North Star work.

### 2.10 Finding purpose — the 2025 Joe Hudson 5-step (his current position)

**[VERIFIED]** *How to Find Your Life's Purpose* (20 Oct 2025, `euNliPIJ7Kc`), built on an interview with **Joe Hudson** (executive coach — Apple, Google, OpenAI). This is his most recent and most philosophically distinct take, and it **partly contradicts** the earlier vision-first material.

| Step | Name | Protocol |
|------|------|----------|
| 1 | **Conduct a purpose audit** | Three yes/no diagnostics (below) |
| 2 | **Think creatively about the money problem** | Binary thinking = fear; "there's literally infinite ways to make money" |
| 3 | **Experiment with authenticity** — *before* you quit | 30-day protocol (below) |
| 4 | **Cut the self-criticism** | "most of the times when people are just thinking 'what's my purpose?' that's just **self-abuse**" |
| 5 | **Find purpose in the present** | "purpose isn't found in the future… **your purpose is expressed in how you do things, not necessarily in what you are specifically doing**" |

**Step 1 — the purpose audit, verbatim (his summary of Hudson's tests):**
> "Firstly, **would you sell what you're doing for a billion dollars?** If yes, then you're probably not living your purpose to some degree.
> Secondly, **are you living for some sort of reward in the future?** Is that the basis of the work that you're doing?
> And thirdly, **do you feel like you should do something, or is there a lot of pressure from your family or society more broadly to do a certain thing?** If you're feeling **the should** behind doing something, that thing is unlikely to be your purpose."

He softens the billion-dollar test himself: "for this one personally, I don't take it too literally… I treat this more as a general feeling of, **am I doing it for the money or am I doing it not for the money?**"

He adds a fourth, felt-sense test from his own life: the **Sunday scaries** as purpose-signal — "back when I was working as a doctor, I felt the gut response every Sunday evening… whereas when I was teaching medical students… I would always have a feeling of 'ooh this is going to be fun'."

**Step 3 — the 30-day authenticity experiment. Full protocol, verbatim:**
> "before you quit your job or make drastic changes to your life, try being completely authentic in how you approach whatever you're doing **for the next 30 days**. Ask yourself: **if I were living my purpose in this work, what would it look like? How would I show up? How would I listen? How would I speak? How would I approach my work?**"
> Hudson's implementation detail: "there's a great timer… we use the one called **Yap**… it'll make a ding **seven, eight, nine times a day**… and you have it remind you to just say, **how do you live on purpose right now?** And you just… take **20 seconds** and do it… if you do that for **two, three, four weeks**, your search for purpose will completely flip on its head."
- **Diagnostic value:** "it helps you figure out — is the problem here **what** you're doing, or is the problem **how** you're doing it."

**[VERIFIED] Related: the "tyranny of purpose" critique**, from **Anne-Laure Le Cunff's *Tiny Experiments*** — he cites her chapter by name in both `euNliPIJ7Kc` and *How to Find Your Purpose (with Tiny Experiments)* (4 Mar 2025, `vmH3seddlPE`):
> "we've replaced [the corporate ladder] with this other thing that's like 'oh man I need to find my one true purpose and I'm a failure in life if I haven't figured out my one true purpose'… even though we like to think we chose freedom in rejecting those traditional corporate roles, we've actually just traded that for this other equally restrictive thing."

Also from Le Cunff, **[VERIFIED]**: **liminal spaces** — "a time in your life where you're not quite sure what you should be doing next where there are no clear rules where there's no definition of success… the automatic reaction… is to try and cross it as quickly as possible" — and the instruction to *stay* in them.

### 2.11 "Where am I now" instruments (mini-series ep 2, `AriiQaEWyI8`)

**(a) What's working / what's not working.** Verbatim: "**when it comes to work right now, what's working and what's not working**" — then repeat for life ("life is usually mostly personal health and relationships").

**(b) The Truths exercise.** Credited to **Robin Waite** (his business coach). Verbatim: "**list off 10 things that you know are true about you right now**". Rules: positive statements only — "please do not write negative statements about yourself; think about **what you do have and who you are** rather than what you don't have and who you are not." Rationale: "nobody can take those away."

**(c) Future Truths.** The second half of the same exercise, and the mechanic is a **tense change**:
> Waite: "when you say **I'm going to**, it kind of gives you permission to kind of put it off into the future… when you're creating future truths you have to treat them like **they've already happened**, so you just change the language to **I have / I am**."
> Abdaal's live answers: "I am a published author. I have lived in Austin for a few months. I've written a book I'm proud of. I am currently working on my second book."
> Waite: "**that's Ali's identity in 12 months time. That's who you are.**"
> Use: "I have a list of like **100 truths** — both future ones and existing ones… whenever I have that moment of self-doubt I go back to that."

**(d) Fulfilment Stories.** **[VERIFIED]** Credited to **Luke Burgess, *Wanting*** (which builds on **René Girard's mimetic desire**). Abdaal reads Burgess's three criteria out verbatim:
> "**think about or write about a time in your life when you did something well and it brought you a sense of fulfillment**. A fulfillment story needs to have these three things:
> 1. **it's an action you took** — some concrete action and you were the main protagonist, as opposed to passively taking in an experience…
> 2. **you believe you did well** — you did it with excellence, by your own estimation and nobody else's…
> 3. **it brought you a sense of fulfillment** — not the fleeting temporary kind like an endorphin rush… you woke up the next morning and you felt a sense of satisfaction about it."
> Quantity: "**identify 3 to 5 or more**".

The reason he puts this exercise here is explicitly **anti-mimetic**: "the things that we want are often not shaped by some internal sense of self… if a friend or a neighbour has bought a Tesla Model 3 you're going to be much more likely to spontaneously decide that you also want a Tesla Model 3."

**B. The withheld detail, recovered from a paying attendee.** **[VERIFIED as a second-hand account]** — *Productive Happiness* Substack, 10 Jan 2024, a paraphrase not a transcript:
1. **Divergent:** "think of all the things you would like to **have, be and do** for the next 10 years"; prompts "What do you want to **learn**? Who do you want to **be**? What do you want to **try**? What do you want to **overcome**?"; find dreams in each of **3 categories — Relationships, Career, Personal development**; "set a timer for **10 minutes** and write everything down. Do not censor yourself!"
2. **Time-horizon sort:** "review your list and write a number next to it from **1, 3, 5, 10**" (= achieve in 1 year / 3 years / 5 years / 10 years), then rewrite grouped by horizon.
3. **Convergent:** "select **3 goals** you absolutely want to achieve this year — if possible, one for career, one for relationships, one for personal development."
4. **Why-laddering:** "ask yourself **why this goal is important for you**… Ali suggests that you **ask yourself why 5 times** for each goal."
5. **Action plan + habits + schedule them.**

**C. The bridge mechanism he teaches publicly — 12-Month Celebration → Ideal Week.** This is the cleanest vision→execution converter in his work. **[VERIFIED]**

The prompt (exact, newsletter *The Power of Questions*):
> "trying to work out what our goals should be for the year is hard. But, if, instead, **you imagine you're sat down with a friend in 12 months time celebrating your biggest achievements of the year, what would the two of you be celebrating?** This is the same question as 'what are your goals this year?', but framed in a different way that makes it easier to answer."

Then the conversion, verbatim from `c_DOG_mXz5w`:
> "let's say in the friends category I wanted to see my friends more often. My **12-month celebration** might be that I'm celebrating the fact that I saw my friends for a group dinner at least once a week. In my **ideal week calendar** I therefore need to have a **block at least once a week for a group dinner with friends**… my 12-month celebration is becoming more lean, and that means I need to have half an hour a day blocked into my ideal week for exercise."

And the constraint check — **the calendar is the reality test on the vision**:
> "if you realize that you don't actually have time for a particular thing because you're busy with other areas of your life, that's totally okay — **but that means you can change your goal**. You can change the 12-month celebration to reflect the time that you actually have… **it forces us to get clarity on what is actually important to us**… and mostly you'll probably realize that this is less time than you thought you had. Which is great, because it means you can start doing fewer things and you can **cross some things off your 12-month celebration list**."

So the full public bridge is:

```
Wheel of Life (which of the 9+1 areas is low?)
      ↓
12-Month Celebration (what would I be toasting in that area in 12 months?)
      ↓
Ideal Ordinary Week (what recurring calendar block does that require?)
      ↓
Real calendar (recurring events)
      ↓  [if it doesn't fit → go back and cut the goal, not the sleep]
```

### High confidence (multiple primary sources, verbatim)
- Odyssey Plan wording, timing, attribution, decision rule, and its causal role in him leaving medicine. Three independent primary sources + wording drift documented.
- Gravestone / funeral / Wikipedia / obituary family — all five variants, with exact prompts.
- Ideal Ordinary Week + Ideal Tuesday — full protocol, both time estimates, both phases.
- Wheel of Life — exact 9+1 taxonomy, "alignment not satisfaction" wording, low-scorer handling.
- Energy Audit — exact prompt, +/- calendar mechanic, both attributions.
- Fear-setting — both versions, plus a filmed instance of him doing it.
- The 10 decision questions — complete and verbatim.
- 80,000 Hours career framework — complete.
- Joe Hudson 5-step purpose road map — complete, incl. 30-day protocol and Yap timer detail.
- The 12-Month Celebration → Ideal Week bridge.

### Medium confidence
- **The annual workshop's Step 3 (divergent→convergent goal selection).** Only source is one attendee's Substack paraphrase (§5.4). The 1/3/5/10 numbering, "have/be/do", "3 goals across 3 categories", and "ask why 5 times" are plausible and consistent with his other material but **not verbatim from him**. Treat as [INFERRED].
- **Wheel-of-Life originality.** The 3×3+Joy split looks like his; I couldn't prove no prior art.
- **The "six ingredients of a satisfying job" list** in `lJ6n52Lsjfo` — almost certainly lifted from 80,000 Hours' research, but he doesn't say so in that segment.

### Could NOT verify / genuine gaps
1. **The ikigai "portfolio" criticism.** Search engines surface it consistently and it is directionally corroborated by a verbatim "portfolio career" quote, but **I could not find a primary source with his exact wording** for "you don't need impact + rich + enjoyment from one pursuit". Do not quote it as verbatim. The one thing I *can* verify is the Forcing Function quote, which uses **three** circles (like / good at / paid) and drops "what the world needs".
2. **He has no dedicated ikigai video or article.** Confirmed by absence across 20 transcripts and the newsletter archive. If a product needs "his ikigai exercise", there isn't one to copy.
3. **No values sort / values list exercise exists publicly.** He infers values from other outputs. The LifeOS "**Life Compass** — 4-part framework" is paywalled and its four parts are undisclosed. This is the single biggest hole for the brief's question 4.
4. **The North Star prompt list in the Dec-2023 newsletter is missing from the rendered page.** He writes "Here's a flavour of some of my favourite prompts" and then the list itself does not appear in the retrievable HTML (likely an embedded image or a block the reader stripped). The prompts are almost certainly the `PtMLCL_T2ak` set, but that's an inference.
5. **The Journalling Prompts Notion template and the Journaling Hub doc are email-gated.** He says the Hub has "hundreds and hundreds" of prompts. Not retrieved. Getting them requires a real email signup.
6. **The 9 LinkedIn prompts (Jan 2026)** are reported but not reproduced by the third-party article; the LinkedIn post itself wasn't retrievable.
7. **No regret-minimisation (Bezos), no 10/10/10 (Suzy Welch), no reversible/irreversible two-way-door framing** anywhere in his corpus. If the brief expected these, they are absent — the nearest equivalents are his deathbed question, the "risk of doing nothing" question, and fear-setting Q5.
8. **Deep Dive podcast episodes were not mined.** He has ~150+ episodes; several (Joe Hudson, Anne-Laure Le Cunff, Chris Sparks, Sahil Bloom, Grace Lordan, Paul Millerd, Robin Waite) almost certainly contain longer versions of these exercises. The YouTube main-channel versions capture his *teaching* of them, which is what this file needed, but the podcasts would add nuance.
9. **He never gives a total time budget for the whole sequence.** Individual exercises are 10–60 min. His paid annual workshop was **3 hours (1400–1700 GMT)**, which is the only bounded figure for the full run.
10. **Auto-caption fidelity.** Names are mangled in captions (Grace Lordan → "Grace Len", Anne-Laure Le Cunff → "anore", Karnjanaprakorn → "kjan prorn", Millerd → "Millard/Miller"). Substance is reliable; exact wording of long quotes from video should be re-checked against audio before being published as verbatim.
