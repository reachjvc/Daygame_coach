## 1. Overview

**Book:** *Feel-Good Productivity: How to Do More of What Matters to You*
**Author:** Dr Ali Abdaal (ex-NHS doctor, Cambridge; YouTuber)
**Publisher:** Celadon Books (US) / Cornerstone-Penguin (UK)
**Publication:** 26 December 2023 (US); ISBNs in the 9781250865038–9781250289803 range. `[VERIFIED — Celadon Books page]`
**Length:** ~270 pages. `[VERIFIED — knowledgeecology review]`

### Core thesis `[VERIFIED]`

Publisher framing: *"The secret to productivity isn't discipline. It's joy."* — Celadon Books.
Retail framing: *"the secret to productivity and success isn't grind – it's feeling good."* — Barnes & Noble publisher copy.

Abdaal's own one-liner, repeated across summaries: **"Success doesn't lead to feeling good. Feeling good leads to success."** `[VERIFIED — quoted identically by roamingreina, ericsandroni, Med School Insiders, grahammann]`

The mechanism he claims:

1. **Broaden-and-build theory** (Barbara Fredrickson). Positive emotions *broaden* awareness (you see more options) and *build* durable resources (resilience, creativity, social capital). `[VERIFIED — Shortform, readingraphics, roamingreina]`
2. **Four "feel-good hormones"**: endorphins, serotonin, dopamine, oxytocin — contrasted against adrenaline/cortisol which power the "grind" and work short-term but damage long-term. `[VERIFIED — grahammann, roamingreina, Shortform blog]`
   - Book quote (via search snippet): *"The grind mindset taps into negative feelings that trigger the production of stress hormones such as adrenaline and cortisol. They provide powerful short-term motivation, but they have harmful long-term effects because we didn't evolve to spend our whole lives feeling stressed and pressured."* `[VERIFIED — snippet surfaced in search of book quotes; single-source, treat as near-verbatim]`
3. **The undoing hypothesis**: positive emotions undo the cardiovascular after-effects of stress faster than neutral or negative ones. `[VERIFIED — grahammann]`
4. **Virtuous cycle**: *"feeling good boosts your energy, energy boosts your productivity, and productivity makes you feel even better."* `[VERIFIED — Shortform blog, quoting/paraphrasing the book]`

### The "productivity scientist" frame `[VERIFIED]`

The closing section is titled **"Last Word: Think Like a Productivity Scientist"** `[VERIFIED — TOC listing surfaced in search; single search-result source, medium confidence]`.

Book quote: *"Think like a productivity scientist… Try as much as you can, figure out what works, and discard the rest."* `[VERIFIED — surfaced as a direct quote in search of book quotes; single-source]`

This matters structurally: the book's unit of adoption is **the experiment**, not the habit or the system. Every chapter ends with experiments to run and keep or bin.

### 2.3 Part 1 — ENERGISE

#### Chapter 1: Play
- Claim: play reduces stress, boosts creativity, improves wellbeing; Abdaal cites Nobel laureates describing their work as play (Watson & Crick on DNA; Fleming "playing with microbes"). `[VERIFIED — grahammann, summrize]`
- Book quote: *"Play is our first energiser. Life is stressful. Play makes it fun. If we can integrate the spirit of play into our lives, we'll feel better – and do more too."* `[VERIFIED — Goodreads quotes page]`
- **8 play personalities**, taken from Dr Stuart Brown / National Institute for Play: **Collector, Competitor, Explorer, Creator, Storyteller, Joker, Director, Kinesthete**. `[VERIFIED — Abdaal's own X post listing all 8; corroborated by 4+ summaries; Brown attribution verified]`
- Signature question: **"What would this look like if it were fun?"** Book quote: *"'What would this look like if it were fun?' has now become a guiding question in my life."* `[VERIFIED — Goodreads]`
- Sub-concepts: **side quests** (curiosity detours that don't advance the main goal), **lowering the stakes**, **failure as data**, **"don't be serious, be sincere"** (attributed to Alan Watts: *"a little bit less serious and a little bit more sincere"*). `[VERIFIED — Art of Manliness, tosummarise, summrize]`

#### Chapter 2: Power
- Power = **self-efficacy + ownership**, i.e. feeling in control of your work — explicitly *not* power over others. `[VERIFIED — summrize]`
- Three strategies (per summrize's grouping): boost confidence → level up (skills) → take ownership. `[VERIFIED, single-source grouping]`
- Book quotes: *"Feeling confident about our ability to complete a task makes us feel good when we're doing it, and helps us do it better."*; *"When we can't take ownership of the situation, we can still take ownership of the process."*; *"Shoshin refers to a state of mind in which we approach every task and situation with the curiosity, openness and humility of a beginner."*; *"By letting go of the idea that we know everything, or somehow should, we actually feel more powerful."*; *"With this mindset shift – from 'have to' to 'choose to'…"* `[VERIFIED — Goodreads quotes page]`
- Abdaal on progress-tracking as a power source: *"the single best thing I ever did to make working out feel good was to start tracking my numbers on an app."* `[VERIFIED — Art of Manliness]`

#### Chapter 3: People
- Concept: **relational energy** — other people are a direct energy input or drain ("energy vampires"). `[VERIFIED — readingraphics, griply]`
- **Comrade mindset** (teammates, not competitors). **Scenius** — creative scenes beat lone geniuses (Brian Eno / London glam-rock example). **Benjamin Franklin effect** — asking for help makes people like you more. **Active-constructive responding** to others' good news. **Candour over mere honesty** for bad news. `[VERIFIED — tosummarise, summrize, grahammann]`
- Swedish proverb used: *"A shared joy is a double joy; a shared sorrow is half sorrow."* `[VERIFIED — grahammann]`

### 2.4 Part 2 — UNBLOCK

Abdaal's model of procrastination: it is **not** a motivation/discipline deficit, it is an **emotional** problem. Three roots and three antidotes:

| Blocker | Diagnostic question | Antidote chapter | Core tools |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Uncertainty** | "Why? What? When?" — do I know the purpose, the concrete next thing, and the time it happens? | Seek Clarity | Commander's Intent, Five Whys, NICE goals, Crystal Ball (pre-mortem), Implementation Intentions, Time Blocking |
| **Fear** | "What exactly am I afraid of?" | Find Courage | Emotion Label, Identity Label, 10/10/10, Confidence Equation, Stop Spotlighting, Batman Effect |
| **Inertia** | "What's the smallest possible next action?" | Get Started | Environmental friction, Emotional friction (5-min rule), Next Action Step, Track Progress, Accountability Buddy, Forgive Yourself |

`[VERIFIED — ericsandroni + roamingreina agree on all 18 experiment names; Four Minute Books confirms the "Why? How? What? When?" clarity questioning; Modern Wisdom transcript confirms "what, why, and when"]`

Note the "Uncertainty Loop" model: overestimation of threat → hypervigilance → failure to register safety cues → avoidance. `[VERIFIED — roamingreina, single-source]`

**Motivation quote:** *"Motivation clearly isn't enough. And telling people to simply 'feel more motivated' isn't just unhelpful, it's potentially harmful."* `[VERIFIED — Goodreads]`

### 2.5 Part 3 — SUSTAIN (the burnout model)

This is the section most relevant to life-direction work, so it gets extra depth.

**Abdaal's three burnout types** `[VERIFIED — Abdaal in his own words on Art of Manliness, Modern Wisdom, and Linking Your Thinking podcasts, plus every book summary]`:

1. **Over-exertion burnout** — "doing too much." Too many concurrent commitments; no slack. Antidote: **Conserve**.
2. **Depletion burnout** — "resting wrong." You take breaks but they don't restore (passive scrolling ≠ rest). Antidote: **Recharge**.
3. **Misalignment burnout** — "doing the wrong things." Antidote: **Align**.

**Definition of misalignment burnout (closest to verbatim I could source):**
> *"Misalignment burnout arises from the negative feelings that arise when our goals don't match up to our sense of self."*
`[VERIFIED — surfaced as a direct book quote in search results; single-source, so treat as near-verbatim rather than certified verbatim]`

Complementary phrasings, all `[VERIFIED]` from Abdaal's spoken interviews / summaries:
- *"a gap has grown between you and your work, and it no longer feels meaningful, which is why you're tired"* (search-surfaced summary phrasing).
- On Linking Your Thinking he calls misalignment **"the most interesting one"** because it strikes people who are objectively succeeding but whose work contradicts their deeper purpose. `[VERIFIED]`
- Art of Manliness: misalignment = *"work conflicts with life direction"*; the fix is to *"clarify your north star."*

**How Abdaal says to DETECT misalignment** `[VERIFIED, assembled from Art of Manliness + tosummarise + ericsandroni]`:
1. Note the symptom: tiredness/emptiness despite objective success or adequate rest — i.e. you've ruled out over-exertion and depletion.
2. Check your **motivation type** against the hierarchy (see below). If your drivers are External or Introjected, that is the misalignment signal.
3. Run the **Wheel of Life** rating to localise *which* of the nine life areas is misaligned.
4. Cross-check daily behaviour against **Eulogy Method** / **Odyssey Plan** outputs: are the things you'd want said about you present in your calendar?

**Motivation hierarchy** (from Self-Determination Theory), worst → best `[VERIFIED — flippingba, roamingreina]`:
1. **External** extrinsic (rewards, others' approval)
2. **Introjected** extrinsic (guilt/shame avoidance)
3. **Identified** extrinsic (you personally value the outcome)
4. **Intrinsic** (the process itself is the reward)

Book quotes: *"Intrinsic motivation comes from the inside: driven by self-fulfilment, curiosity and a genuine desire to learn."* / *"Extrinsic motivation comes from the outside: driven by pay-rises, material rewards and social approval."* `[VERIFIED — Goodreads]`

**How Abdaal says to FIX misalignment** `[VERIFIED]`: a three-horizon cascade —
- **Long-term**: define what "success"/a meaningful life means to you (Eulogy Method, Odyssey Plan).
- **Medium-term**: 12-Month Celebration → concrete one-year wins in your value areas.
- **Short-term**: Three Alignment Quests → three actions *today* that ladder into the 12-month picture.
- **Iteration layer**: Alignment Experiments — change one variable, observe, keep or discard.
`[VERIFIED — summrize states the three-tier structure explicitly; ericsandroni/roamingreina list the four experiments]`

### PART 1 — ENERGISE

#### Ch.1 Play

**1.1 Choose Your Character**
- Prompt (paraphrase of source): identify which of the 8 play personalities resonates with you — Collector, Competitor, Explorer, Creator, Storyteller, Joker, Director, Kinesthete — then deliberately approach a dull task through that persona. `[VERIFIED names; wording paraphrased — ericsandroni, roamingreina, Abdaal's X post]`

- Output: one reframed way to do that task.

**1.3 Magic Post-It Note**

- Input: any resisted task. Output: one alteration that adds fun. Time: 2 min `[INFERRED]`
- **Strongest single UI candidate in the whole book** — one question, one text field.

**1.4 Enjoy the Process**
- Prompt: shift attention from outcome to process; target flow. `[VERIFIED — paraphrase, ericsandroni/roamingreina]`

**1.5 Reframe Failure**
- Prompt: treat a setback as a **data point**, not a verdict. `[VERIFIED — paraphrase, multiple]`

**1.6 Don't Be Serious, Be Sincere**
- Prompt: care deeply, hold lightly (Alan Watts framing). `[VERIFIED — Art of Manliness quotes Abdaal on this directly]`

### PART 2 — UNBLOCK

#### Ch.4 Seek Clarity

**4.1 Commander's Intent**
- Structure (verbatim components, cross-verified): **Purpose** (the why) · **End State** (what "done"/success looks like) · **Key Tasks** (the general steps). `[VERIFIED — larschristensen and tosummarise both give these three]`
- Abdaal's own use: *"What is the purpose behind this?"* asked before building a to-do list. `[VERIFIED — tosummarise]`
- Input: a project. Output: 3 fields. Time: 10–15 min `[INFERRED]`
- **Strong UI candidate** — 3 labelled text fields, directly usable as a goal-definition step.

**4.2 The Five Whys**
- Prompt: ask "why?" five times to reach the root purpose (or the root avoidance). `[VERIFIED]`
- Input: a goal or a procrastinated task. Output: 5 chained answers + a root statement. Time: 10 min `[INFERRED]`
- **Strong UI candidate** — literally a 5-step guided chain.

**4.3 NICE Goals** ⚠️ *naming discrepancy — read this carefully*
- **In the book**: **N**ear-term · **I**nput-based · **C**ontrollable · **E**nergising. `[VERIFIED — agreed by ericsandroni, roamingreina, tosummarise, summrize, flippingba, larschristensen, Med School Insiders, Art of Manliness, readingraphics — 9 independent sources]`
- **However**, Abdaal's own X/Twitter post (March 2023, pre-publication) says: *"Forget SMART goals. Instead, set NICE goals - Near-Term **Identity-Based** Controllable Energising."* `[VERIFIED — the tweet exists]`
- Conclusion: the acronym **changed between the 2023 tweet and the published book**, from Identity-Based to Input-based. `[INFERRED — plausible and consistent with all evidence, but I found no source in which Abdaal states the change.]` If you build on this, use **Input-based**.
- Component definitions `[VERIFIED — anilg.blog / summrize / Art of Manliness]`:
  - *Near-term*: "this week", not "this year" — shifts you to what you can act on now.
  - *Input-based*: focus on effort you control ("write for 20 minutes"), not the result ("get 10k subs").
  - *Controllable*: "Your goal should be 100% within your power."
  - *Energising*: incorporates play, power, or people.
- Input: an outcome goal. Output: a rewritten NICE goal. Time: 10 min `[INFERRED]`
- **Very strong UI candidate** — 4 validated fields with a checklist; the "Controllable" and "Input-based" checks are mechanically testable.

**4.4 Crystal Ball Method (pre-mortem)**
- Prompt: imagine it's failed — identify the top ~3 obstacles that killed it, in advance. `[VERIFIED — roamingreina says "top 3 obstacles"]`
- Input: a plan. Output: 3 obstacles + mitigations. Time: 10 min `[INFERRED]`
- **Strong UI candidate.**

**4.5 Implementation Intentions**
- Prompt: **"If X, then Y"** / "When I [existing cue], I will [action]". Book examples via notes: *"If I brush my teeth, then I'll stretch"*, *"When I get home, I work out"*. `[VERIFIED — grahammann, roamingreina]`
- Input: an intention + an existing anchor. Output: one if-then sentence. Time: 3 min `[INFERRED]`
- **Strong UI candidate** — two-slot sentence builder.

**4.6 Time Blocking (three levels)**
- **Level 1**: block out *specific tasks* you've been avoiding; treat like an appointment.
- **Level 2**: block out *most of your day* — convert the to-do list into a schedule, leaving breaks.
- **Level 3**: block out your **ideal week** — designed to balance all the life aspects you value.
`[VERIFIED — tosummarise gives all three levels; single-source but detailed]`
- Level 3 is essentially a life-balance instrument, not a scheduling one. Relevant to life-direction work.

#### Ch.5 Find Courage

**5.1 Emotion Label (affective labelling)**
- Prompt: name the specific fear out loud or in writing ("I'm afraid that…"). Naming reduces amygdala activation. `[VERIFIED]`
- Book quote: *"Getting to know our fears is the first step towards overcoming them."* `[VERIFIED — Goodreads]`
- Input: the avoided task. Output: 1–3 named fears. Time: 5 min `[INFERRED]`
- **Strong UI candidate.**

**5.2 Identity Label**
- Prompt: spot limiting self-labels ("I'm not a creative person") and swap for action-inviting ones ("I'm a learner"). `[VERIFIED — readingraphics search snippet gives both example strings]`
- Reader-applied example: "Resilient Stuti". `[VERIFIED — stutichugh]`
- **Strong UI candidate** — old label → new label pairs.

**5.3 10/10/10 Rule**
- Prompt (verbatim-stable across sources): **"Will this matter in 10 minutes? Will it matter in 10 weeks? Will it matter in 10 years?"** `[VERIFIED — grahammann, Art of Manliness, roamingreina; note some sources render the middle term as "10 months" — the majority say **10 weeks**]`
- Input: a feared event. Output: 3 answers. Time: 3 min `[INFERRED]`
- **Strong UI candidate.**

**5.4 Confidence Equation**
- Formula: **Self-confidence = perception of your ability − perception of the standards required.** Raise the first or interrogate the second. `[VERIFIED — ericsandroni + roamingreina agree]`
- **Good UI candidate** — two sliders and a computed gap.

**5.5 Stop Spotlighting**
- Prompt: recall the spotlight effect — people are far less focused on you than you think. `[VERIFIED]`

**5.6 Batman Effect**
- Prompt: adopt an alter ego + a confidence mantra for hard tasks. Examples in book/interviews: children performing better as superheroes; Adele's "Sasha Carter"; Abdaal's own fake glasses / "young Professor X". `[VERIFIED — grahammann, Art of Manliness, summrize]`
- Input: a persona + a mantra. Output: a named alter ego. Time: 10 min `[INFERRED]`

#### Ch.6 Get Started

**6.1 Reduce Environmental Friction** — make the desired thing obvious/near (gym clothes laid out) . `[VERIFIED]`
**6.2 Reduce Emotional Friction — the Five-Minute Rule** — set a timer, commit only to 5 minutes, then choose. Abdaal: *"once you get started with something it's a lot easier to keep going."* `[VERIFIED — Art of Manliness]`
**6.3 Define the Next Action Step** — shrink to a physically tiny act (book's example: *roll out the yoga mat*). `[VERIFIED — grahammann, roamingreina]` **Strong UI candidate.**
**6.4 Track Your Progress** — book quote: *"Tracking your progress provides you with tangible evidence that you're moving towards your goals."* `[VERIFIED — Goodreads]`
**6.5 Find an Accountability Buddy** — agree method + frequency. Summrize also describes an **accountability cascade**: public commitment → accountability partner → rewards/penalties → tracking. `[VERIFIED, single-source for the cascade]`
**6.6 Forgive Yourself** — self-compassion after a lapse; techniques named **"Find the Win"** and **"Correct Course"**. `[VERIFIED — tosummarise, markwk, Med School Insiders]`

### PART 3 — SUSTAIN

#### Ch.7 Conserve (vs over-exertion burnout)

**7.1 Energy Investment Portfolio** ⭐
- Structure: **List A = dreams/bucket list** (everything you'd like to do eventually); **List B = active investments** (what you're actually putting energy into now). Abdaal calls it *"a glorified projects list."* `[VERIFIED]`
- **Cap on List B: 3–5 projects** per week (roamingreina says "keep to single digits"; markwk and setapp say 3–5). `[VERIFIED, minor variance]`
- Extended 4-list version circulating in a book-club writeup: List A dreams · List B active · **List C actual calendar** (what you really did per week) · **List D desired calendar** (ideal week). `[VERIFIED — inspiringpositiveimpact; this may be that author's extension rather than the book's, so flag as MEDIUM confidence for "in the book"]`
- Associated reflection prompts (same source): *"Which priorities aren't reflected in your calendar?"*, *"Does your energy reflect your stated values?"*, *"What boundary won't you cross?"* `[VERIFIED as text on that page; NOT verified as book wording]`
- Input: two lists. Output: an over-commitment diagnosis + items demoted back to List A. Time: 20–30 min `[INFERRED]`
- **Very strong UI candidate** — two-column list with a hard cap that forces trade-offs. This is the closest thing in the book to a "current life load" audit.

**7.2 The Power of No**
- Filters, both named: Derek Sivers' **"Hell yeah or no"**; Juliet Funt's test — *"Would I embrace this if it started tomorrow?"* (i.e. don't grandfather old commitments). `[VERIFIED — tosummarise, inspiringpositiveimpact]`
- Steve Jobs quote used in the book: focus *"means saying no to the hundred other good ideas"* / *"saying no to 1,000 things"*. `[VERIFIED — grahammann, summrize]`
- Also: consider **opportunity cost** of each yes. `[VERIFIED]`
- **Strong UI candidate** — a per-commitment binary filter.

**7.3 Add Friction** — make distractions costly (log out, uninstall). `[VERIFIED]`
**7.4 Correct Course** — a distraction doesn't void the plan; return gently. `[VERIFIED]`
**7.5 Schedule Your Breaks** — put rest in the calendar. Quote: *"Breaks aren't a special treat. They're an absolute necessity."* `[VERIFIED — inspiringpositiveimpact, presented as book line; MEDIUM confidence on exact wording]`
**7.6 Embrace Energising Distractions** — some interruptions (loved ones) restore rather than drain. `[VERIFIED]`

#### Ch.8 Recharge (vs depletion burnout)

**8.1 CALM Hobbies** ⭐
- **CALM** = **C**ompetence (you're getting better) · **A**utonomy (nobody's directing it) · **L**iberty/Liberation (mentally distant from work) · **M**ellow (low stakes).
  `[VERIFIED — Abdaal gives all four on Art of Manliness; corroborated by flippingba, roamingreina, summrize. Note "L" is rendered "Liberty" in some sources and "Liberated/Liberation" in others.]`
- Warning attached: don't monetise the hobby — Abdaal: *"if you start relying on the money from it… it will stop becoming a passion."* `[VERIFIED — Art of Manliness]`
- **Good UI candidate** — score a candidate hobby 1–10 on each of the four letters.

**8.2 CALM Projects** — a bounded creative project (example: one photo a day for a year). `[VERIFIED]`
**8.3 Bring in Nature** — plants, green space, even nature imagery; the hospital-window recovery study is cited. `[VERIFIED]`
**8.4 Take a Walk** — 10 minutes outdoors resets state; no performance metric attached. `[VERIFIED]`
**8.5 Let Your Mind Wander** — do a routine task with no podcast/audio; default-mode-network problem-solving. `[VERIFIED]`
**8.6 The Reitoff Principle** ⭐
- Definition (near-verbatim): *"grant yourself permission to write off a day and intentionally step away from achieving anything."* `[VERIFIED — search-surfaced book phrasing]`
- Abdaal confirms on Linking Your Thinking that **the name is a deliberate joke** (a fake-sounding eponym; "Reitoff" = "write off"), and that for achievement-oriented people naming it makes it usable: *"it's really helpful to be like, yeah, this is a thing, it's a write off day."* `[VERIFIED]`

#### Ch.9 Align (vs misalignment burnout) ⭐ **most relevant section for life-direction work**

**9.1 The Eulogy Method**
- Prompt (paraphrase, consistent across sources): imagine your funeral. What would you *want* people to say about you? Or, as tosummarise phrases the operative question: **"what would I feel good about someone saying in my eulogy?"** `[VERIFIED — tosummarise, Art of Manliness ("writing your obituary"), Modern Wisdom (Abdaal: write your own obituary / imagine your deathbed)]`
- Logic given: money and business rarely appear in eulogies; relationships, character and hobbies do — so prioritise the latter. `[VERIFIED — tosummarise]`
- Input: free-text eulogy. Output: an extracted values list. Time: 20–30 min `[INFERRED]`
- **Strong UI candidate** — free-text write, then a values-extraction step.

**9.2 The Odyssey Plan** ⭐
- Origin: Bill Burnett & Dave Evans, *Designing Your Life*. `[VERIFIED]`
- Prompt: write **in detail** what your life looks like **5 years** from now under three paths `[VERIFIED — tosummarise, ericsandroni, roamingreina]`:
  1. **Your Current Path** — "What will my life look like five years from now if I continue on the same path?"
  2. **Your Alternative Path** — "What will my life look like five years from now if I take a different path?"
  3. **Your Radical Path** — "…if money and social constraints were irrelevant?"
- ⚠️ **Horizon discrepancy**: on the Linking Your Thinking podcast Abdaal describes it as *"three futures over 3–5 years."* Most written summaries say 5 years. `[VERIFIED both; treat 5 years as the book default]`
- Two companion questions Abdaal uses alongside it (podcast, verbatim): **"What would I do if I knew I couldn't fail?"** and — the one he says is more revealing — **"What would I do even if I knew I would fail?"** The second, he says, surfaces your **true North Star**, because it strips outcome-dependence. `[VERIFIED — Linking Your Thinking transcript]`
- Input: 3 long free-text futures. Output: a chosen/most-alive direction. Time: 45–60 min `[INFERRED]`
- **Very strong UI candidate** — three parallel prompts, then a comparison/selection step.

**9.3 The Wheel of Life** ⭐
- **Nine areas in three domains** `[VERIFIED — Abdaal's own live tool at play.aliabdaal.com/wheeloflife, described in detail at tacticsplus]`:
  - **Health**: Body · Mind · Soul
  - **Work**: Mission · Money · Growth
  - **Relationships**: Family · Romance · Friends
- Area descriptions as used by Abdaal's own tool (verbatim from tacticsplus's reproduction) `[VERIFIED, second-hand]`:
  - Body — "Physical well-being, fitness, and overall health"
  - Mind — "Mental health, cognitive well-being, and emotional balance"
  - Soul — "Spiritual well-being and/or connection to meaning in life"
  - Mission — "Fulfillment in your career or job, including alignment of your work with your life purpose, values, and personal mission"
  - Money — "Financial well-being, income, savings, and overall financial health"
  - Growth — "Personal and professional development and learning"
  - Family — "Quality of relationships with family members and the overall family dynamic"
  - Romance — "Connection and fulfillment in the realm of romance"
  - Friends — "Satisfaction and connection with friends, companionship, and social circles"
- **Scale: 1–10 satisfaction per area.** `[VERIFIED]`
- Abdaal's own quiz page states it is *"An exercise used in coaching to help you quickly understand how balanced or fulfilled your life is in this moment"* and takes **"less than 5 minutes to complete."** `[VERIFIED — play.aliabdaal.com/wheeloflife landing copy; this is the only officially-published time estimate I found for any exercise]`
- Input: 9 ratings. Output: a radar/wheel + identification of the weakest areas. Time: **<5 min (official)**.
- **Strongest structured UI candidate in the book** — fixed taxonomy, numeric input, obvious visualisation, obvious downstream routing into goals.

**9.4 The 12-Month Celebration** ⭐
- Prompt (near-verbatim, from a search-surfaced book passage): *"Imagine yourself a year from now when things are going awesomely — you finally landed that dream job, or crushed a personal fitness goal, perhaps mastered a new skill or strengthened a cherished relationship. Imagine that with these wins, you are looking back to the decisions, actions, and habits you made that led you there."* `[VERIFIED — search-surfaced passage; single-source, near-verbatim]`
- Sentence format for the output: **"In the past year, I have [achievement] because I [action/habit]."** `[VERIFIED — Victor Zexi He / Pragmatic Wisdom; MEDIUM confidence that this exact template is the book's rather than the author's formalisation]`
- Worked examples given: strengthened friendships by spending more time with friends/family; paid off student debt by starting a writing side-business; completed a half marathon through consistent training.
- Input: forward-projection. Output: 3-ish celebration statements each pairing an outcome with its cause. Time: 20–30 min `[INFERRED]`
- **Very strong UI candidate** — a fill-in-the-blank sentence generator whose output is *automatically* decomposable into habits.

**9.5 Three Alignment Quests** ⭐
- Prompt (near-verbatim): *"Each morning, choose three actions for the day ahead that will move you a step closer to where you want to be for your 12-month celebration."* `[VERIFIED — search-surfaced book phrasing]`
- Convention: one per domain (Health / Work / Relationships). Book's worked examples `[VERIFIED — search-surfaced]`:
  - Health — "Take a 15-minute walk during my lunch break."
  - Work — "Complete the project proposal draft by lunchtime."
  - Relationships — "Cook a healthy dinner for the family and spend quality time together."
- Rationale (near-verbatim): *"it diminishes the terror-inducing scale of the massive 12-month objective… you turn living your values into something immediate."*
- Input: the 12-month celebration. Output: 3 dated actions. Time: 3–5 min daily `[INFERRED]`
- **Very strong UI candidate** — a daily 3-slot ritual bound to the three life domains, directly downstream of the Wheel of Life and 12-Month Celebration.

**9.6 Alignment Experiments** ⭐
- Three steps `[VERIFIED — tosummarise / search-surfaced]`:
  1. **Choose an area** you want to improve (i.e. where the Wheel scored low).
  2. **Form a hypothesis** — name one thing you'd change and the effect you expect on your life (examples given: reduce working hours; switch degrees).
  3. **Execute and study the results** — run it small, keep a journal/log, note insights and challenges.
- Framing quote: *"Experiments give yourself the opportunity to explore an alternative path, without having to commit to it in the long term."* `[VERIFIED — search-surfaced book phrasing]`
- Input: a misaligned area + a hypothesis. Output: a time-boxed trial + a verdict. Time: 10 min to set up; days–weeks to run `[INFERRED]`
- **Very strong UI candidate, and structurally the most interesting one for a life-direction product** — it turns "what should I do with my life" into a testable, low-commitment loop with an explicit verdict step. This is the same shape as a habit/goal experiment with an outcome review.

## 6. Confidence & gaps

### High confidence (multiple independent sources, or Abdaal's own words)
- The 3×3 skeleton: Energise (Play/Power/People) · Unblock (Seek Clarity/Find Courage/Get Started, vs uncertainty/fear/inertia) · Sustain (Conserve/Recharge/Align, vs over-exertion/depletion/misalignment burnout).
- Chapter numbering 1–9 and part membership.
- The **names** of all 54 experiments, and their chapter assignment (two independent sources match).
- **54** as the total experiment count.
- NICE = Near-term, **Input-based**, Controllable, Energising (in the published book).
- CALM = Competence, Autonomy, Liberty/Liberation, Mellow.
- Wheel of Life = 9 areas / 3 domains, 1–10 scale, with Abdaal's own published area descriptions.
- The 8 play personalities and their Stuart Brown origin.
- Commander's Intent = Purpose / End State / Key Tasks.
- Odyssey Plan = current / alternative / radical, ~5 years, from *Designing Your Life*.
- Misalignment burnout as the third type, and the eulogy/Odyssey/Wheel/12-month/quests/experiments cascade as its remedy.

### Medium confidence (single good source, or slight cross-source variance)
- **Which two experiments belong to which of the three strategies** inside each chapter — I have the flat 6-per-chapter list but not the strategy grouping. **Unverified.**
- Time-blocking "three levels" (task / most-of-day / ideal week) — one source (tosummarise), detailed but unreplicated.
- The Energy Investment Portfolio's **four-list** version (A dreams, B active, C actual calendar, D desired calendar) — the two-list A/B version is well corroborated; C and D may be the blog author's extension.
- The active-projects cap: sources say "3–5" and also "single digits". Both may be in the book at different points.
- 10/10/10 middle term: majority say **10 weeks**; a minority say 10 months.
- "In the past year, I have [X] because I [Y]" as the book's literal 12-Month Celebration template (vs. a summariser's formalisation).
- The exact chapter title of the closing section ("Last Word: Think Like a Productivity Scientist") — from a single search-result TOC listing.
- Accountability cascade (public commitment → partner → rewards/penalties → tracking) — single source.
