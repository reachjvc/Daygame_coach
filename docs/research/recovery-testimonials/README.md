# Recovery testimonials and techniques — corpus

**Purpose.** Two things, from people who actually changed an addictive behaviour:
a library of real, cited first-person accounts, and the specific techniques,
prompts and tools those people credit. The first is a product surface in its own
right — reading other people's accounts is itself one of the more consistently
credited things in this whole literature. The second is what the flows get built
out of.

**Status:** gathering, 2026-08-17. Fifteen sources, one agent each, written to
the numbered files in this directory.

## The integrity rule

Every quotation in every file must be verbatim text from a URL that was actually
fetched, sitting next to that URL. Nothing reconstructed, nothing quoted from
memory, nothing paraphrased into quotation marks.

This is not fussiness. A fabricated testimonial in a product aimed at somebody
deciding whether their drinking is a problem is a serious harm: it manufactures
a false peer consensus, and if it is ever spotted it discredits every real thing
on the page alongside it. **A source file that honestly reports "I could only
verify nine of these" is worth more than one with forty unsourced quotes.**

Where an agent could not fetch something, it belongs under an explicit
`UNVERIFIED` heading, without quotation marks, and it may not be promoted into
the product.

Anything that reaches the product gets spot-checked against the live URL first.

## Privacy

These are health disclosures, and several categories here carry legal or
employment exposure on top of stigma.

- Pseudonymous handles only for forum and community material — `u/handle` as
  published, never resolved to a person.
- Never the real name of a private individual. Published authors, named
  creators and consenting campaign participants are different and may be cited
  as published.
- Nothing self-identifying: no employer, town, or unusual biographical detail,
  even where the original post included it.
- Any quote that identifies its author despite a pseudonym gets dropped or
  redacted, not published.

## Licensing

Mixed, and it matters per source:

| Source type | Position |
|---|---|
| US Government works (NIAAA, SAMHSA, CDC) | Public domain, usable close to verbatim |
| Forum posts | User-owned; short quotes with attribution and link, fair dealing for criticism and review |
| In-copyright books | Short attributed quotes only. Never long passages |
| SMART Recovery, Therapist Aid | Free to use, republishing forbidden — **rebuild tools in our own words** |
| Rational Recovery, AVRT | Trademarked names. Technique may be used, names may not |
| YouTube / podcasts | Copyrighted performances. Short attributed quotes with timestamp |
| Commercial testimonials | Marketing. Tag `commercial`, weight down, never present as peer testimony |
| **Recovery Dharma** | **CC BY-NC — NonCommercial. CANNOT ship in a paid product.** Research use only |
| AA, 1939 first edition | US public domain. Later editions are not, and the wording differs — state the edition |
| AA Grapevine | Paywalled and in copyright. Free opening paragraphs only |
| Women for Sobriety, 13 statements | Core copyrighted text. Do not reproduce |

**The Recovery Dharma one is a trap worth naming:** it is the most openly
licensed source in the set, which makes it the most tempting to lean on, and the
NonCommercial clause makes it the one thing here that definitely cannot ship.
Research use only, and it must not leak into a product file.

## Techniques are not copyrightable; expression is

This is what makes the corpus usable at all. A method — externalise the craving
and answer it, debrief the evening against fixed questions, hand your card to
somebody — is an idea, and ideas are free. The *words* a book or programme uses
to teach it are not. So the rule throughout: take the mechanism, rebuild the
wording, cite the origin honestly. That is already what this module does for
SMART Recovery and AVRT.

## Source-type weighting

Not all of these are the same kind of evidence and the corpus should never
flatten them into one voice:

1. **Peer-reviewed qualitative studies** — systematically sampled, participant
   quotes published. The only source here without survivorship bias.
2. **Peer community posts** — huge, contemporaneous, honest, and heavily
   survivorship-biased: people whose attempt failed largely stop posting.
3. **Contemporaneous blogs** — written during the change rather than
   reconstructed after it. Best source for the middle of it.
4. **Memoirs and long-form** — best-articulated mechanisms, most retrospective
   smoothing, and selected for having a publishable arc.
5. **Mutual-aid published stories** — testimony *for* a programme. Advocacy-adjacent.
6. **Commercial** — treat as marketing.

## The bias every file has to state

Almost everything here is written by people for whom it worked. That is the
central limitation of the whole corpus and no amount of volume fixes it. Each
source file states its own version of this at the top, and any product surface
built on it says so on the page rather than in a footnote.
