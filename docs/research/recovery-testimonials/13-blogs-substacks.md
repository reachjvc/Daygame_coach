## (B) Techniques and small tactical inventions

Ranked with the small, odd, self-invented ones first — those are the ones that do not appear in books.

### Tier 1 — small, odd, self-invented, specific

**T1. "I peeled them instead of cutting them"** — manufacture a trivial novel choice to occupy the drinking hour.
What they DO: at the 5pm–11pm wine window, assemble a sequence of consumables and introduce an arbitrary variation in how one is prepared, purely to make it a decision rather than a substitute.
> "I did 30 minutes on the eliptical and gave myself some 'FUN!' choices with the oranges – I peeled them instead of cutting them."
UnPickled, 2011-03-22. Recurrence: 1. No backfire reported.
*The point is not the orange. It is that the replacement had to contain a choice.*

**T2. The evening consumable stack** — a named, ordered list of things to put in your mouth between 5pm and bed.

UnPickled, 2011-03-22. Recurrence: **high** — Mrs D's "stock up on non-alcoholic drinks… fancy garnishes"; Sober School's "herbal tea, chocolate and a good movie"; "a Ginger beer at 5.30pm". Consistently phrased as a *stack*, not one swap.

**T3. Yell out loud at the named voice.**
What they DO: personify the craving ("Wolfie"), then swear at it audibly.

Belle, 2013-04-21. Recurrence: 1 author, but the whole "Wolfie" naming convention propagated across her 28,000-subscriber community. Note the script is short, rhythmic and repeatable — designed to be said, not thought.

**T4. Secret jewellery with the phrase in very small print.**

Belle, 2013-04-21. A wearable private token, deliberately illegible to others.

**T5. Always take your own car.**

Sinead, Living Sober, 2025-03-23. ✅ **spot-check verified verbatim in surrounding paragraph.** Context confirms it is specifically a first-social-occasions tactic: it sits between "The first few occasions when I was around alcohol was very difficult" and "I found it (and still find it) difficult to be around people that are drunk." Removes the exit-permission problem. Structurally identical to T6/T7 (pre-committed physical escape/avoidance).

**T6. Don't do the grocery shop at all for a month; never enter the liquor aisle; never let white wine in the house.**
> "I did not go grocery shopping for at least a month. I never walked down the liquor aisle." … "I did not and still do not have white wine in the house."
Aria, Living Sober, 2020-07-15. ✅ **spot-check verified verbatim in surrounding paragraph.** Three nested rules: a shop, an aisle within it, and one specific product permanently banned from one specific building. Note the third rule is named as still active years later.

**T7. Drive the other way home.**
> "Yesterday it got to 4pm and I kept thinking I'd love a glass of wine later...I decided to drive that way making my choices easier."
Tracey Harris, Sober School comment, 2024-07-29. Route selection as craving management, decided at 4pm for a 6pm problem.

**T8. Bring your own bottle to the restaurant and ask permission.**
> "What I do is bring my own bottle of AF wine to the table – I have never found this a problem and I always ask politely if it's OK."
Sarah, Sober School comment, 2016-07-25. Solves the empty-hands problem at a table where wine is poured.

**T9. "I think I am allergic to alcohol."**

CG, Sober School comment, 2016-07-25. A disclosure line chosen because it terminates the conversation rather than opening one.

**T10. Be the one pouring.**
> "I found myself playing hostess and pouring drinks for others" while having "nothing myself."
UnPickled, 2011-03-31. Take the host role so your empty hands have a reason.

**T11. Never return to a destination that was great when you drank.**
> "never go back to a previous destination- particularly if there was one, once, that was great, when your drinking was truly social."
Felicity, Sober School comment, 2016-07-25. **First holiday.** Note this is the opposite of the usual "recreate it sober" advice.

**T12. Leave the phone in the car.**
> "I leave it in the car. I forget it's even there."
C. A. McLaren, 2025-04-10. Physical distance, not software.

**T13. Collapse the evening.**
> "After work I spent time in the garden and went to bed very early as well as having an early dinner."
Aria, Living Sober, 2020-07-15. Shorten the dangerous window by moving dinner and bedtime earlier rather than filling it.

**T14. Call the crew from the car, immediately, before you can reconsider.**

Jamie, After Gambling. Burn the social bridge inside the same hour as the decision.

**T15. Come clean by phone within minutes of the slip.**

Jamie, After Gambling. Shortens the concealment window to near zero.

## The specific stages you asked about

### Day 4 — well attested, and the accounts conflict productively
- **Named as the personal barrier before it arrives:** > "I've haven't gone four days without a drink in over ten years." (UnPickled, day 1)
- **As physical turning point:** > "day four marked a turning point. That's when the worst of the physical symptoms started to subside" (Benya Clark)
- **As the danger zone precisely because it feels better:** > "I'm feeling good: no more hangover or shakes... Maybe one little glass tonight as a reward. Why do I feel like I'm in the danger zone?" (Club Soda reader, 2021-06-09)
- **As the day the support arrived:** UnPickled's day-4 post is the one where 13 Twitter followers appear and > "I stood in the kitchen and wept with unexpected relief."
- ⚠️ **UNVERIFIED:** Belle has an indexed post titled *"Day 4: what can i give up"* at `.../2016/12/14/day-4-what-can-i-give-up/` which **404s on fetch**. No quote taken.

**Synthesis:** day 4 is not a difficulty peak in these accounts. It is where physical symptoms lift and the reward-bargain appears. That is a different product requirement from "day 4 is hard."

### The three-week wall — **NEGATIVE FINDING**
**No blogger in this sample names a three-week wall.** The concept appears almost exclusively in rehab-marketing SEO pages ("3 Weeks Without Alcohol – What to Expect", Genesis House; Recovery Atlanta; Primrose Lodge), not in first-person diaries.

What contemporaneous writers actually report at that point is the **opposite**:
> "By the end of the second week, I could go an hour or so without even thinking about alcohol, which felt truly remarkable." … "After about two weeks sober, I woke up one day feeling better than I had in years" (Benya Clark)

The nearest real wall found is at **week 4, and in vaping, not alcohol**: > "i caved after 4 weeks and hit someone else's" (Sophie diGrazia). Diarists otherwise describe a long undifferentiated grind rather than a dated wall: > "I just white knuckled it through the first few weeks/months" (Tom).

**Recommendation: do not build a "three-week wall" feature on this evidence.** It is a marketing construct, not a diary observation.

### First social occasion
Rarely planned; usually an ambush.
- > "my inlaws noticed at dinner last night that I turned down wine and innocently asked, 'What, are you on the wagon now?'" (UnPickled, day ~7)
- > "My first evening at a cafe was hard. I watched people around me having a glass of wine and felt sad." (Aria)
- Tactics used: own car (T5), be the host (T10), own bottle (T8), the allergy line (T9), or total avoidance with an acknowledged loneliness cost (Rae, Tom).

### First holiday
- > "I made sure I had my own alcohol free drinks and good food" — Aria, first Christmas ⚠️ *quote boundary uncertain, "during Christmas" was extractor narration.*
- > "I ended up being seated beside a guest who had been in recovery for 7 years and he was great support." — Aria (luck, not tactic)
- > "never go back to a previous destination" — Felicity (T11)
- McKowen's holiday post is about relatives, not drink: > "you can't control—will never be able to control—what other people say about you."

### Telling people
The richest single artifact is UnPickled's *Keeping It Quiet* (2011-03-27), which records a **lie told in real time** and the true decision underneath it — something a memoir would smooth away:
> "'Until when?' he asked. 'I don't know,' I lied." … "I do know. I already know that I must never ever drink again."

Range of positions: tell one person (UnPickled) → keep quiet six months (Tom) → use a conversation-ending line (CG) → go fully public as the mechanism itself (Pinsky). **No consensus. Build for the range, not a default.**
