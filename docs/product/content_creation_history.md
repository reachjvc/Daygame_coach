## What others do (researched 2026-11-18)

Animation studios:
- Order: script, storyboard, then 2 to 4 style frames (finished stills of
  key scenes that lock the look), then an animatic (the frames timed to the
  voice), then animation.
- The client signs off each stage before the next starts.
- Style options are shown as the same scene side by side, so only the style
  differs. The client picks one of 2 or 3 directions.
- Sources: [Milanote](https://www.milanote.com/guide/motion-design-style-frames),
  [Blue Carrot](https://bluecarrot.io/blog/essential-animation-process-steps-and-timeline/),
  [Advids](https://advids.co/insights/understanding-the-process-storyboards-vsstyle-frames-vsanimatics-and-what-to-expect).

Creators working with AI (2026 posts and 15 YouTube videos):
- The script is approved before anything is designed.
- The voice is recorded first, and scenes are timed to it.
- They want a storyboard or contact sheet before the build.
- The human judges the stills; the AI makes the changes.
- Every note becomes a rule in a file, so a mistake happens only once.
- Only 1 of 15 claims one prompt with no review.
- Sources: [Hack](https://coachlindsayhack.substack.com/p/i-made-my-first-explainer-video-in),
  [Crist](https://michaelcrist.substack.com/p/make-ai-videos-with-claude),
  [Tyson](https://theagentarchitect.substack.com/p/claude-code-remotion-video-rendering),
  [anima](https://github.com/seanwinslow28/anima),
  [Peverelli](https://youtu.be/HzXD4GVqXwM),
  [Abdaal](https://youtu.be/C0jOKDJtK9s),
  [Flick](https://youtu.be/zakFNEOPq0M).
- Fuller notes (private): `~/.cache/daygame-research/content/process-research/youtube.md`.

## What others do about pace, pictures and breaths (researched 2026-11-20)

Mostly blogs by companies that sell editing tools; none shows a controlled
test. Full notes (private):
`~/.cache/daygame-research/content/process-research/2026-11-20-*.md`.

- Line tests: cut a line unless it raises curiosity, makes the stakes clear,
  gives value or sets up the next beat; merge lines that do the same job.
- Pauses: editors close natural gaps to 0.1 to 0.4 seconds and keep a few
  chosen beats.
- Pictures: one row per spoken line with its picture; change the picture
  when the line's job changes; cutaways of 2 to 5 seconds in Reels; the face
  for personal lines and for the ask (one seller's test).
- After posting: lay the retention graph over the lines; a drop names the
  line before it.
- Not found: proof that reader panels predict viewers; an example of one
  animated world cutting to footage of its maker and back.

## The first night run (2026-11-20 to 21) and his verdict

What he asked for (2026-11-20, 20:06): keep his GPU rendering reels at full
capacity until 06:00, with Claude choosing the work from the content chats.
The work should be mostly animation, not token use.

What Claude made: 18 whole "Outvoted" films. Every scene was an AI still

was 6 painted looks × 3 versions of the same 32 shots.

### His verdict (owner, 2026-11-21) — his rule: never make this kind again

- Not usable at all. It is the same video in a few looks, with almost no
  creativity. The variance is far too low.
- AI still paintings with a slow zoom are the opposite of what he enjoys
  about AI. The quality is low: wrong fingers, pictures often all wrong, no
  real animation, only zooming in and out.
- **Do not make these pictures, paintings or videos again.** This drops way
  8 (AI paintings with a slow camera) and step 8's "same voice in many
  looks".
- What he had said he liked: the blocks film, with **more** kinds of
  animation at some spots (his notes of 2026-11-20, under "The owner's
  words"). The night did not apply any of those notes to the Outvoted film.
- He does not trust that the GPU was near full. He wants a way to be sure.

### Why it went wrong (Claude's own reading)

1. **The wrong brief won.** The doc held his Outvoted film notes (keep the
   blocks, add other animation at "at night" and "in the morning", film
   himself) and, lower down, the 👍 on way 8 and step 8. The night session
   built from the newer list and never named his film notes. No line said
   "this is the film to improve next".
2. **"Little token use" was read as "no Claude at all".** Adding new
   animated scenes to the blocks film takes Claude work to build each
   scene. The night picked the one thing a GPU can do alone: still
   pictures. The right split is that Claude builds the scenes in the
   evening and the GPU renders them at night.
3. **Volume was planned, not variety.** The queue repeated one shot list.
4. **Claude judged its own output.** It called the first painting "came out
   well" and never compared it with the film he liked. It did not check the
   hands either.
5. **"Full use" was claimed, not measured.** Only one GPU reading a minute
   was kept, and each new reading replaced the old one. Another session's
   capture batch shared the card all night. For the first 45 minutes and
   from 01:19 to 01:39 the reel work was nearly stopped.

### Proposed change (Claude, proposed 2026-11-21, not decided)

From Claude's own reading, not researched:

- **A night brief before every night.** One short message names the film,
  the notes of his it carries out, and what "done" looks like. Claude sends
  it before the night starts, so he can stop a wrong night in one line.
- **Variety by design.** A night tries different kinds of animation for the
  same spots, not the same shots in other colours.
- **Evening build, night render.** Claude builds the new scenes (code and
  3D, like the blocks) by evening. Then the GPU renders full-size versions
  overnight.
- **A check against what he liked.** Before a night's output reaches him,
  a reviewer compares frames with the film he liked and checks hands and
  faces. A failed kind is cut early, not rendered 18 times.
- **Proof of GPU use.** A logger records GPU use every 10 seconds all
  night. The morning page shows a chart: busy % per hour, and every stretch
  under 80%. On WSL the GPU tool cannot name the programs using the card.
  So the reels' share comes from the render log: seconds per picture or
  frame, against its speed with the card to itself. A drop under 80% for 5 minutes wakes Claude. The queue always holds
  more than one night of work. Tool:
  `~/.cache/daygame-research/tools/gpu_log.sh` (start and summary).

## Celebrity clip reels (owner, 2026-11-20)

This is a separate kind of reel. We take short celebrity interview moments
about quitting (TV shows, podcasts) that went viral on clip channels. We
re-cut them from the show's own full upload and add value of our own.
Round 1 made 10 near-reposts. Round 2 tests ways to make them ours. The
plan is `Plans/celeb-clip-reels.md`; it points to a private plan, because
it names people and video ids.

His rules for these (2026-11-20):
- Full screen 9:16. No black bars or blurred bands.
- Every review page plays the viral original next to ours, so he can
  compare.
- Add real value, not tricks. Instagram or YouTube spotting a repost
  account worries him more than copyright claims from the owners.
- His own voice is preferred. An AI voice is fine as a placeholder.
- Don't only make them longer. Cut differently, or cut something and add
  something new. Some versions must get shorter.

Ways to make them ours (Claude's research, 2026-11-20). He rated #5,
taken broader, especially strong, then #1 and #2:
1. **Tell his story, not one clip.** Join two or three moments of the same
   person from different shows or years with short on-screen lines of ours.
2. **Our point on quitting.** End with one line of ours on what the moment
   teaches.
3. **A narrator with opinions.** A voice that sets up the clip and reacts.
   It gives an opinion or context and never describes the screen.
4. **Fact cards with new information.** Years, numbers, events the clip
   doesn't say, each with a source.
5. **Themed episodes.** 2–4 people per reel, linked by our lines or
   narration. Examples: "Men who quit for their kids", "Rockstars who quit
   alcohol", "Celebrities who quit hard drugs".
6. **Keep them a minority on the account.** Post them between our own
   original reels.

Left out on purpose: mirroring, speed or pitch changes, borders, emoji
stickers, caption removal. Both platforms call these low-effort.

What the platforms say (checked 2026-11-20):
- Instagram (30 April 2026): accounts that mainly post other people's
  content stop being recommended to non-followers.
  - A border, watermark, subtitles or credit line do not make it original.
  - On-screen text with more context, graphics with new information,
    voiceover and a point of view do count.
  - It judges the share of recent posts that are original over a rolling
    30 days.
  - Source: https://creators.instagram.com/blog/rewarding-original-creators-on-instagram
- YouTube (1 October 2026): Shorts re-uploaded from others without
  anything added reach fewer people.
  - Commentary, unique edits and storytelling count.
  - A voiceover that only describes the screen does not count.
  - Separately, YouTube's ad rules exclude clips "edited together with
    little or no narrative".
  - Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/blog/470890423/prioritizing-original-content-on-shorts?hl=en
- Copyright is a separate question. The show owners' matching systems
  ignore credit lines and edits, and posting each reel stays his call.

Tools (private): `~/.cache/daygame-research/celeb-reels/code/`.
- `render.py` makes one-source reels: captions, a face-tracked 9:16 crop,
  the hook and the source tag.
- Round 2 adds `render2.py` for multi-source cuts, cards and the
  placeholder voice.
- `render2.py` takes an edit list. Each clip can set:
  - a picture from another source (B-roll),
  - muted sound,
  - a trim for VHS black edges,
  - a tighter reframe for wide shots,
  - the speaker's name in the source tag.
- Learned in round 2 (2026-11-20):
  - Check every contact sheet: wide shots and cutaways often put the
    wrong person, a poster or a logo in the 9:16 crop.
  - Cover an interviewer cutaway in the middle of his line with a
    picture of him.
  - Measure caption width against the right-side buttons.
  - The review gate found facts a card overstated. It also found two
    versions that were only recuts, with nothing added.
- Round 3 (2026-11-21) uses `render3.py` (render2 plus the fixes below):
  - Cut gate: `code/cutsafe.py` measures the sound itself (10 ms
    loudness) at every cut of a speech clip. A cut must sit in a gap at
    least 10 dB below the speech level, or nothing renders. Transcript
    word times are not enough: they end before the sound does.
  - The interview answer stays one uncut clip. Where the show cuts to the
    interviewer listening, `pics` lays a picture of him over it and the
    sound is untouched.
  - Captions come from the checked source transcript, not from re-hearing
    the final mix (that dropped mumbled words such as "I try").
  - After rendering, every source word within 1.2 s of a cut must be heard
    near its place, or the word is reported. Flags are checked by hand.
  - No dark cards. The source tag is 22 px in the bottom-left corner.
  - Narrator lines over footage of him; music under the narrator comes
    from concert footage (`bed`), never a cut inside speech.
